# Digital Presence and Marketing for Small Business

## A. Session Identification
<!-- kind:identification -->
**Week / module:** Week 3 — Module 3: Run and Grow

**Recommended duration:** 105 minutes

**Difficulty:** Beginner (first contact with digital marketing)

**Prerequisite knowledge:** The Session 7 invoice and tracker. Learners must have a smartphone with WhatsApp. No prior marketing knowledge required.

**Connection to previous work:** In Session 7 you learned to get paid and manage money. Today we get you found: how to build a free digital presence, set up WhatsApp Business, create a Google Business Profile, choose one social platform, build trust, and plan seven days of free marketing. Being found is half the battle — and it can cost nothing.

**Preparation before class:** Print the WhatsApp Business setup checklist (Section F.2); print the Google Business Profile steps (Section F.3); print the seven-day marketing plan template (Section F.6); have the FastFix case pack ready; prepare a sample WhatsApp Business profile on the projector; bring a sample seven-day marketing plan. Instructor note on tone: Many learners think marketing means paying for adverts or being an "influencer". Reframe it: marketing is simply being findable and trustworthy. A small business can be found and trusted with a phone, free tools, and consistency. Keep the mood practical and encouraging — everyone can do this.

## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Explain why being found online matters for a small business — and why it can be free.
2. Set up a complete WhatsApp Business profile: profile photo or logo, clear description, business hours, catalogue of at least
three items, greeting message, customer labels and broadcast lists.
3. Describe the steps to create a Google Business Profile — and why "near me" searches matter.
4. Choose one social platform to maintain well — and explain why one platform done well beats five done poorly.
5. Plan seven days of free marketing: real photos, prices, before/after, customer feedback, availability.
6. Apply trust-building principles: consistent name and logo, fast replies, honest descriptions, no fake testimonials.
7. Produce a complete WhatsApp Business profile and a seven-day free marketing plan (LO10).

## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (6 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
Instructor setup: On the projector, show two "findability stories" without labelling them.
Story A: "Chidi from FastFix has a personal Facebook page he rarely updates. He has no WhatsApp Business profile. When
someone searches 'phone repair near me' on Google, Chidi does not appear. When someone asks in a WhatsApp group
'who can fix my phone?', Chidi does not see the message. He is invisible — even though he is good at his work."
Story B: "Adaeze from Adaeze Fashion has a complete WhatsApp Business profile — a clear photo, a description, a
catalogue of her dresses, and a greeting message. She has a Google Business Profile — when someone searches 'fashion
designer Port Harcourt', she appears. She posts on WhatsApp status every week — a clear photo, a clear price, a clear call
to action. She is findable — and she is busy."
What the instructor says:
"Two businesses. Hands up — which one gets found by customers? … Story B, almost always. Why? Because Adaeze is
findable — she has a WhatsApp Business profile, a Google Business Profile, and a regular presence. Chidi is invisible —
even though he is just as skilled. Today we learn to be Adaeze. You will learn to set up WhatsApp Business, create a Google
Business Profile, choose one social platform, build trust, and plan seven days of free marketing. Being found is half the
battle — and it can cost nothing."
Expected learner response: Learners will say "B is findable", "A is invisible", "B has a profile", "B posts regularly". Accept all
answers and draw out the difference: visible vs invisible.
Then reveal the hook: Ask, "How many of you have searched for a business on Google or WhatsApp — and could not find it?"
Many hands go up. Say:
"That is the experience your customers are having with you. Today you change that."
Why this opener works: It uses the running example and a contrasting example, it isolates visibility as the variable, and it
frames digital presence as a practical necessity — not a luxury.

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (7 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Run this as a fast oral round-robin. Put the questions on the projector.
Question 1: "What are the ten parts of a complete invoice?"
Expected answer: Business name, date, invoice number, customer, description, quantity/unit price, total, payment details,
terms, thank-you.
Common gap: learners remember five or six. Clarify: "The invoice gets you paid — today we get you found."
Question 2: "What are the four steps of the payment-chasing sequence?"
Expected answer: Friendly reminder, follow-up call, firm written message, documented escalation.
Common gap: learners remember two or three. Clarify: "The chasing sequence is how you get paid — today we learn how
to get found."
Question 3: "What are the three taxes to be aware of?"
Expected answer: Income tax (on profit), withholding tax (prepayment), VAT (for some businesses).
Common gap: learners remember one or two. Clarify: "Taxes are part of money management — today we learn visibility."
Bridge to today (say this):
"You can get paid and manage money. Now you get found. A business that cannot be found does not exist — no matter
how good the work. Today you learn to be findable: WhatsApp Business, Google Business Profile, one social platform, trust-
building, and a seven-day free marketing plan. All free. All with your phone."
If more than a third of the class cannot name the ten parts of an invoice, spend 5 extra minutes reviewing them on the board
before continuing.

## F. Full Lecture Notes
<!-- kind:lecture -->
F.1 Why being found online matters — and why it is free
The idea. Customers now search before they buy. They search Google for "phone repair near me", they ask in WhatsApp
groups, they check Instagram for photos. If you are not findable, you do not exist — no matter how good your work is.
The three places customers look:
1. Google: When they search for a service — "phone repair Port Harcourt", "fashion designer near me".
2. WhatsApp: When they ask for recommendations in groups, or when they check your profile before calling.
3. Social media: When they look at your photos, your prices, your customer feedback.
Why it is free:
WhatsApp Business is a free app — no monthly fee.
Google Business Profile is free — no cost to create or maintain.
Social media (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok) is free to join and post.
The only cost is your time — a few hours to set up, and a few minutes a week to maintain.
Real-life comparison. Think of a market stall. A stall with a clear signboard, a clean display, and a friendly trader — that stall
gets customers. A stall with no sign, a messy display, and a silent trader — that stall is invisible. Digital presence is the
signboard, the display, and the friendly trader — online.
Important limitation / misconception. "I need to pay for adverts to be found." No — adverts can help, but they are not the
foundation. A business with a complete WhatsApp Business profile, a Google Business Profile, and regular social media posts
— that business is findable without spending a naira. Adverts come later, when you have the money and the offer is proven.
How a learner recognises a findable business: They can point to a complete WhatsApp Business profile, a Google Business
Profile, and a regular social media presence.
F.2 WhatsApp Business — your digital shopfront
The idea. WhatsApp Business is a free app that turns your WhatsApp into a business tool. It has a profile, a catalogue, labels,
and a greeting message — so customers can find you, learn about you, and contact you easily.
The seven parts of a complete WhatsApp Business profile — teach them one by one:
1. Profile photo or logo: A clear photo of you, your logo, or your work — so customers know who they are dealing with.
FastFix example: A photo of Chidi repairing a phone, or a simple logo with "FastFix Phone Repair".
2. Business name: Your business name — consistent with your other profiles.
FastFix example: "FastFix Phone Repair".
3. Description: A clear, short description of what you do, where you are, and what makes you different.
FastFix example: "Phone repair and accessories at Mile 3 Market, Diobu, Port Harcourt. Same-day screen and battery
repair with 30-day warranty. Call or WhatsApp 0803-XXX-XXXX."
4. Business hours: When you are open — so customers know when to contact you.
FastFix example: "Mon–Sat: 8 a.m.–6 p.m. Sun: Closed."
5. Catalogue: At least three items with photos, prices and descriptions — so customers can see what you offer.
FastFix example: (1) Screen repair — ₦4,000–₦7,000. (2) Battery replacement — ₦2,500–₦4,000. (3) Phone case
— ₦1,500–₦3,000.
6. Greeting message: An automatic message that greets customers who contact you.
FastFix example: "Hello! Thank you for contacting FastFix Phone Repair. How can I help you today? Please describe your
phone problem."
7. Customer labels and broadcast lists: Labels to organise customers (new, quoted, paid, completed) and broadcast lists to
send messages to many customers at once — with permission.
FastFix example: Labels: "New enquiry", "Quoted", "Paid", "Completed". Broadcast list: "Happy customers" — for
occasional updates and offers.
Real-life comparison. Think of a shop. A shop with a clear signboard, a clean window display, a price list, and a friendly
greeting — that shop gets customers. A shop with no sign, a messy window, no prices, and a silent trader — that shop is
invisible. WhatsApp Business is the digital version of the good shop.
Misconception to correct. "WhatsApp Business is only for big companies." No — it is for any business that wants to be
findable and professional. A phone repairer, a tailor, a food seller — all benefit from a complete profile.
F.3 Google Business Profile — when customers search "near me"
The idea. A Google Business Profile is a free listing that appears when someone searches for your business or service on
Google. It shows your name, address, hours, photos, and reviews — so customers can find you and trust you.
Why "near me" searches matter:
When someone searches "phone repair near me" on Google, Google shows businesses with a Google Business Profile.
If you do not have a profile, you do not appear — and the customer goes to your competitor.
A complete profile with photos and reviews builds trust before the customer even calls you.
The steps to create a Google Business Profile — teach them one by one:
1. Go to Google Business Profile: Search "Google Business Profile" on Google, or go to business.google.com.
2. Sign in with your Gmail account.
3. Enter your business name: The name customers know you by.
4. Choose your business category: E.g., "Phone repair shop", "Fashion designer", "Restaurant".
5. Add your location: Your address — or the area you serve if you work from home.
6. Add your contact information: Phone number, website (if you have one), and WhatsApp link.
7. Add your business hours: When you are open.
8. Add photos: A photo of you, your work, your products, and your location.
9. Verify your business: Google will send a verification code — by phone, email, or postcard. Enter the code to confirm you
are real.
10. Keep it updated: Update your hours, photos, and offers regularly.
Real-life comparison. Think of a phone book. A business listed in the phone book with a clear address, phone number, and
description — that business gets calls. A business not listed — that business is invisible. Google Business Profile is the digital
phone book.
Misconception to correct. "Google Business Profile is only for shops with a physical location." No — if you work from home
or serve customers at their location, you can list your service area instead of your address. The profile is for any business that
wants to be found.
F.4 Choose one social platform — and do it well
The idea. You do not need to be on every social platform. You need to be on one platform — and do it well. One platform
done well beats five done poorly.
How to choose — a practical decision rule:
Where is your customer? If your customer is on Instagram, choose Instagram. If on Facebook, choose Facebook. If on
TikTok, choose TikTok.
What content can you create easily? If you can take good photos, choose Instagram or Facebook. If you can make short
videos, choose TikTok or Instagram Reels.
Where are you most comfortable? Choose the platform you enjoy — because you will maintain it better.
The one-platform rule:
Choose one platform.
Post regularly — at least 3–4 times a week.
Use real photos of real work.
Include a clear price and a clear call to action.
Engage with comments and messages — reply fast.
Real-life comparison. Think of a market trader. A trader who sets up in one market, with a clear display and a friendly manner
— that trader has customers. A trader who runs between five markets, with no display and no manner — that trader is
exhausted and invisible. One market done well beats five done poorly.
Misconception to correct. "I need to be on every platform." No — you need to be on one platform, consistently. A business
with a strong Instagram presence and no Facebook — that business is findable. A business with five weak profiles — that
business is invisible everywhere.
F.5 Building trust — the signals that make customers choose you
The idea. Being found is not enough — customers must also trust you. Trust is built through consistent signals: a consistent
name and logo, fast replies, honest descriptions, and real customer feedback.
The six trust signals — teach them one by one:
1. Consistent name and logo: Use the same name and logo on WhatsApp Business, Google Business Profile, and social media
— so customers know it is the same business.
FastFix example: "FastFix Phone Repair" with the same logo everywhere.
2. Fast replies: Respond to messages within minutes — even if it is "I will get back to you in one hour." A fast reply signals
reliability.
FastFix example: Chidi replies to every WhatsApp message within 15 minutes.
3. Honest descriptions: Describe your work honestly — do not promise what you cannot deliver. An honest description
builds trust; a false one destroys it.
FastFix example: "Same-day screen repair with a 30-day warranty" — and Chidi delivers exactly that.
4. Real photos of real work: Use real photos of your actual work — not stock photos or a competitor's work. Real photos
prove you exist and can deliver.
FastFix example: Photos of actual phones Chidi has repaired.
5. Customer feedback: Ask happy customers for a testimonial — and share it (with permission). Real feedback is the
strongest trust signal.
FastFix example: "Chidi fixed my screen in 2 hours. It looks brand new. I recommend him." — Mr. Obi.
6. No fake testimonials: Never invent testimonials or reviews. Fake trust is worse than no trust — because when it is
discovered, it destroys your reputation permanently.
Say this: "A fake testimonial is a lie — and lies are always discovered. Real work builds real trust."
Real-life comparison. Think of a doctor. A doctor with a clear signboard, a clean office, a friendly manner, and real patient
testimonials — that doctor gets patients. A doctor with a fake degree, a dirty office, and invented testimonials — that doctor is
discovered and destroyed. Trust is the same in business.
Misconception to correct. "I need many testimonials to be trusted." No — you need a few real ones. Three honest
testimonials are worth more than thirty fake ones.
F.6 Seven days of free marketing — a plan that works
The idea. Marketing is not a one-off event — it is a regular habit. A seven-day plan gives you a week of content that builds
visibility and trust — without spending money.
The seven-day plan — teach it day by day:
Day 1 — Real work photo: Post a photo of your actual work — a repaired phone, a sewn dress, a plate of food. Include a
clear price and a call to action.
Day 2 — Before and after: Post a before-and-after photo — the problem and the solution. This shows your skill and the
value you provide.
Day 3 — Customer feedback: Share a real testimonial (with permission). This builds trust.
Day 4 — Price list: Post your price list — clear, honest, and easy to read. This attracts customers who are ready to buy.
Day 5 — Availability: Post your availability — "I am taking orders for this week. Call or WhatsApp to book." This creates
urgency.
Day 6 — Behind the scenes: Post a photo of you working — your workspace, your tools, your process. This builds
connection and trust.
Day 7 — Offer or reminder: Post a special offer (e.g., "10% off this week") or a reminder of your services. This encourages
action.
The rules of the seven-day plan:
1. Use real photos — of your real work.
2. Include a clear price — so customers know what to expect.
3. Include a clear call to action — "Call or WhatsApp to order."
4. Post at a consistent time — so customers know when to expect you.
5. Reply to every comment and message — fast.
Real-life comparison. Think of a market trader. A trader who puts out a clear display every day, with clear prices and a friendly
manner — that trader has regular customers. A trader who puts out a display once a month — that trader is forgotten. The
seven-day plan is the digital version of the daily display.
Misconception to correct. "Marketing is posting random photos." No — marketing is a plan. Seven days, seven types of
content, one goal: be visible and build trust.
F.7 Summary of the session
Being found online matters — customers search Google, WhatsApp and social media before they buy.
WhatsApp Business is a free digital shopfront — profile photo, description, hours, catalogue, greeting, labels.
Google Business Profile is a free listing for "near me" searches — create it, verify it, keep it updated.
Choose one social platform — and do it well. One platform done well beats five done poorly.
Build trust with six signals: consistent name and logo, fast replies, honest descriptions, real photos, real feedback, no fake
testimonials.
Plan seven days of free marketing — real work, before/after, feedback, price list, availability, behind the scenes, offer.

## H. GUIDED PRACTICAL EXERCISE (18 minutes)
<!-- kind:guided_practical -->
Objective: With the instructor guiding, each learner sets up a WhatsApp Business profile (or completes the checklist on paper)
and writes a seven-day free marketing plan.
Starting materials: A smartphone with WhatsApp (or a paper checklist); the WhatsApp Business setup checklist and seven-day
marketing plan template (projected and printed).
Procedure (instructor calls out each step; learners follow; instructor walks the room):
1. Write your name and today's date at the top of your workbook or notes app.
2. Set up WhatsApp Business: If you have a smartphone, download WhatsApp Business and set up your profile. If not,
complete the checklist on paper. (Worksheet 8.1.)
3. Profile photo: Choose a photo — of you, your logo, or your work.
4. Business name and description: Write your business name and a clear description — what you do, where you are, what
makes you different.
5. Business hours: Write your hours.
6. Catalogue: Add at least three items with photos, prices and descriptions.
7. Greeting message: Write your greeting message.
8. Labels: Create labels — new, quoted, paid, completed.
9. Pause and predict: Ask yourself: "If a customer found this profile, would they know what I do, where I am, and how to
contact me?"
10. Write the seven-day marketing plan: Fill in the template (Worksheet 8.5) — one type of content for each day.
11. Share: Show your profile and plan to your partner. Ask: "Is this complete? Is it clear? Would you trust this business?"
12. Revise: Based on your partner's feedback, revise if needed.
Expected result: A complete WhatsApp Business profile (or checklist) and a seven-day marketing plan.

## J. Real-World Case Study
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Situation. Blessing is a hairdresser in Port Harcourt. She works from a small salon near a busy junction. She is good at her work
— her customers are happy. But she has a problem: new customers do not know she exists. She has a personal Facebook page
she rarely updates. She has no WhatsApp Business profile. She has no Google Business Profile. When someone searches
"hairdresser near me" on Google, Blessing does not appear. She relies entirely on walk-in customers and word of mouth — and
business is slow.
Relevant facts.
Blessing has been a hairdresser for five years — she is skilled.
She charges ₦3,000–₦8,000 per style — a fair price.
She has a smartphone and uses WhatsApp personally.
She has about 200 contacts on her phone — mostly customers and friends.
She has never posted her work on social media — she feels shy.
Decision point. What is Blessing's real problem?
Possible approaches.
1. Lower her prices — "maybe I am too expensive."
2. Move to a bigger salon — "maybe my location is the problem."
3. Build a digital presence: WhatsApp Business, Google Business Profile, one social platform, regular posts.
Recommended solution. Approach 3. Blessing's problem is not her skill, her price, or her location — it is that she is invisible. A
hairdresser with a complete WhatsApp Business profile, a Google Business Profile, and regular Instagram posts — that
hairdresser gets found. And getting found is free.
Reasoning / example actions for Blessing:
WhatsApp Business: Set up a profile — photo of her work, description, hours, catalogue of styles with prices, greeting
message.
Google Business Profile: Create a profile — so she appears when someone searches "hairdresser near me."
Instagram: Choose Instagram — her customers are there, and she can post photos of her work. Post 3–4 times a week
— real photos, clear prices, clear call to action.
Trust-building: Ask happy customers for testimonials — and share them. Reply to every message fast. Use a consistent
name and logo.
Seven-day plan: Day 1 — a photo of a recent style. Day 2 — before and after. Day 3 — a customer testimonial. Day 4 —
a price list. Day 5 — availability. Day 6 — behind the scenes (her workspace). Day 7 — a special offer.
Trade-offs.
Approach 1 (lower prices) reduces her income without increasing customers.
Approach 2 (move to a bigger salon) costs money and does not solve the real problem.
Approach 3 (build a digital presence) costs nothing but time — and it solves the real problem.
Professional connection. The difference between a busy hairdresser and an empty hairdresser is not talent — it is visibility. A
hairdresser who is findable and trusted gets customers; a hairdresser who is invisible does not — no matter how skilled. Digital
presence is not a luxury — it is a necessity.

## K. Common Mistakes and Troubleshooting
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Use this table as the live guide. Point learners to the row rather than giving the answer.
K.1 No WhatsApp Business profile
Symptoms: The learner uses personal WhatsApp for business — no profile, no catalogue, no greeting. Likely cause: They do
not know WhatsApp Business exists — or they think it is only for big companies. How to investigate: Ask: "Do you have a
WhatsApp Business profile? What does it say about your business?" Corrective steps: Download WhatsApp Business and set up
a complete profile — seven parts. How to confirm resolved: The learner has a complete WhatsApp Business profile.
Prevention: WhatsApp Business is free and essential — set it up from day one.
K.2 No Google Business Profile
Symptoms: The learner does not appear when someone searches "near me" on Google. Likely cause: They do not have a
Google Business Profile. How to investigate: Ask: "Search your business name on Google. Do you appear?" Corrective steps:
Create a Google Business Profile — ten steps, verify it, keep it updated. How to confirm resolved: The learner appears in
Google searches. Prevention: Google Business Profile is free — create it and keep it updated.
K.3 Being on every platform — poorly
Symptoms: The learner has five social media profiles — all inactive or inconsistent. Likely cause: They think they need to be
everywhere — so they are nowhere. How to investigate: Ask: "Which platform do you post on regularly? When did you last
post?" Corrective steps: Choose one platform — and do it well. Post 3–4 times a week. How to confirm resolved: The
learner has one active platform. Prevention: One platform done well beats five done poorly.
K.4 Fake testimonials
Symptoms: The learner has testimonials that sound fake — or they are. Likely cause: They think testimonials must be
impressive — so they invent them. How to investigate: Ask: "Are these testimonials real? Can the customer confirm them?"
Corrective steps: Remove fake testimonials. Ask real customers for real feedback. How to confirm resolved: All testimonials
are real and confirmed. Prevention: A fake testimonial is a lie — and lies are always discovered. Real work builds real trust.
K.5 Inconsistent name and logo
Symptoms: The learner uses different names on different platforms — customers are confused. Likely cause: They have not
chosen a consistent brand identity. How to investigate: Ask: "What is your business name? Is it the same on WhatsApp, Google
and social media?" Corrective steps: Choose one name and one logo — and use them everywhere. How to confirm resolved:
The learner uses a consistent name and logo. Prevention: A consistent name and logo build recognition — choose them and
stick to them.
K.6 No regular posting
Symptoms: The learner posts once a month — or less. They are forgotten. Likely cause: They do not have a plan — so they
post randomly. How to investigate: Ask: "When did you last post? What did you post?" Corrective steps: Use the seven-day
plan — one type of content for each day. How to confirm resolved: The learner has a seven-day plan and posts regularly.
Prevention: Marketing is a habit — plan seven days, post consistently.
Distinguish likely vs confirmed: A "likely cause" becomes "confirmed" only after you check it with the learner — ask them the
investigation question and listen to the answer. Teach learners to confirm before concluding.
