# Document Formatting

## A. Session Identification

## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Change fonts and font sizes to create a professional appearance.
2. Apply bold, italic and underline to emphasise text.
3. Change text colour and highlighting for visual impact.
4. Set text alignment (left, centre, right, justify) appropriately.
5. Adjust line spacing and paragraph spacing for readability.
6. Create bulleted and numbered lists for organised information.
7. Use the Format Painter to copy formatting from one section to another.
8. Explain why consistent formatting matters in professional documents.
9. Apply the "less is more" principle — using formatting sparingly and purposefully.

## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
"Spot the Difference"
Instructor Setup: Display two versions of the same document on the projector: one plain (no formatting) and one
professionally formatted.
Instructor Script: "Good morning, everyone. Look at these two documents. They contain the exact same text. But one looks like
a school assignment, and the other looks like it was made by a professional. What is the difference? [Pause for answers — fonts,
sizes, bold, spacing, alignment.]
The difference is formatting. Formatting is how you make text look — the font, the size, the colour, the spacing, the alignment.
Good formatting makes a document easy to read and professional in appearance. Bad formatting makes a document hard to
read and amateur.
Today, you will learn the formatting tools that turn a plain document into a professional one. By the end of this session, you will
be able to take any plain text and make it look like it was designed by a professional. Let us begin."
Expected Learner Response: Learners will identify visible differences: fonts, bold text, spacing, alignment. This activates prior
knowledge and sets the context for the session.

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: Fonts and Font Sizes (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "A font is the style of the letters. The font you choose affects how your document looks and feels. Some fonts
are formal and professional. Others are casual and playful. Choosing the right font is an important part of document design.
Common Fonts:
Calibri — the default font in modern Word. Clean, modern, professional. Good for most documents.
Arial — similar to Calibri. Clean and professional. Good for business documents.
Times New Roman — the traditional serif font. Formal and academic. Good for reports and essays.
Cambria — a serif font. Formal and elegant. Good for professional documents.
Verdana — a sans-serif font. Clear and readable. Good for documents that will be read on screen.
Font Size: Font size is measured in points (pt). The default size in Word is 11pt or 12pt. Here is a general guide:
Body text: 11pt or 12pt
Headings: 14pt, 16pt or 18pt
Title: 18pt, 20pt or larger
Footnotes or captions: 9pt or 10pt
How to Change Font and Size:
1. Select the text you want to format.
2. Go to the Home tab on the Ribbon.
3. In the Font group, click the font name dropdown and choose a font.
4. Click the font size dropdown and choose a size.
5. Or use the Increase Font Size (A with an up arrow) and Decrease Font Size (A with a down arrow) buttons.
The "Less Is More" Rule: Use a maximum of two fonts in a professional document. One font for headings and one for body
text. Using more than two fonts makes a document look cluttered and unprofessional.
For example:
Headings: Calibri, 16pt, bold
Body text: Calibri, 11pt, regular
This is clean, consistent and professional."
Topic 2: Bold, Italic and Underline (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Bold, italic and underline are three ways to emphasise text.
Bold: Bold makes text thicker and darker. It is used for headings, subheadings and important words. To make text bold, select it
and click the Bold button (B) on the Home tab. Or press Ctrl+B.
Italic: Italic slants the text to the right. It is used for titles of books, foreign words and emphasis. To make text italic, select it and
click the Italic button (I) on the Home tab. Or press Ctrl+I.
Underline: Underline draws a line under the text. It is used for hyperlinks and sometimes for headings. To underline text, select it
and click the Underline button (U) on the Home tab. Or press Ctrl+U.
When to Use Each:
Bold: Headings, subheadings, important terms
Italic: Book titles, foreign words, subtle emphasis
Underline: Hyperlinks, some headings
Common Mistake — Over-Emphasis: Some learners make everything bold, italic and underlined. This is a mistake. When
everything is emphasised, nothing is emphasised. Use bold, italic and underline sparingly. Emphasise only the most important
words or phrases."
Topic 3: Text Colour and Highlighting (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Text colour and highlighting add visual impact to a document. But they should be used carefully.
Text Colour: To change the colour of text:
1. Select the text.
2. Go to the Home tab.
3. Click the Font Colour button (A with a coloured bar underneath).
4. Choose a colour from the palette.
Highlighting: Highlighting puts a coloured background behind text, like using a highlighter pen on paper. To highlight text:
1. Select the text.
2. Go to the Home tab.
3. Click the Text Highlight Colour button (a marker pen icon).
4. Choose a colour.
When to Use Colour:
Headings: A dark colour (dark blue, dark green, dark red) can make headings stand out.
Important information: Red or orange can draw attention to critical information.
Hyperlinks: Blue is the standard colour for hyperlinks.
When NOT to Use Colour:
Do not use bright colours (yellow, pink, lime green) for body text. They are hard to read.
Do not use too many colours. Stick to one or two colours for the entire document.
Do not use colour as the only way to convey meaning. Some people are colour-blind."
Topic 4: Alignment (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Alignment determines how text is positioned on the page. There are four types of alignment:
Left Align: Text is aligned to the left margin. The right edge is uneven. This is the default alignment and is used for most body
text. It is easy to read and looks clean.
Centre Align: Text is centred between the left and right margins. Both edges are uneven. This is used for titles, headings and
short lines of text. It is not used for long paragraphs because it is hard to read.
Right Align: Text is aligned to the right margin. The left edge is uneven. This is used for dates, page numbers and some design
elements. It is not used for body text.
Justify: Text is aligned to both the left and right margins. Both edges are even. This is used for formal documents like reports
and essays. It gives a clean, professional look but can create awkward spacing between words.
How to Change Alignment:
1. Select the text (or click in the paragraph you want to align).
2. Go to the Home tab.
3. In the Paragraph group, click one of the alignment buttons:
Align Left (Ctrl+L)
Centre (Ctrl+E)
Align Right (Ctrl+R)
Justify (Ctrl+J)
Best Practice: Use left align for body text. Use centre align for titles and headings. Use justify for formal reports if you want a
clean, even look."
Topic 5: Line Spacing and Paragraph Spacing (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Spacing is the amount of space between lines of text and between paragraphs. Good spacing makes a
document easy to read. Bad spacing makes it cramped or disjointed.
Line Spacing: Line spacing is the space between lines within a paragraph. The default is 1.15 or 1.5. For most documents, 1.5 is a
good choice. It gives enough space to read comfortably.
To change line spacing:
1. Select the text (or click in the paragraph).
2. Go to the Home tab.
3. In the Paragraph group, click the Line and Paragraph Spacing button (lines with an up and down arrow).
4. Choose a spacing option (1.0, 1.15, 1.5, 2.0, etc.).
Paragraph Spacing: Paragraph spacing is the space between paragraphs. By default, Word adds a small space after each
paragraph. You can increase or decrease this space.
To change paragraph spacing:
1. Select the text (or click in the paragraph).
2. Go to the Home tab.
3. In the Paragraph group, click the Line and Paragraph Spacing button.
4. Choose "Add Space Before Paragraph" or "Add Space After Paragraph."
Best Practice:
Use 1.5 line spacing for most documents.
Use a small space after each paragraph (6pt or 8pt).
Do not press Enter multiple times to create space between paragraphs. Use the paragraph spacing options instead."
Topic 6: Bullets and Numbering (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Bullets and numbering are used to organise information into lists. They make information easier to scan and
understand.
Bullets: Bullets are used for lists where the order does not matter. For example:
Apples
Oranges
Bananas
Numbering: Numbering is used for lists where the order matters. For example:
1. Preheat the oven
2. Mix the ingredients
3. Bake for 30 minutes
How to Create a List:
1. Type each item on a separate line.
2. Select all the items.
3. Go to the Home tab.
4. In the Paragraph group, click the Bullets button (three dots) or the Numbering button (three numbers).
5. The list is created.
Best Practice:
Use bullets for unordered lists.
Use numbering for ordered lists.
Keep list items short and parallel in structure.
Do not mix bullets and numbering in the same list."
Topic 7: Format Painter (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "The Format Painter is a tool that copies formatting from one section of text to another. It is like a paintbrush
for formatting.
How to Use Format Painter:
1. Select the text that has the formatting you want to copy.
2. Go to the Home tab.
3. In the Clipboard group, click the Format Painter button (a paintbrush icon).
4. The cursor changes to a paintbrush.
5. Select the text you want to apply the formatting to.
6. The formatting is copied.
Double-Click Trick: If you want to apply the same formatting to multiple sections, double-click the Format Painter button. This
keeps the Format Painter active until you press Escape or click the Format Painter button again.
When to Use Format Painter:
When you want to apply the same formatting to multiple headings.
When you want to copy the formatting of a paragraph to another paragraph.
When you want to ensure consistency across a document."

## G. INSTRUCTOR DEMONSTRATION (15 minutes)
<!-- kind:demo -->
Demonstration 1: Formatting a Document (10 minutes)
What the Instructor Prepares:
A plain-text document open on the projector (e.g., a sample CV)
What the Instructor Says and Does: "I am going to take this plain document and make it professional. Watch each step."
1. "First, I select the name at the top. I change the font to Calibri, size 18, bold, and centre align. This is the heading."
2. "I select the contact information below the name. I change the font to Calibri, size 10, and centre align."
3. "I select the 'Education' heading. I change the font to Calibri, size 14, bold, dark blue colour, and left align."
4. "I select the body text under Education. I change the font to Calibri, size 11, and set line spacing to 1.5."
5. "I repeat the same formatting for the 'Experience' heading and its body text."
6. "I select the 'Skills' heading. I apply the same formatting as the other headings."
7. "I select the list of skills. I click the Bullets button to create a bulleted list."
8. "I click the Format Painter. I select the 'Education' heading to copy its formatting. I then select the 'Experience' and 'Skills'
heads to apply the same formatting. Now all headings look consistent."
What Learners Should Observe: The instructor applying fonts, sizes, bold, colour, alignment, spacing, bullets and Format
Painter.
Expected Result: Learners can describe the formatting tools used and explain why each choice was made.
Questions the Instructor Can Ask:
"Why did I use the same formatting for all the headings?" (Answer: Consistency makes a document look professional.)
"Why did I use 1.5 line spacing?" (Answer: It makes the document easier to read.)
"Why did I use the Format Painter?" (Answer: It saves time and ensures consistency.)
Demonstration 2: Common Formatting Mistakes (5 minutes)
What the Instructor Prepares:
A document with intentional formatting mistakes
What the Instructor Says and Does: "Now I am going to show you some common formatting mistakes. See if you can spot
them."
1. "This document uses five different fonts. [Point to each font.] This looks cluttered and unprofessional. The fix: use a
maximum of two fonts."
2. "This heading is in bright yellow. [Point to the heading.] Yellow is hard to read on a white background. The fix: use a dark
colour like dark blue or dark green."
3. "This paragraph has no spacing between lines. [Point to the paragraph.] It looks cramped. The fix: use 1.5 line spacing."
4. "This list uses dashes instead of bullets. [Point to the list.] It looks unprofessional. The fix: use the Bullets button."
5. "This text is justified but has big gaps between words. [Point to the gaps.] Justify can create awkward spacing. The fix: use
left align for most documents."
What Learners Should Observe: The instructor identifying and correcting common formatting mistakes.
Expected Result: Learners can identify common formatting mistakes and explain how to fix them.

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "Chinedu's CV Disaster"
Situation: Chinedu is applying for a job. He creates a CV in Word, but he makes many formatting mistakes. He uses five
different fonts, bright colours, no spacing, and dashes instead of bullets. He sends the CV to the employer. He does not get an
interview.
The Problem: Chinedu's CV looks unprofessional. The employer receives hundreds of applications. A poorly formatted CV is an
easy one to reject.
Why It Matters: Your CV is your first impression. A well-formatted CV gets you an interview. A poorly formatted CV gets you
rejected. It is that simple.
Relevant Concept: Professional formatting. If Chinedu knew how to use fonts, sizes, alignment, spacing and bullets, his CV
would stand out — for the right reasons.
Worked Solution: If Chinedu had taken this course, here is what he would do:
1. Use one font (Calibri) for the entire document.
2. Use size 18 for the name, size 14 for headings, size 11 for body text.
3. Use bold for headings, regular for body text.
4. Use dark blue for headings, black for body text.
5. Use 1.5 line spacing for readability.
6. Use bullets for lists.
7. Centre align the name and contact information.
8. Left align everything else.
Reasoning: Consistent formatting shows that Chinedu is detail-oriented and professional. It makes the CV easy to read and
visually appealing. The employer can quickly find the information they need.
Possible Mistake: Chinedu might think that more formatting makes a better CV. He might use bright colours, multiple fonts
and lots of bold text. This makes the CV look cluttered and amateur.
Correction: Professional does not mean fancy. Professional means clean, consistent and easy to read. Use formatting sparingly
and purposefully.
Transfer Exercise: "Your friend Adaeze has a CV with no formatting at all — just plain text. What are three things you would tell
her to do to make it look professional?"
Professional Connection: Every job application requires a CV. Every CV should be professional. The formatting skills you learn in
this course will serve you every time you create a document for work, school or personal use.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Using Too Many Fonts
Symptoms: The learner uses four or five different fonts in one document. Likely Cause: The learner thinks more fonts make the
document look interesting. How to Investigate: Count the number of fonts in the document. Safe Corrective Steps: Explain
the "less is more" rule. Use a maximum of two fonts — one for headings and one for body text. How to Confirm the Problem Is

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (5 minutes)

Questions
1. Multiple Choice: What is the maximum number of fonts you should use in a professional document? a) One b) Two c) Three
d) Five
Answer: b) Two
2. Short Answer: What are the four types of text alignment?
Answer: Left align, centre align, right align, justify.
3. Multiple Choice: What does the Format Painter do? a) Copies text from one place to another b) Copies formatting from one
section to another c) Changes the font of the entire document d) Creates a bulleted list
Answer: b) Copies formatting from one section to another
4. Explain in Your Own Words: Why is it important to use consistent formatting in a document?
Answer: Consistent formatting makes a document look professional and organised. It shows that the creator is detail-oriented
and takes pride in their work. Inconsistent formatting makes a document look cluttered and amateur.
5. Practical Identification: Open a Word document. Identify the Font group, the Paragraph group, and the Format Painter
button on the Home tab.
Answer: The learner correctly identifies all three elements.
6. Scenario-Based: Your friend says, "I used five different fonts in my CV because I wanted it to look interesting." What advice
would you give?
Answer: Using too many fonts makes a document look cluttered and unprofessional. Use a maximum of two fonts — one for
headings and one for body text. Keep it clean and consistent.
7. Error-Detecting: A learner writes in their assignment: "I pressed Enter four times to create space between paragraphs." What
is wrong with this, and what should they have done instead?
Answer: Pressing Enter multiple times creates inconsistent spacing. The learner should have used the Line and Paragraph
Spacing options on the Home tab to set a specific amount of space after each paragraph.

## N. REFLECTION AND RECAP (5 minutes)

Plain-English Summary
Today you learned how to format documents professionally in Microsoft Word. You learned about fonts, font sizes, bold, italic,
underline, text colour, highlighting, alignment, line spacing, paragraph spacing, bullets, numbering and the Format Painter. You
also learned the "less is more" rule — use formatting sparingly and purposefully. These are the tools that turn a plain document
into a professional one.
Five Important Takeaways
1. Use a maximum of two fonts in a professional document.
2. Use bold, italic and underline sparingly. Emphasise only the most important text.
3. Use left align for body text and centre align for titles and headings.
4. Use 1.5 line spacing for readability.
5. Use the Format Painter to ensure consistency across your document.
Questions That Help You Recognise What You Can Now Do
Can you change fonts and font sizes?
Can you apply bold, italic and underline?
Can you change text alignment?
Can you adjust line spacing and paragraph spacing?
Can you create bulleted and numbered lists?
Can you use the Format Painter?
Connection to the Next Session

tables. You will also learn how to print and export to PDF. These are the skills that complete your Word toolkit. You now know
how to format text. Next, you will learn to format entire pages.

Common Misconceptions
"More formatting is better." Some learners think that using lots of fonts, colours and effects makes a document look
professional. Correct this by explaining the "less is more" rule.
"Format Painter is only for experts." Some learners think the Format Painter is an advanced tool. Correct this by showing
them how simple it is to use.
"Justify is always the best alignment." Some learners think justify is the most professional alignment. Correct this by
explaining that justify can create awkward spacing and that left align is usually the best choice for body text.
Suggested Remedial Exercises
For learners who use too many fonts: Give them a document with five fonts and ask them to reduce it to two.
For learners who over-emphasise text: Give them a document where everything is bold, italic and underlined. Ask them to
remove the unnecessary emphasis.
For learners who cannot use the Format Painter: Walk them through the process step by step. Let them practise multiple
times.
