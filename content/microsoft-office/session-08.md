# Professional Presentations

## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Insert and format images in a presentation.
2. Insert and format shapes and SmartArt for visual communication.
3. Add transitions between slides for smooth flow.
4. Add simple animations to text and objects.
5. Use Presenter View to see notes and upcoming slides while presenting.
6. Deliver a presentation aloud with confidence and clarity.
7. Apply the principles of good presentation design: minimal text, strong visuals, clear structure.
8. Give and receive constructive feedback on presentations.

## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
"The 2-Minute Pitch"
Instructor Setup: Deliver a short, engaging presentation on a simple topic (e.g., "Why I Love Port Harcourt") using good
presentation skills — minimal text, strong visuals, confident delivery.
Instructor Script: "Good morning, everyone. Before we start the session, I am going to give you a short presentation. Watch
carefully — I want you to notice what makes it engaging."
- Deliver a 2-minute presentation with 3 slides: a title slide, one image slide with a single sentence, and a closing slide. Use
minimal text, a strong image and confident delivery.]
"What made that presentation engaging? [Pause for answers — minimal text, image, confidence, eye contact, enthusiasm.]
Today, you will learn how to create and deliver presentations like that. You will learn how to insert images, add transitions and
animations, and deliver a presentation with confidence. By the end of this session, you will present your own short presentation
to the class. Let us begin."
Expected Learner Response: Learners will identify the key elements of a good presentation: minimal text, strong visuals,
confidence, enthusiasm. This sets the context for the session.

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Instructor Script: "Before we start, let us remember what we learned in the last session. Quick review."
Review Questions and Expected Answers:
1. "How do you open Microsoft PowerPoint?"
Click the Start button, type "PowerPoint," and click on Microsoft PowerPoint.
2. "What is a slide layout?"
A pre-designed arrangement of text boxes, images and other elements.
3. "What is a theme?"
A pre-designed colour scheme, font and effect that gives a presentation a consistent appearance.
4. "What is the 6x6 rule?"
No more than 6 words per line and 6 lines per slide.
5. "How do you add a new slide?"
Go to the Home tab and click the New Slide button.
How to Address Common Gaps:
If a learner cannot remember the 6x6 rule, write it on the whiteboard.
If a learner forgot how to apply a theme, remind them: "Go to the Design tab."

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: Inserting and Formatting Images (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Images make presentations engaging and memorable. A good image can communicate an idea more
effectively than a paragraph of text.
Inserting an Image:
1. Go to the Insert tab.
2. Click the Pictures button.
3. Choose "This Device" to insert an image from your computer.
4. Navigate to the image file.
5. Click "Insert."
Formatting an Image:
1. Click on the image to select it.
2. The Picture Format tab appears on the Ribbon.
3. Use the tools to:
Crop the image (remove unwanted parts).
Adjust brightness and contrast.
Apply a picture style (border, shadow, reflection).
Resize the image (drag the corners).
Move the image (drag it to a new position).
Image Tips:
Use high-quality images. Blurry or pixelated images look unprofessional.
Use images that support your message. Do not use images just for decoration.
Do not stretch images. Drag the corners to resize proportionally.
Use the "Compress Pictures" tool (Picture Format tab) to reduce file size if needed."
Topic 2: Shapes and SmartArt (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Shapes and SmartArt are visual elements that help you communicate ideas clearly.
Inserting a Shape:
1. Go to the Insert tab.
2. Click the Shapes button.
3. Choose a shape (e.g., rectangle, oval, arrow).
4. Click and drag on the slide to draw the shape.
5. Use the Shape Format tab to change the fill colour, outline and effects.
Inserting SmartArt: SmartArt is a collection of pre-designed diagrams — processes, hierarchies, cycles, relationships. They are
useful for showing:
Processes (e.g., steps in a workflow)
Hierarchies (e.g., organisational structure)
Cycles (e.g., a repeating process)
Relationships (e.g., how different elements connect)
To insert SmartArt:
1. Go to the Insert tab.
2. Click the SmartArt button.
3. Choose a category (e.g., Process, Hierarchy, Cycle).
4. Choose a diagram.
5. Click OK.
6. Type your text in the text boxes.
Shape and SmartArt Tips:
Use shapes to highlight important information or create simple diagrams.
Use SmartArt to show processes, hierarchies and relationships.
Keep it simple. Do not use too many shapes or too much text in SmartArt."
Topic 3: Transitions and Animations (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Transitions and animations add movement and flow to a presentation. But they should be used sparingly.
Too many transitions and animations are distracting.
Transitions: A transition is the effect that occurs when you move from one slide to the next. Common transitions include:
Fade: The previous slide fades out and the next slide fades in.
Push: The previous slide pushes the next slide into view.
Wipe: The next slide wipes across the previous slide.
None: No transition. The next slide simply appears.
To apply a transition:
1. Select the slide you want to apply the transition to.
2. Go to the Transitions tab.
3. Choose a transition from the gallery.
4. Click "Apply to All" if you want the same transition for all slides.
Animations: An animation is the effect that occurs when an object (text, image, shape) appears on a slide. Common animations
include:
Appear: The object simply appears.
Fade: The object fades in.
Fly In: The object flies in from a direction.
Wipe: The object wipes into view.
To apply an animation:
1. Select the object you want to animate.
2. Go to the Animations tab.
3. Choose an animation from the gallery.
4. Use the Timing group to set the start option (On Click, With Previous, After Previous).
Best Practice:
Use transitions sparingly. One transition for the entire presentation is usually enough.
Use animations to reveal information step by step, not to entertain.
Avoid distracting transitions like "Spinner," "Gallery" or "Conveyor Belt."
Keep it simple and professional."
Topic 4: Presenter View and Delivery (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Presenter View is a special view that helps you deliver a presentation confidently. It shows:
The current slide (large, for the audience).
The next slide (small, so you know what is coming).
Your speaker notes (so you do not have to memorise everything).
A timer (so you know how long you have been speaking).
How to Use Presenter View:
1. Connect your computer to a projector or second screen.
2. Go to the Slide Show tab.
3. Click "From Beginning" or "From Current Slide."
4. PowerPoint automatically uses Presenter View if a second screen is detected.
5. If not, right-click on the slide and choose "Presenter View."
Delivery Tips:
Know your content. Practise your presentation several times before delivering it.
Speak clearly and slowly. Do not rush. Pause between points.
Make eye contact. Look at the audience, not at the screen.
Use body language. Stand up straight, use hand gestures, move around.
Do not read from the slides. The slides are for the audience. Your notes are for you.
Handle questions confidently. If you do not know the answer, say so and offer to follow up."

Practical Objective: The learner will create a 5-slide business pitch presentation with images, transitions, animations and

Starting Materials:
PowerPoint open on each machine with a blank presentation

Instructions:
Part 1: Create the Slides (10 minutes)
1. "Create a title slide with the name of a fictional business (e.g., 'Adaeze's Fashion Design') and your name."
2. "Add a slide with the title 'About Us' and a short description of the business."
3. "Add a slide with the title 'Our Products' and insert an image of a product."
4. "Add a slide with the title 'Pricing' and a simple price list."
5. "Add a slide with the title 'Contact Us' and contact information."
Instructor Prompts:
"How many slides do you have?" (Answer: 5.)
"Did you follow the 6x6 rule?" (Answer: Yes — minimal text on each slide.)
Part 2: Add Images, Transitions and Animations (5 minutes)
6. "Insert an image on the 'About Us' slide. Resize and format it."
7. "Go to the Transitions tab. Choose 'Fade.' Click 'Apply to All.'"
8. "Select the title on the first slide. Go to the Animations tab. Choose 'Appear.'"
9. "Select the image on the 'Our Products' slide. Go to the Animations tab. Choose 'Fade.'"
Instructor Prompts:
"Why did we use 'Apply to All' for the transition?" (Answer: To keep the presentation consistent.)
"Why did we use different animations for the title and the image?" (Answer: To reveal information step by step.)
Part 3: Add Speaker Notes and Save (5 minutes)
10. "Click on the first slide. Go to the Notes Pane at the bottom. Type a short note about what you will say."
11. "Repeat for all slides."
12. "Press Ctrl+S. Navigate to the Desktop. Type 'My Business Pitch' in the File name box. Click Save."

speaker notes.

If the learner cannot add a transition: "Go to the Transitions tab and choose a transition."
If the learner cannot add speaker notes: "Look at the bottom of the screen for the Notes Pane. If you do not see it, go to the
View tab and click 'Notes.'"
Completion Checklist:
5 slides created (Title, About Us, Products, Pricing, Contact)
Image inserted and formatted on the About Us slide
Transition applied to all slides
Animations added to title and image
Speaker notes added to all slides
Presentation saved as "My Business Pitch" on the Desktop

## I. INDEPENDENT CLASS EXERCISE (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:exercise -->
Exercise: "Present My Business Pitch"
Scenario: "You are pitching your fictional business to a group of investors (the class). You have 2 minutes to present your 5-slide
presentation."
Task: Deliver your "My Business Pitch" presentation aloud to the class. Use the following guidelines:
1. Stand up and face the audience.
2. Speak clearly and slowly.
3. Make eye contact with the audience.
4. Do not read from the slides — use your speaker notes.
5. Use the 6x6 rule — let the slides support your message, not replace it.
Requirements:
Present all 5 slides
Speak for at least 1 minute but no more than 3 minutes
Use Presenter View if possible
Time Allowance: 2–3 minutes per learner (depending on class size)
Success Criteria:
The learner presents all 5 slides
The learner speaks clearly and confidently
The learner makes eye contact with the audience
The learner does not read from the slides
The learner finishes within the time limit
Hints Available If the Learner Gets Stuck:
Hint 1: "Take a deep breath before you start. It is normal to be nervous."
Hint 2: "If you forget what to say, look at your speaker notes. They are there to help you."
Hint 3: "Remember: the slides are for the audience. Your words are the presentation."
Complete Answer Key for the Instructor:
There is no single correct answer for a presentation. The instructor should use the "Presentation Feedback Form" to evaluate
each learner on:
Content (Is the information clear and organised?)
Delivery (Does the speaker speak clearly and confidently?)
Eye contact (Does the speaker look at the audience?)
Time management (Does the speaker finish within the time limit?)
Slide design (Are the slides clean and professional?)

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "Emmanuel's Investor Pitch"
Situation: Emmanuel has developed a mobile app for small businesses. He has been invited to pitch to investors. He creates a
presentation with 20 slides, too much text and no design. He delivers it by reading from the slides. The investors are bored. They
do not invest.
The Problem: Emmanuel's presentation was too long, too text-heavy and poorly delivered. The investors could not see the
value of his app.
Why It Matters: A good presentation can win investors, customers and partners. A bad presentation can lose them. In the
professional world, your presentation is often your only chance to make a good impression.
Relevant Concept: Professional presentation design and delivery. If Emmanuel knew how to create clean slides, use minimal
text and deliver with confidence, he would have a better chance of winning the investors.
Worked Solution: If Emmanuel had taken this course, here is what he would do:
1. Create 7 slides: Title, Problem, Solution, How It Works, Market, Business Model, Call to Action.
2. Use minimal text — just key points.
3. Use images and screenshots to illustrate the app.
4. Apply a clean, professional theme.
5. Add a simple transition (Fade) to all slides.
6. Add animations to reveal information step by step.
7. Write speaker notes for each slide.
8. Practise the presentation several times.
9. Deliver with confidence, eye contact and enthusiasm.
Reasoning: A good presentation tells a story. It has a beginning (the problem), a middle (the solution) and an end (the call to
action). It uses visuals to support the message, not replace it. It is delivered with confidence and enthusiasm.
Possible Mistake: Emmanuel might think that more slides means a more thorough presentation. In reality, fewer slides with
clear, concise information are more effective.
Correction: Aim for 1 slide per minute of presentation time. A 10-minute presentation should have no more than 10–12 slides. A
2-minute pitch should have no more than 5–7 slides.
Transfer Exercise: "Your friend Adaeze is presenting a school project. She has 30 slides and reads from each one. What advice
would you give her?"
Professional Connection: Every professional needs to present information at some point. Job interviews, business pitches,
school presentations, church sermons — everyone. Knowing how to create and deliver a good presentation is a valuable
professional skill.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Reading from the Slides
Symptoms: The learner reads the text on the slides word for word. Likely Cause: The learner is nervous or has not practised.
How to Investigate: Watch the learner present. Are they looking at the screen or the audience? Safe Corrective Steps: Teach
the learner to use speaker notes instead of reading from the slides. Encourage them to practise several times. How to Confirm
the Problem Is Resolved: The learner does not read from the slides. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make speaker notes a
standard requirement for every presentation.
Mistake 2: Using Too Many Transitions and Animations
Symptoms: The learner uses a different transition for every slide and multiple animations per slide. Likely Cause: The learner
thinks more effects make the presentation more engaging. How to Investigate: Watch the presentation. Are the transitions and
animations distracting? Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to use one transition for the entire presentation and
animations sparingly. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner uses transitions and animations sparingly. How
to Prevent Recurrence: Make "one transition for all slides" a standard requirement.
Mistake 3: Not Making Eye Contact
Symptoms: The learner looks at the screen or the floor while presenting. Likely Cause: The learner is nervous or reading from
the slides. How to Investigate: Watch the learner present. Where are they looking? Safe Corrective Steps: Encourage the
learner to look at the audience. Teach them to use speaker notes instead of reading from the slides. How to Confirm the
Problem Is Resolved: The learner makes eye contact with the audience. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make eye contact a
standard part of presentation delivery practice.
Mistake 4: Speaking Too Fast
Symptoms: The learner rushes through the presentation. Likely Cause: The learner is nervous or has not practised. How to
Investigate: Time the presentation. Is it much shorter than expected? Safe Corrective Steps: Encourage the learner to speak
slowly and pause between points. Teach them to practise with a timer. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner
speaks at a normal pace. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make practising with a timer a standard part of presentation
preparation.
Mistake 5: Not Using Presenter View
Symptoms: The learner presents without Presenter View. They cannot see their notes or the next slide. Likely Cause: The learner
does not know about Presenter View. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Did you use Presenter View?" Safe Corrective
Steps: Teach the learner to use Presenter View. Show them how to access it. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The
learner uses Presenter View. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make Presenter View a standard part of presentation delivery
practice.

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
Questions
1. Multiple Choice: What is the purpose of a transition in PowerPoint? a) To add text to a slide b) To create an effect when
moving from one slide to the next c) To insert an image d) To apply a theme
Answer: b) To create an effect when moving from one slide to the next
2. Short Answer: What is Presenter View and what does it show?
Answer: Presenter View is a special view that shows the current slide, the next slide, speaker notes and a timer. It helps the
presenter deliver the presentation confidently.
3. Multiple Choice: What is the 6x6 rule? a) No more than 6 slides and 6 themes b) No more than 6 words per line and 6 lines per
slide c) No more than 6 images and 6 charts d) No more than 6 colours and 6 fonts
Answer: b) No more than 6 words per line and 6 lines per slide
4. Explain in Your Own Words: Why should you not read from the slides during a presentation?
Answer: Reading from the slides makes the presentation boring and disengaging. The slides are for the audience — they should
support your message, not replace it. The presenter should speak to the audience, using the slides as visual aids.
5. Practical Identification: Open PowerPoint. Identify the Insert tab, the Transitions tab, the Animations tab, and the Slide Show
tab.
Answer: The learner correctly identifies all four tabs.
6. Scenario-Based: Your friend says, "I used a different transition for every slide because I wanted the presentation to be
interesting." What advice would you give?
Answer: Using too many different transitions is distracting and unprofessional. Use one transition for the entire presentation.
This keeps the presentation consistent and allows the audience to focus on the content.
7. Error-Detecting: A learner writes in their assignment: "I read from the slides because I was nervous and did not want to
forget what to say." What is wrong with this, and what should they have done instead?
Answer: Reading from the slides makes the presentation boring. The learner should have written speaker notes and practised
the presentation several times. Speaker notes are there to help you remember what to say without reading from the slides.

Common Misconceptions
"More transitions and animations make a better presentation." Some learners think that more effects make a
presentation more engaging. Correct this by explaining that too many effects are distracting.
"Reading from the slides is fine." Some learners think that reading from the slides is acceptable. Correct this by explaining
that reading from the slides makes the presentation boring.
"Presenter View is only for experts." Some learners think Presenter View is an advanced feature. Correct this by showing
them how simple it is to use.
Suggested Remedial Exercises
For learners who read from the slides: Have them practise presenting with speaker notes only (no looking at the screen).
For learners who use too many transitions: Have them create a presentation with one transition for all slides.
For learners who do not make eye contact: Have them practise presenting to a mirror or a friend.
