## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Sort data alphabetically, numerically and by custom criteria.
2. Filter data to show only specific records.
3. Apply conditional formatting to highlight important data.
4. Create simple charts (bar, pie, line) from data.
5. Format charts with titles, labels and colours.
6. Explain why data management tools are essential for analysis.
7. Choose the right chart type for different data.

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Instructor Script: "Before we start, let us remember what we learned in the last session. Quick review."
Review Questions and Expected Answers:
1. "What must every formula start with?"
An equals sign (=).
2. "What does the SUM function do?"
It adds a range of cells.
3. "What is the difference between a formula and a function?"
A formula is any calculation that starts with =. A function is a predefined formula (e.g., SUM, AVERAGE).
4. "What does AutoSum do?"
It automatically creates a SUM formula for adjacent cells.
5. "What does the #VALUE! error mean?"
The formula contains the wrong type of data (e.g., text instead of a number).
How to Address Common Gaps:
If a learner cannot remember the functions, write them on the whiteboard: SUM, AVERAGE, COUNT, MIN, MAX.
If a learner forgot the equals sign, remind them: "Every formula starts with =."

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: Sorting Data (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Sorting means arranging data in a specific order. You can sort alphabetically (A to Z or Z to A), numerically
(smallest to largest or largest to smallest), or by date (oldest to newest or newest to oldest).
How to Sort Data:
1. Click anywhere in the column you want to sort by.
2. Go to the Data tab on the Ribbon.
3. Click the Sort A to Z button (for ascending order) or the Sort Z to A button (for descending order).
4. Excel sorts the entire dataset based on the selected column.
Custom Sort: If you want to sort by multiple criteria (e.g., first by Category, then by Total), use the Custom Sort:
1. Go to the Data tab.
2. Click the Sort button.
3. In the Sort dialog box, choose the first column to sort by.
4. Click "Add Level" to add a second criterion.
5. Choose the second column to sort by.
6. Click OK.
Sort Tips:
Always include the header row in your selection. Excel will ask if your data has headers. Say "Yes."
Sorting does not change the data — it only changes the order.
You can undo a sort with Ctrl+Z."
Topic 2: Filtering Data (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Filtering means showing only the records that meet specific criteria. For example, you might want to see
only the sales for a specific product, or only the sales above a certain amount.
How to Filter Data:
1. Click anywhere in your dataset.
2. Go to the Data tab.
3. Click the Filter button.
4. Dropdown arrows appear in the header row.
5. Click on a dropdown arrow to see the filter options.
6. Check or uncheck the items you want to show or hide.
7. Click OK.
Filter Tips:
You can filter by multiple columns at the same time.
To clear a filter, click the Filter button again or click "Clear" in the dropdown.
Filtering does not delete data — it only hides the records that do not meet the criteria.
You can use the Search box in the filter dropdown to quickly find specific items."
Topic 3: Conditional Formatting (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Conditional formatting automatically applies formatting (e.g., colour, bold, icon) to cells that meet specific
criteria. For example, you could highlight all sales above ₦500,000 in green, or all sales below ₦300,000 in red.
How to Apply Conditional Formatting:
1. Select the cells you want to format.
2. Go to the Home tab.
3. Click the Conditional Formatting button.
4. Choose a rule type (e.g., Highlight Cells Rules, Top/Bottom Rules, Data Bars, Color Scales).
5. Set the criteria (e.g., "Greater than 500000").
6. Choose the formatting (e.g., green fill).
7. Click OK.
Common Uses:
Highlight cells greater than or less than a value.
Highlight the top 10 or bottom 10 items.
Use data bars to show relative values visually.
Use colour scales to show a gradient from low to high."
Topic 4: Creating Charts (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Charts are visual representations of data. They make it easy to see patterns, trends and comparisons. Excel
can create many types of charts, but we will focus on the three most common: bar charts, pie charts and line charts.
Bar Charts: Bar charts are used to compare values across categories. For example, you could use a bar chart to compare the
total sales of different products.
Pie Charts: Pie charts are used to show proportions of a whole. For example, you could use a pie chart to show what percentage
of total sales comes from each product.
Line Charts: Line charts are used to show trends over time. For example, you could use a line chart to show how sales have
changed over the past 12 months.
How to Create a Chart:
1. Select the data you want to chart (including headers).
2. Go to the Insert tab.
3. Click the type of chart you want (e.g., Clustered Column for a bar chart, Pie, Line).
4. Excel creates the chart.
5. Use the Chart Tools tabs (Design and Format) to customise the chart.
Chart Tips:
Always include headers in your selection. Excel uses them for chart labels.
Choose the right chart type for your data.
Add a chart title and axis labels.
Keep it simple — do not use too many colours or effects."

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "Adaeze's Sales Analysis"
Situation: Adaeze is the sales manager of a provision shop in Port Harcourt. She has a dataset of 100 sales transactions. She
needs to analyse the data to find out which products are selling well, which salespeople are performing best, and what the total
revenue is.
The Problem: Adaeze has 100 records to analyse. Doing it manually would take hours and be error-prone.
Why It Matters: Data management tools save time and provide insights that manual analysis cannot. If Adaeze can sort, filter
and chart her data, she can make better business decisions.
Relevant Concept: Data management. If Adaeze knew how to use sorting, filtering, conditional formatting and charts, she
could analyse her data in minutes.
Worked Solution: If Adaeze had taken this course, here is what she would do:
1. Sort the data by Total in descending order to find the highest sales.
2. Filter the data by salesperson to see individual performance.
3. Apply conditional formatting to highlight high-value sales.
4. Create a summary table with total sales by product.
5. Create a bar chart to visualise the data.
6. Use the insights to make better business decisions.
Reasoning: Data management tools turn raw data into actionable insights. They help you see patterns, identify problems and
make informed decisions.
Possible Mistake: Adaeze might create a chart without a title or labels. This makes the chart hard to understand.
Correction: Always add a chart title and axis labels. A chart without labels is just a pretty picture — it does not communicate
anything.
Transfer Exercise: "Your friend Chinedu has a dataset of student grades. He wants to find out which students are performing
well and which need help. What advice would you give him?"
Professional Connection: Every job that involves data requires data management skills. Sales managers, accountants, teachers,
administrators — everyone. Knowing how to sort, filter and chart data makes you more employable and more capable.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Sorting Without Headers
Symptoms: The learner sorts the data but the header row is included in the sort. Likely Cause: The learner did not tell Excel that
the data has headers. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Did Excel ask if your data has headers? What did you say?" Safe
Corrective Steps: When sorting, Excel asks "Does your data have headers?" Make sure to say "Yes." This keeps the header row at
the top. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The header row stays at the top after sorting. How to Prevent
Recurrence: Remind the learner to always say "Yes" when Excel asks about headers.
Mistake 2: Filtering the Wrong Column
Symptoms: The learner filters the data but the wrong records are shown. Likely Cause: The learner filtered by the wrong
column. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Which column did you filter by?" Safe Corrective Steps: Clear the filter and
apply it to the correct column. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The correct records are shown. How to Prevent
Recurrence: Remind the learner to check which column they are filtering by.
Mistake 3: Creating a Chart Without Headers
Symptoms: The chart has no labels or the labels are wrong. Likely Cause: The learner did not include headers in the chart
selection. How to Investigate: Check the chart. Are there labels? Safe Corrective Steps: Select the data including headers,
then create the chart. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The chart has correct labels. How to Prevent Recurrence:
Remind the learner to always include headers in the chart selection.
Mistake 4: Using the Wrong Chart Type
Symptoms: The chart does not effectively communicate the data. Likely Cause: The learner chose the wrong chart type. How
to Investigate: Ask the learner, "What type of chart did you create? Is it the best choice for this data?" Safe Corrective Steps:
Explain the different chart types and when to use each. Help the learner choose the right one. How to Confirm the Problem Is
Resolved: The learner chooses the correct chart type. How to Prevent Recurrence: Provide a "Chart Types Reference"
handout.
Mistake 5: Not Adding Chart Titles and Labels
Symptoms: The chart has no title or axis labels. Likely Cause: The learner does not know how to add them. How to Investigate:
Look at the chart. Is there a title? Are there axis labels? Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to add chart titles and axis labels
from the Chart Tools Design tab. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The chart has a title and axis labels. How to
Prevent Recurrence: Make chart titles and labels a standard requirement for every chart exercise.

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
Questions
1. Multiple Choice: What does sorting do? a) Deletes data that is not needed b) Arranges data in a specific order c) Highlights
important data d) Creates a visual representation of data
Answer: b) Arranges data in a specific order
2. Short Answer: What is the difference between sorting and filtering?
Answer: Sorting arranges all the data in a specific order. Filtering shows only the records that meet specific criteria and hides the
rest.
3. Multiple Choice: What type of chart is best for comparing values across categories? a) Pie chart b) Line chart c) Bar chart d)
Scatter plot
Answer: c) Bar chart
4. Explain in Your Own Words: Why is it important to add titles and labels to a chart?
Answer: Titles and labels tell the viewer what the chart is about and what the axes represent. Without them, the chart is just a
pretty picture that does not communicate anything.
5. Practical Identification: Open Excel. Identify the Data tab, the Sort button, the Filter button, and the Conditional Formatting
button.
Answer: The learner correctly identifies all four elements.
6. Scenario-Based: Your friend says, "I created a chart but it does not have any labels. What did I do wrong?" What advice would
you give?
Answer: You probably did not include headers in your chart selection. To fix this, select the data including headers and create a
new chart. Then add a chart title and axis labels from the Chart Tools Design tab.
7. Error-Detecting: A learner writes in their assignment: "I sorted my data but the header row is now mixed in with the data."
What went wrong, and how can they fix it?
Answer: The learner did not tell Excel that the data has headers. To fix this, undo the sort (Ctrl+Z), then sort again and make sure
to say "Yes" when Excel asks if your data has headers.

Common Misconceptions
"Sorting and filtering are the same thing." Some learners confuse sorting and filtering. Correct this by explaining that
sorting arranges all data, while filtering hides some data.
"Charts are just for decoration." Some learners think charts are only for making documents look pretty. Correct this by
explaining that charts communicate data visually.
"Conditional formatting is too advanced." Some learners think conditional formatting is an advanced feature. Correct this
by showing them how simple it is to apply.
Suggested Remedial Exercises
For learners who cannot sort: Have them practise sorting a small dataset (5 rows) multiple times.
For learners who cannot filter: Have them practise filtering by different criteria (e.g., by product, by salesperson, by date).
For learners who cannot create charts: Walk them through the process step by step. Let them practise creating different
chart types.
