## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Explain what Microsoft PowerPoint is and what it is used for.
2. Open Microsoft PowerPoint from the Start menu.
3. Identify the main parts of the PowerPoint interface: the Ribbon, slides pane, slide area, notes pane, and status bar.
4. Create a new presentation from a blank slide or a template.
5. Add, delete and rearrange slides.
6. Apply slide layouts (Title Slide, Title and Content, Two Content, etc.).
7. Apply themes and colour schemes for a professional appearance.
8. Save and open presentations.
9. Explain why PowerPoint is different from Word and Excel and when to use each.

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: What Is Microsoft PowerPoint? (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Microsoft PowerPoint is a presentation program. It is used to create slideshows — a series of slides that
contain text, images, charts and other content. Presentations are used for:
Business pitches and sales presentations
School projects and lectures
Church sermons and community meetings
Training sessions and workshops
Conferences and seminars
PowerPoint vs Word vs Excel:
Word is for documents (letters, reports, CVs).
Excel is for spreadsheets (budgets, data, calculations).
PowerPoint is for presentations (slides, speeches, visual aids).
Why PowerPoint Matters: In the professional world, you will often need to present information to others. A good presentation
can help you win a job, secure a loan, sell a product or teach a class. Knowing how to use PowerPoint is a valuable professional
skill."
Topic 2: Opening PowerPoint and the Interface (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Let us open PowerPoint and explore the interface.
Opening PowerPoint:
1. Click the Start button.
2. Type "PowerPoint" in the search box.
3. Click on "Microsoft PowerPoint."
4. PowerPoint opens. You will see a start screen with templates. Click "Blank Presentation" to create a new presentation.
The PowerPoint Interface — Main Parts:
1. The Title Bar (top): Shows the name of the presentation and the program. By default, a new presentation is called
"Presentation1."
2. The Ribbon (below the title bar): Like Word and Excel, the Ribbon contains all the tools, organised into tabs:
File: Open, save, print, export
Home: Font, paragraph, drawing, editing
Insert: Tables, images, shapes, charts, text boxes
Design: Themes, variants, slide size
Transitions: Slide transitions
Animations: Object animations
Slide Show: Presenter view, setup
Review: Spelling, comments
View: Layout views, zoom
3. The Slides Pane (left): This shows thumbnails of all the slides in your presentation. You can click on a thumbnail to select
and edit a slide. You can also drag thumbnails to rearrange slides.
4. The Slide Area (centre): This is the large area where you create and edit the current slide. It shows the slide exactly as it will
appear during the presentation.
5. The Notes Pane (bottom): This is where you can add speaker notes for each slide. These notes are visible to the presenter but
not to the audience.
6. The Status Bar (bottom): Shows the slide number, total slides, language and zoom level."
Topic 3: Creating and Managing Slides (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Now let us learn how to create and manage slides.
Adding a New Slide:
1. Go to the Home tab.
2. Click the New Slide button.
3. A dropdown menu appears with different slide layouts.
4. Choose a layout (e.g., Title Slide, Title and Content, Two Content).
5. A new slide is added to the presentation.
Slide Layouts: A slide layout is a pre-designed arrangement of text boxes, images and other elements. Common layouts include:
Title Slide: A title and a subtitle. Used for the first slide.
Title and Content: A title and a content area (text, images, charts, etc.). Used for most slides.
Two Content: A title and two content areas. Used for comparisons.
Section Header: A title and a subtitle. Used to separate sections.
Blank: No pre-designed elements. Used for custom layouts.
Deleting a Slide:
1. Click on the slide thumbnail in the Slides Pane.
2. Press Delete or right-click and choose "Delete Slide."
Rearranging Slides:
1. Click and drag a slide thumbnail to a new position in the Slides Pane.
2. The slide moves to the new position.
Duplicating a Slide:
1. Right-click on a slide thumbnail.
2. Choose "Duplicate Slide."
3. An exact copy of the slide is created."
Topic 4: Themes and Design (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Themes are pre-designed colour schemes, fonts and effects that give your presentation a professional,
consistent appearance. Instead of choosing colours and fonts manually, you can apply a theme and PowerPoint does the work
for you.
Applying a Theme:
1. Go to the Design tab.
2. The Themes gallery appears. Hover over a theme to see a preview.
3. Click on a theme to apply it to the entire presentation.
4. The theme changes the colours, fonts and background of all slides.
Theme Variants: Each theme has variants — different colour schemes based on the same design. To see the variants:
1. Go to the Design tab.
2. Click the Variants dropdown.
3. Choose a colour variant.
Customising a Theme: You can customise a theme by changing the colours, fonts and effects:
1. Go to the Design tab.
2. Click the Variants dropdown.
3. Click "Colours" to choose a colour scheme.
4. Click "Fonts" to choose a font scheme.
5. Click "Effects" to choose an effect scheme.
Best Practice: Choose a theme that matches the tone of your presentation. For business presentations, use clean, professional
themes. For creative presentations, use more colourful themes. Avoid themes that are too busy or distracting."

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "Emmanuel's Business Pitch"
Situation: Emmanuel runs a small tech startup in Port Harcourt. He has developed a mobile app that helps small businesses
manage their inventory. He has been invited to pitch his app to a group of investors. He needs to create a presentation.
The Problem: Emmanuel has never used PowerPoint. He creates a presentation with too many slides, too much text and no
design. The investors are bored. They do not invest.
Why It Matters: A good presentation can win investors, customers and partners. A bad presentation can lose them. In the
professional world, your presentation is often your only chance to make a good impression.
Relevant Concept: PowerPoint fundamentals. If Emmanuel knew how to create clean, professional slides with minimal text and
good design, he would have a better chance of winning the investors.
Worked Solution: If Emmanuel had taken this course, here is what he would do:
1. Create a title slide with the app name and his name.
2. Create a slide about the problem the app solves.
3. Create a slide about how the app works.
4. Create a slide about the market opportunity.
5. Create a slide about the business model.
6. Create a slide with a call to action.
7. Apply a clean, professional theme.
8. Use minimal text — just key points.
9. Use images and screenshots to illustrate the app.
10. Deliver the presentation with confidence.
Reasoning: A good presentation tells a story. It has a beginning (the problem), a middle (the solution) and an end (the call to
action). It uses visuals to support the message, not replace it. It is delivered with confidence and enthusiasm.
Possible Mistake: Emmanuel might put too much text on each slide. This makes the slides hard to read and the audience stops
listening.
Correction: Use the 6x6 rule: no more than 6 words per line and no more than 6 lines per slide. If you have more to say, use
speaker notes or additional slides.
Transfer Exercise: "Your friend Adaeze is presenting a school project. She has 20 slides with paragraphs of text on each one.
What advice would you give her?"
Professional Connection: Every professional needs to present information at some point. Job interviews, business pitches,
school presentations, church sermons — everyone. Knowing how to create and deliver a good presentation is a valuable
professional skill.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Putting Too Much Text on Slides
Symptoms: The learner creates slides with paragraphs of text. Likely Cause: The learner does not know the 6x6 rule. How to
Investigate: Look at the slides. How much text is on each one? Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the 6x6 rule: no more than 6
words per line and no more than 6 lines per slide. Use bullet points, not paragraphs. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved:
The learner uses minimal text on slides. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make the 6x6 rule a standard requirement for every
presentation exercise.
Mistake 2: Not Using a Theme
Symptoms: The learner creates a presentation with no theme. The slides look plain and unprofessional. Likely Cause: The
learner does not know about themes. How to Investigate: Go to the Design tab. Is a theme applied? Safe Corrective Steps:
Teach the learner to apply a theme from the Design tab. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner applies a
theme. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make applying a theme a standard step in every presentation exercise.
Mistake 3: Not Saving the Presentation
Symptoms: The learner creates a presentation but does not save. The computer crashes and the work is lost. Likely Cause: The
learner forgets to save. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Did you save your presentation?" Safe Corrective Steps: Teach
the learner to press Ctrl+S regularly. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner saves regularly. How to Prevent
Recurrence: Set a timer that goes off every 5 minutes. When it rings, everyone presses Ctrl+S.
Mistake 4: Using Too Many Different Layouts
Symptoms: The learner uses a different layout for every slide. The presentation looks inconsistent. Likely Cause: The learner
does not understand the purpose of layouts. How to Investigate: Look at the slides. Are the layouts consistent? Safe
Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to use the same layout for similar slides (e.g., "Title and Content" for all content slides).
How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner uses consistent layouts. How to Prevent Recurrence: Remind the
learner to use the same layout for similar slides.
Mistake 5: Not Rearranging Slides
Symptoms: The learner creates slides in the wrong order and does not rearrange them. Likely Cause: The learner does not know
how to rearrange slides. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Can you rearrange the slides?" Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the
learner to click and drag slide thumbnails in the Slides Pane. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner rearranges
slides. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make rearranging slides a standard part of every presentation exercise.

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
Questions
1. Multiple Choice: What is Microsoft PowerPoint? a) A word processing program b) A spreadsheet program c) A presentation
program d) A web browser
Answer: c) A presentation program
2. Short Answer: What is a slide layout?
Answer: A slide layout is a pre-designed arrangement of text boxes, images and other elements. Common layouts include Title
Slide, Title and Content, Two Content and Blank.
3. Multiple Choice: What is the 6x6 rule? a) No more than 6 slides and 6 themes b) No more than 6 words per line and 6 lines per
slide c) No more than 6 images and 6 charts d) No more than 6 colours and 6 fonts
Answer: b) No more than 6 words per line and 6 lines per slide
4. Explain in Your Own Words: Why is it important to apply a theme to a presentation?
Answer: A theme gives a presentation a consistent, professional appearance. It ensures that all slides have the same colours,
fonts and design. Without a theme, a presentation looks plain and unprofessional.
5. Practical Identification: Open PowerPoint. Identify the Ribbon, the Slides Pane, the Slide Area, the Notes Pane, and the
Design tab.
Answer: The learner correctly identifies all five elements.
6. Scenario-Based: Your friend says, "I created a presentation but it looks plain. What can I do to make it look better?" What
advice would you give?
Answer: Apply a theme from the Design tab. A theme will give your presentation a consistent colour scheme, font and design.
You can also change the colour variant to customise the look.
7. Error-Detecting: A learner writes in their assignment: "I put paragraphs of text on each slide because I wanted to include all
the information." What is wrong with this, and what should they have done instead?
Answer: Putting paragraphs of text on slides makes them hard to read and the audience stops listening. The learner should have
used the 6x6 rule: no more than 6 words per line and 6 lines per slide. Additional information can go in speaker notes or
additional slides.

Common Misconceptions
"PowerPoint is just for school projects." Some learners think PowerPoint is only for students. Correct this by showing
them how PowerPoint is used in business, churches and conferences.
"More text is better." Some learners think that putting lots of text on slides makes the presentation more informative.
Correct this by teaching the 6x6 rule.
"Themes are only for designers." Some learners think themes are an advanced feature. Correct this by showing them how
simple it is to apply a theme.
Suggested Remedial Exercises
For learners who put too much text on slides: Have them rewrite a text-heavy slide using the 6x6 rule.
For learners who do not apply themes: Have them apply a theme to an existing presentation and see the difference.
For learners who cannot add slides: Walk them through the process step by step. Let them practise multiple times.
Differentiation Guidance
Level 1 — Supported (for learners who struggle):
Provide a labelled PowerPoint interface diagram for reference.
Pair with a more confident learner for practical exercises.
Allow extra time for the independent exercise.
Focus on creating slides and applying themes before moving to advanced features.
Level 2 — Independent (standard):
Complete all exercises as written.
Answer all knowledge check questions.
