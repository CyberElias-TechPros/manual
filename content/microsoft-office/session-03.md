# Professional Documents

## A. Session Identification
<!-- kind:identification -->
Field Detail

Week 1
Field Detail

## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Set page size and orientation (portrait vs landscape) appropriately.
2. Adjust margins to control the amount of white space around the text.
3. Insert and edit headers and footers with page numbers, dates and document titles.
4. Insert and format tables for organised information.
5. Insert page breaks to control where new pages begin.
6. Print a document with the correct settings.
7. Export a document to PDF for professional sharing.
8. Explain why page layout matters in professional documents.

## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
"The Unprofessional Report"
Instructor Setup: Display two versions of the same report on the projector: one with no page layout (default margins, no
headers, no page numbers) and one with professional page layout.
Instructor Script: "Good morning, everyone. Look at these two reports. They contain the exact same text and formatting. But
one looks like a draft, and the other looks like a finished, professional document. What is the difference? [Pause for answers —
margins, headers, footers, page numbers.]
The difference is page layout. Page layout is how you set up the page itself — the margins, the orientation, the headers, the
footers and the page numbers. Good page layout makes a document look polished and professional. Bad page layout makes it
look unfinished.
Today, you will learn how to set up pages like a professional. You will learn about margins, orientation, headers, footers, page
numbers, tables and printing. These are the final skills you need to create complete, professional documents in Word. Let us
begin."
Expected Learner Response: Learners will identify visible differences: margins, headers, footers, page numbers. This sets the
context for the session.

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: Page Size and Orientation (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Page size and orientation determine the shape and size of your document.
Page Size: The default page size in Word is A4 (210 x 297 mm), which is the standard size in Nigeria and most of the world. In
the United States, the standard is Letter (8.5 x 11 inches). For most documents, A4 is the correct choice.
To change the page size:
1. Go to the Layout tab on the Ribbon.
2. Click the Size button.
3. Choose A4, Letter, or another size.
Orientation: Orientation determines whether the page is taller than it is wide (portrait) or wider than it is tall (landscape).
Portrait: The default. Used for most documents — letters, reports, CVs, essays.
Landscape: Used for wide documents — tables, charts, certificates, flyers.
To change the orientation:
1. Go to the Layout tab.
2. Click the Orientation button.
3. Choose Portrait or Landscape.
When to Use Each:
Use portrait for text-heavy documents.
Use landscape for documents with wide tables or images.
You can even mix orientations in the same document by using section breaks (advanced topic)."
Topic 2: Margins (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Margins are the white space between the text and the edge of the page. They make a document look clean
and professional. They also make it easier to read — text that goes all the way to the edge of the page is hard to read.
Default Margins: The default margins in Word are 1 inch (2.54 cm) on all sides. This is a good standard for most documents.
Changing Margins:
1. Go to the Layout tab.
2. Click the Margins button.
3. Choose a preset (Normal, Narrow, Moderate, Wide) or click "Custom Margins" to set your own.
Common Margin Settings:
Normal: 1 inch on all sides. Good for most documents.
Narrow: 0.5 inch on all sides. Good for documents with lots of content.
Moderate: 1 inch top and bottom, 0.75 inch left and right. Good for reports.
Wide: 1 inch top and bottom, 2 inch left and right. Good for documents with comments.
Best Practice: Use the default Normal margins unless you have a specific reason to change them. Consistent margins make a
document look professional."
Topic 3: Headers and Footers (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Headers and footers are areas at the top and bottom of every page. They are used for information that
should appear on every page of a document — the document title, the author's name, the date, the page number, the company
logo.
Headers: The header is the area at the top of the page. It is used for:
Document title
Company name or logo
Chapter or section name
Author's name
Footers: The footer is the area at the bottom of the page. It is used for:
Page numbers
Date
Company contact information
Confidentiality notices
Inserting a Header or Footer:
1. Go to the Insert tab on the Ribbon.
2. Click the Header button (for headers) or the Footer button (for footers).
3. Choose a style from the gallery (e.g., Blank, Austin, Banded).
4. The header or footer area opens. Type your text.
5. Click "Close Header and Footer" or double-click in the document area to close.
Editing a Header or Footer:
1. Double-click in the header or footer area.
2. Make your changes.
3. Click "Close Header and Footer" or double-click in the document area.
Page Numbers: Page numbers are usually placed in the footer. To insert page numbers:
1. Go to the Insert tab.
2. Click the Page Number button.
3. Choose a position (Top of Page, Bottom of Page, Page Margins, Current Position).
4. Choose a style.
5. The page number appears on every page.
Different First Page: If you do not want a header or footer on the first page (e.g., a title page), you can set the first page to be
different:
1. Double-click in the header or footer area.
2. Go to the Header & Footer Tools Design tab.
3. Check the "Different First Page" box.
4. The header and footer will not appear on the first page."
Topic 4: Tables (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Tables are used to organise information into rows and columns. They are useful for:
CVs (education, experience)
Invoices (item, quantity, price)
Schedules (date, time, event)
Comparisons (feature, option A, option B)
Inserting a Table:
1. Go to the Insert tab.
2. Click the Table button.
3. A grid appears. Drag your mouse to select the number of rows and columns you need.
4. Click to insert the table.
Entering Data in a Table:
1. Click in a cell.
2. Type your text.
3. Press Tab to move to the next cell.
4. Press Enter to create a new line within a cell.
Formatting a Table:
1. Click anywhere in the table.
2. The Table Tools tabs appear (Design and Layout).
3. Use the Design tab to apply a table style (e.g., Grid Table, Light Shading).
4. Use the Layout tab to add or delete rows and columns, merge cells, and adjust cell size.
Table Tips:
Use a table style to make your table look professional.
Keep your table simple — do not use too many colours or borders.
Make sure your table fits within the margins of the page."
Topic 5: Page Breaks and Printing (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "A page break is where one page ends and the next one begins. Word creates page breaks automatically
when a page is full. But sometimes you want to control where a page break occurs.
Inserting a Manual Page Break:
1. Click where you want the new page to begin.
2. Go to the Layout tab.
3. Click the Breaks button.
4. Choose "Page Break."
5. A new page begins at the cursor position.
When to Use Manual Page Breaks:
To start a new chapter on a new page.
To prevent a heading from being separated from the text that follows it.
To create a title page that is separate from the rest of the document.
Printing a Document:
1. Click "File."
2. Click "Print."
3. Choose your printer.
4. Choose the number of copies.
5. Choose which pages to print (All, Current Page, or a range).
6. Click "Print."
Print Preview: Before printing, always check the Print Preview. This shows you exactly how the document will look on paper. To
see the Print Preview, click "File" and then "Print." The preview appears on the right side of the screen."

2. "I click 'Print.' The document prints."
3. "Now I will export to PDF. I click 'File.' I click 'Export.' I click 'Create PDF/XPS Document.' I choose where to save. I type a file
name. I click 'Publish.'"
4. "I now have a PDF version of the document. I can send this to anyone and they will see it exactly as I designed it."
What Learners Should Observe: The instructor using Print Preview, printing and exporting to PDF.
Expected Result: Learners can describe the steps to print and export to PDF.

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "The Unfinished Report"
Situation: Chinedu is a student at a university in Port Harcourt. He writes a 10-page report for his final year project. He spends
weeks researching and writing the content. But when he prints the report, it looks terrible. The margins are too narrow, there are
no page numbers, the header is missing, and the table of contents is not formatted. His supervisor is not impressed.
The Problem: Chinedu spent weeks on the content but no time on the presentation. The report looks unfinished because it
lacks professional page layout.
Why It Matters: In academic and professional settings, presentation matters. A well-written report with poor presentation
suggests the author is careless. A well-written report with good presentation suggests the author is thorough and professional.
Relevant Concept: Page layout. If Chinedu knew how to set margins, insert headers and footers, add page numbers and format
tables, his report would look polished and professional.
Worked Solution: If Chinedu had taken this course, here is what he would do:
1. Set margins to Normal (1 inch on all sides).
2. Insert a header with the report title and his name.
3. Insert a footer with page numbers.
4. Format the table of contents with proper styles.
5. Insert page breaks before each major section.
6. Print the report and check the Print Preview.
Reasoning: Good page layout is like good packaging. The content is the product, but the packaging is what people see first. A
well-packaged product looks more valuable.
Possible Mistake: Chinedu might think that page layout is not important — that the content is all that matters. This is a
mistake. In the real world, people judge by appearances. A poorly presented report may not be read at all, no matter how good
the content is.
Correction: Always spend time on page layout. It does not take long — maybe 15 minutes for a 10-page report. But it makes a
huge difference in how the report is received.
Transfer Exercise: "Your friend Adaeze has a 5-page essay with no formatting. What are three things you would tell her to do to
make it look more professional?"
Professional Connection: Every report, proposal, CV and business letter needs good page layout. This is not optional — it is
expected. The skills you learn in this session will serve you every time you create a multi-page document.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Forgetting to Check Print Preview
Symptoms: The learner prints a document without checking the Print Preview. The printed document looks wrong. Likely
Cause: The learner does not know about Print Preview or does not think to use it. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Did you
check the Print Preview before printing?" Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to always check the Print Preview before
printing. Show them how to access it (File > Print). How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner checks Print Preview
before printing. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make Print Preview a standard step in every printing exercise.
Mistake 2: Not Using Headers and Footers
Symptoms: The learner creates a multi-page document with no header, footer or page numbers. Likely Cause: The learner does
not know how to insert headers and footers. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Does your document have a header or
footer?" Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to insert headers and footers from the Insert tab. How to Confirm the
Problem Is Resolved: The learner inserts headers and footers. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make headers and footers a
standard requirement for multi-page documents.

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
Questions
1. Multiple Choice: What is the default page size in Microsoft Word? a) Letter b) A4 c) Legal d) A3
Answer: b) A4
2. Short Answer: What are headers and footers used for?
Answer: Headers are used for information at the top of every page (e.g., document title, author's name). Footers are used for
information at the bottom of every page (e.g., page numbers, date).
3. Multiple Choice: How do you insert a table in Word? a) Go to the Home tab and click Table b) Go to the Insert tab and click
Table c) Go to the Layout tab and click Table d) Go to the Design tab and click Table
Answer: b) Go to the Insert tab and click Table
4. Explain in Your Own Words: Why is it important to check the Print Preview before printing?
Answer: Print Preview shows you exactly how the document will look on paper. It helps you catch formatting errors, check
margins, and ensure that headers, footers and page numbers are correct before you waste paper and ink.
5. Practical Identification: Open a Word document. Identify the Layout tab, the Insert tab, and the Header & Footer Tools
Design tab.
Answer: The learner correctly identifies all three tabs.
6. Scenario-Based: Your friend says, "I printed my report but the margins are too narrow and there are no page numbers. What
did I do wrong?" What advice would you give?
Answer: You should have set the margins to Normal (1 inch on all sides) before printing. You should also have inserted page
numbers in the footer. Always check the Print Preview before printing to catch these issues.
7. Error-Detecting: A learner writes in their assignment: "I inserted a page break before every paragraph." What is wrong with
this, and what should they have done instead?
Answer: Word creates page breaks automatically when a page is full. Manual page breaks should only be used when you want
to start a new section on a new page. Inserting a page break before every paragraph creates unnecessary blank space and makes
the document hard to edit.
