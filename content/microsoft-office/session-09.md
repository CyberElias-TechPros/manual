# Final Project Capstone

## A. Session Identification
<!-- kind:identification -->
Field Detail

## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Plan and organise a multi-part project from a brief.
2. Create a formatted CV or business letter in Microsoft Word, exported to PDF.
3. Create a working spreadsheet in Microsoft Excel with live formulas and at least one chart.
4. Create a 5–7 slide presentation in Microsoft PowerPoint.
5. Present the presentation aloud to the class with confidence.
6. Explain their design and formatting decisions for all three deliverables.
7. Reflect on their learning and identify areas for continued growth.

## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
"You Are Ready"
Instructor Script: "Good morning, everyone. This is it — the final session. Over the past three weeks, you have learned
Microsoft Word, Excel and PowerPoint. Today, you are going to prove that you can use all three to create a professional
document package.
I am not going to help you. I am not going to give you step-by-step instructions. I am going to give you a project brief, and you
are going to complete it on your own. This is not a test of perfection. It is a test of competence. Can you sit down at a computer
and produce professional work?
If you have attended the previous sessions and practised, you are ready. If you are nervous, that is normal. Take a deep breath.
You know how to do this. Let us begin."
Expected Learner Response: A mix of excitement and nervousness. Some learners will be confident. Others will be anxious. The
instructor's calm, encouraging tone sets the atmosphere.

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Instructor Script: "Before we start, let us do a quick review of everything you have learned. I will ask questions and you answer
out loud."
Review Questions and Expected Answers:
1. "How do you change the font in Word?"
Select the text, go to the Home tab, and choose a font from the Font group.
2. "What does the SUM function do in Excel?"
It adds a range of cells.
3. "What is the 6x6 rule in PowerPoint?"
No more than 6 words per line and 6 lines per slide.
4. "How do you apply a theme in PowerPoint?"
Go to the Design tab and click on a theme.
5. "How do you export a Word document to PDF?"
Click File > Export > Create PDF/XPS Document.
6. "What is the most important rule of presentation delivery?"
Do not read from the slides. Speak to the audience.
How to Address Common Gaps:
If a learner cannot answer a question, do not embarrass them. Ask another learner to help. The goal is to activate prior
knowledge, not to test.

Any questions?"

have been taught. If you have been paying attention and practising, you will pass.

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "Emmanuel's Business Package"
Situation: Emmanuel completed the Microsoft Office course at CEA. He passed the capstone assessment. Two weeks later, he
starts a new job as an office assistant at a company in Port Harcourt. On his first day, his boss says: "Create a folder on your
desktop called 'Q4 Report.' Create a formatted report, a spreadsheet with our sales data and a presentation summarising the key
points. Have it ready by tomorrow."
Emmanuel sits down at the computer. He takes a deep breath. He has done this before. He creates the folder, opens Word, types
and formats the report, opens Excel, enters the data and creates formulas and a chart, opens PowerPoint, creates a clean
presentation, and saves everything. Total time: 2 hours.
His boss is impressed. "You are the fastest new starter we have had," she says.
The Problem: Emmanuel's colleague, Chinedu, also started on the same day. Chinedu did not take the CEA course. When his
boss gives him the same task, he stares at the screen. He does not know how to format a document, create a formula or design
a presentation. He has to ask for help. His boss is not impressed.
Why It Matters: Emmanuel and Chinedu have the same job. But Emmanuel can work independently. Chinedu cannot. In a
workplace, independence is valuable. Employers do not have time to hold your hand. They expect you to be able to sit down
and get to work.
Relevant Concept: All the skills taught in Sessions 1–8. Emmanuel's success is the result of three weeks of focused, practical
learning.
Worked Solution: Emmanuel succeeded because he had practised every step of the workflow multiple times. He had created
documents, spreadsheets and presentations. By the time he did it in a real workplace, it was second nature.
Reasoning: The capstone assessment is not just a test. It is a rehearsal for real life. When you can create a complete document
package independently in class, you can create it independently in a workplace.
Possible Mistake: Emmanuel might have been nervous on his first day. Nervousness can make you forget things. But because
he had practised so much, his hands remembered what to do even when his brain was stressed.
Correction: Practice until the skills become automatic. Do not just practise until you can do it. Practise until you cannot fail.
Transfer Exercise: "Think about a task you might be asked to do in a real job. Write down the steps you would take. Then
practise those steps until they become automatic."
Professional Connection: Every office job requires the skills taught in this course. Administrators, sales officers, teachers,
accountants, managers — everyone. The ability to create professional documents, working spreadsheets and polished
presentations is the foundation of all professional office work.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Choosing a Business That Is Too Complex
Symptoms: The learner chooses a business that is too complex (e.g., a multinational corporation) and cannot complete the
project in time. Likely Cause: The learner does not understand the scope of the project. How to Investigate: Ask the learner,
"What business did you choose? How complex is it?" Safe Corrective Steps: Encourage the learner to choose a simple business
(e.g., a small shop, a salon, a restaurant). The goal is to demonstrate skills, not to create a realistic business plan. How to
Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner chooses a simple business. How to Prevent Recurrence: Provide a list of
simple business examples in the project brief.
Mistake 2: Not Managing Time
Symptoms: The learner spends too much time on one document and does not have time for the others. Likely Cause: The
learner does not plan their time. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "How much time have you spent on each document?"
Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to allocate time for each document (e.g., 15 minutes for Word, 20 minutes for Excel,
20 minutes for PowerPoint, 5 minutes for saving). How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner manages their time
effectively. How to Prevent Recurrence: Provide a time allocation guide in the project brief.
Mistake 3: Not Using Formulas in Excel
Symptoms: The learner types calculated values manually instead of using formulas. Likely Cause: The learner forgets to use
formulas or does not know how. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Did you use formulas in your spreadsheet?" Safe
Corrective Steps: Remind the learner to use formulas for calculations. Show them how to use SUM, AVERAGE and other
functions. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner uses formulas. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make formulas
a standard requirement for every spreadsheet exercise.
Mistake 4: Not Following the 6x6 Rule in PowerPoint
Symptoms: The learner puts too much text on slides. Likely Cause: The learner forgets the 6x6 rule. How to Investigate: Look
at the slides. How much text is on each one? Safe Corrective Steps: Remind the learner of the 6x6 rule. Encourage them to use
bullet points and speaker notes. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner follows the 6x6 rule. How to Prevent
Recurrence: Make the 6x6 rule a standard requirement for every presentation exercise.
Mistake 5: Not Saving Documents
Symptoms: The learner creates documents but does not save them. Likely Cause: The learner forgets to save or runs out of
time. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Did you save your documents?" Safe Corrective Steps: Remind the learner to save
early and save often. Provide a "Save All Documents" step at the end of the project. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved:
The learner saves all documents. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make saving a standard step in every project.

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (15 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
Quiz
Instructions: Answer all 20 questions.
Section A: Word (5 questions)
1. How do you change the font of selected text in Word?
2. What does the Format Painter do?
3. How do you insert a table in Word?
4. What is the correct way to shut down a computer? (Trick question — this is from Session 1!)
5. How do you export a Word document to PDF?
Section B: Excel (5 questions)
6. What must every Excel formula start with?
7. What does the SUM function do?
8. What is the difference between a workbook and a worksheet?
9. How do you format numbers as currency in Excel?
10. What type of chart is best for comparing values across categories?
Section C: PowerPoint (5 questions)
11. What is the 6x6 rule?
12. How do you apply a theme in PowerPoint?
13. What is the difference between a transition and an animation?
14. What does Presenter View show?
15. Why should you not read from the slides during a presentation?
Section D: Integration (5 questions)
16. What are the three applications in Microsoft Office that you learned in this course?
17. Which application would you use to create a budget? Why?
18. Which application would you use to create a CV? Why?
19. Which application would you use to create a presentation? Why?
20. What is the most important skill you learned in this course? Why?
Answer Key
Section A: Word
1. Select the text, go to the Home tab, and choose a font from the Font group.
2. The Format Painter copies formatting from one section of text to another.
3. Go to the Insert tab and click the Table button.
4. Click Start, click Power, click Shut down. (Trick question — always check your work!)
5. Click File > Export > Create PDF/XPS Document.
Section B: Excel
6. An equals sign (=).
7. It adds a range of cells.
8. A workbook is a collection of worksheets. A worksheet is a single page.
9. Select the numbers, go to the Home tab, click the Number Format dropdown, and choose Currency.
10. A bar chart.
Section C: PowerPoint
11. No more than 6 words per line and 6 lines per slide.
12. Go to the Design tab and click on a theme.
13. A transition is the effect when moving from one slide to the next. An animation is the effect when an object appears on a
slide.
14. Presenter View shows the current slide, the next slide, speaker notes and a timer.
15. Reading from the slides makes the presentation boring. The slides are for the audience. Your words are the presentation.
Section D: Integration
16. Microsoft Word, Microsoft Excel and Microsoft PowerPoint.
17. Excel — because it is designed for calculations and working with numbers.
18. Word — because it is designed for creating text documents.
19. PowerPoint — because it is designed for creating presentations.
20. (Open-ended — any reasonable answer is acceptable.)

## O. Instructor Evaluation Notes
<!-- kind:instructor_notes -->
Observable Signs of Competence
The learner creates a formatted document in Word and exports to PDF.
The learner creates a working spreadsheet with formulas and a chart in Excel.
The learner creates a 5–7 slide presentation in PowerPoint with a theme, image, transition and animations.
The learner presents the presentation with confidence and eye contact.
The learner explains their design and formatting decisions.
The learner works independently without step-by-step guidance.
Common Misconceptions
"I can just learn this on my own." Some learners think they do not need a course. They may be right — some people can
figure things out on their own. But most people benefit from structured, guided learning.
"I will never need to use Office." Some learners think they will avoid Office in their daily life. In 2026, this is almost
impossible. Office skills are required in almost every job.
"I am too old to learn this." Some older learners feel they cannot learn new software. This is not true. Age is not a barrier to
learning computer skills.
Suggested Remedial Exercises
For learners who do not pass the capstone: Allow them to practise on academy machines during opening hours. Provide a
step-by-step guide they can follow. Let them retake the assessment when they are ready.
