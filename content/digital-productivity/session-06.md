# Final Project — My Productivity System (Capstone)

## A. Session Identification
<!-- kind:identification -->
**Week / module:** Week 2 — Module 3: Task Management and Workflow

**Recommended duration:** 105 minutes

**Difficulty:** Beginner — but this session tests every skill from Sessions 1–5

**Prerequisite knowledge:** Sessions 1–5 completed. The learner should already have an organised Drive, a managed Gmail, working Docs and Sheets, a Google Tasks system and a draft workflow document. This session is build, polish, present and viva.

**Connection to previous work:** Session 1 gave you organised storage; Session 2 gave you a managed inbox; Session 3 gave you documents and spreadsheets; Session 4 gave you a task system; Session 5 gave you a documented workflow. Today those five pieces become one complete, demonstrable productivity system — the certificate requirement.

**Preparation before class:** Academy PCs on and logged into Gmail; projector for learner demonstrations; printed capstone briefs (one per learner); printed rubric sheets for the instructor; printed viva question bank (instructor copy); printed certificate sign-off sheets; a completed model system on the instructor's demo account to show the standard.

## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Assemble a complete productivity system in one Google account, integrating Drive, Gmail, Docs, Sheets and Tasks.
2. Demonstrate an organised Google Drive with a logical folder structure, at least fifteen correctly named files, and at least
one folder shared with the correct permission.
3. Demonstrate a managed Gmail with at least five labels, at least two filters, a professional signature, at least one saved
email template, and an inbox below ten conversations.
4. Demonstrate Docs and Sheets competence — a formatted one-page business profile document and a spreadsheet with
working formulas and naira formatting.
5. Demonstrate a task management system with three or more lists, at least ten dated tasks, three starred MITs and at least
one task with subtasks.
6. Present a five-minute walkthrough of the complete system that clearly explains the decisions made.
7. Answer viva questions about the system accurately and honestly.
8. Self-assess against the capstone rubric and reflect on growth since Session 1.
9. Explain honestly what the CEA Certificate of Digital Productivity means — and what it does not mean.

D. OPENING ACTIVITY (6 minutes) — "The Interview"
Instructor setup: On the projector, display a simple job advertisement:
WANTED: Office Assistant, Port Harcourt. Must be organised, reliable with files, email and deadlines. Send CV to HR.
What the instructor says:
"Imagine two people apply. The first says, in the interview, 'I am organised.' The second opens a laptop and shows a tidy
Drive, a Gmail inbox at zero with labels and filters, a task list showing exactly what she is working on this week, and a one-
page document explaining how she runs her day. If you were the employer, who would you believe?
Today you become the second person — not by talking about being organised, but by showing a real system. This is your
final project. In the next hour you will complete, polish and present the whole thing, and then you will receive your
certificate if you meet the standard."
The hook: Open the instructor's model system and take the class on a 60-second tour — Drive first, then Gmail, then Tasks,
then the workflow doc. Say: "This is the finish line. By the end of today, this is you."
Expected learner response: Learners may feel nervous ("I have not finished everything"). Reassure them: today is exactly the
time to finish, and the instructor will move around the room helping. Emphasise that presentation quality is not about fancy
words — it is about honest, clear explanation.
Why this opener works: It frames the capstone as a real-world outcome (employability and trust) rather than a school exercise,
and it lowers anxiety by showing a concrete, achievable destination.

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (7 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Run this fast as a whole-class review with the questions on the projector. This doubles as a warm-up before the build.
Question 1: "What are the three sharing permissions in Google Drive, and which one should you use when a person only needs
to read?"
Expected answer: Viewer, Commenter, Editor. Use Viewer for reading only. (Least privilege.)
Question 2: "Give one example of a Gmail filter you built, and say what it does."
Expected answer: any valid filter, for example From: bank → label Finance/Bills → Skip the Inbox .
Question 3: "What is your task-title rule, and what is a MIT?"
Expected answer: verb + object + due date + context. A MIT is one of your three Most Important Tasks for the day.
Question 4: "What is the capital of Nigeria? " (no — wrong course!)
Expected answer: learners should laugh. The instructor says: "Just checking you are awake. Notice how easy it is to give a
confident answer to the wrong question — in your viva, answer the question that is asked, and if you do not know, say so
honestly." This is a deliberate teaching moment about viva conduct.
Question 5: "In one line, what is your daily workflow?"
Expected answer: any sensible morning / work block / shutdown routine using the course tools.
Bridge to today (say this):
"You have already learned every skill. Today is not new learning — it is assembly, polish and proof. If something is
missing, now is the moment to fix it. Then you present, you answer questions, and you finish."
If a majority of learners cannot answer Questions 1–5, spend 10 extra minutes reviewing the key concepts before the build
begins.

## F. Full Lecture Notes and Capstone Brief
<!-- kind:lecture -->
F.1 The capstone brief (restated)
Scenario. You are the digital administrator for Adaeze Fashion Design, a small Port Harcourt tailoring and fashion business. The
owner, Adaeze, is overwhelmed: files are everywhere, email is chaotic, deadlines are missed, and nobody has written down how
the work is done. She has hired you to set up her complete digital productivity system. At the end of the project, you will
present the system and answer her questions.
Your deliverables (the five pillars):
1. An organised Google Drive
A main folder named CEA-Capstone-YourName .
At least four subfolders with a logical structure (for example 01-Admin , 02-Designs , 03-Customers , 04-Marketing ).
At least fifteen files, each correctly named with the convention YYYY-MM-DD - Topic - Name (or another consistent
convention), and each in the correct folder.
At least one folder shared with the instructor as Viewer.
2. A managed Gmail
At least five labels, sensibly named and (where useful) nested.
At least two filters that automatically label, archive or star incoming mail.
A professional signature with your name, role and a contact line.
At least one saved email template (for example "Client Follow-up" or "Order Confirmed").
An inbox below ten conversations (ideally at or near zero).
3. Docs and Sheets competence
A one-page business profile in Google Docs using heading styles, at least one table and one image.
A Google Sheet with at least ten rows of sales or stock data, using =SUM , =AVERAGE and =COUNT , formatted with the
naira sign (₦), and with a filter applied.
4. A task management system
At least three lists (for example Shop , School , Personal ) plus Today .
At least ten tasks with dates; three starred MITs; at least one task with subtasks.
5. A documented daily workflow
A one-page Google Doc describing your morning / work block / shutdown routine, and at least two automations.
Presentation and viva. Each learner gives a five-minute walkthrough of their system to the class and answers questions from
the instructor (the viva).

F.3 The recommended build order
When time is short, build in this order (the same order the certificate looks for):
1. Drive first — everything else is stored there.
2. Gmail second — filters and signature matter most for daily work.
3. Docs and Sheets third — the actual work products.
4. Tasks fourth — the plan for using it all.
5. Workflow document last — it describes the system you just built.
Why this order? Each pillar depends on the one before it. A workflow doc makes no sense until the system it documents exists.
F.4 How to present your system (the five-minute structure)
A confident presentation is simply a clear story in a fixed order. Use this script:
1. Greeting and purpose (20 seconds): "Good morning. I am [name], and this is the productivity system I built for Adaeze
Fashion Design."
2. Drive (45 seconds): open the folder, name your structure, explain why you chose it, show one file name, and show the
shared folder.
3. Gmail (60 seconds): show labels, open a filter and explain what it does, show the signature and one template, and point at
the inbox count.
4. Docs and Sheets (60 seconds): show the business profile, scroll to the table; open the sheet and click one formula cell to
show =SUM .
5. Tasks (60 seconds): show your lists, three MITs, one subtask, and explain your daily review.
6. Workflow (45 seconds): open the workflow doc and summarise your routine and one automation.
7. Close (20 seconds): "That is my system. What I am most proud of is [one thing]."
Presentation tips: speak slowly; point at the screen; do not read word-for-word; if something breaks, stay calm and move on; it
F.5 How to answer viva questions
The viva is a short conversation, not an exam trap. Three rules:
1. Answer the question you are asked. Do not wander.
2. If you do not know, say so honestly and add what you do know. Honesty scores better than a confident wrong answer.
3. Use the course vocabulary — labels, filters, least privilege, MIT, as-is/to-be, template. It shows you understand.
The full viva bank with model answers is in Section M.
F.6 The capstone rubric explained
Your capstone is marked out of 35 in total:
30 marks for the quality of the system itself, across eight criteria (Drive, Gmail, templates, Docs/Sheets, Tasks, workflow,
consistency, presentation delivery).
5 marks for the viva / live demonstration, where the instructor asks you to show and explain parts of your system live.
The full rubric table with performance descriptions is in Section L. Read it before you present so you know exactly what
"excellent" looks like.
F.7 An honest word about what this certificate means
This course makes you a competent digital-productivity beginner. That is a real achievement — most office workers never
receive this training. But be honest with yourself and with employers:
You can set up and run a productivity system with Google's free tools.
You cannot yet call yourself a productivity consultant or coach.
You have not covered advanced automation, programming or enterprise tools.
A certificate does not guarantee a job — it proves a skill you must now keep using.
Say this to the class: "A certificate is a key, not a house. It opens a door. What you build behind that door — by practising
every week — is up to you."

J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY — "Emmanuel's First Office Job"
Situation. Emmanuel and Uche both finished a short computer course and got hired at the same Port Harcourt company on the
same day. On their first morning, Uche asks a colleague where the client contracts are kept; nobody is sure, and it takes an hour
to find the latest version. Emmanuel opens his laptop, goes to an organised shared folder, and finds the right contract in thirty
seconds. He also has a labelled inbox, a task list of what HR asked for, and a short note describing how he will run his week.
Relevant facts.
Both men have similar qualifications and intelligence.
Only one of them was trained in digital productivity.
Files and email chaos cost the company money and trust every day.
Decision point. Within the first week, which new employee will the manager trust with important client work?
Possible approaches.
1. Wait to be told how the office works and hope for the best.
2. Take five minutes to ask where things live, then build a personal system (folders, labels, tasks) aligned to the company's
setup.
3. Ignore organisation and rely on memory like everyone else.
Recommended solution. Approach 2. Emmanuel builds a small system around the company's tools. It costs him a few minutes
a day and quickly makes him the person who "never loses anything" — which is exactly who gets promoted.
Trade-offs.
Approach 1 and 3 are effortless now but create repeated failure later.
Approach 2 takes a small, consistent effort and pays compounding returns in trust and opportunity.
Professional connection. This is the true value of the CEA Digital Productivity certificate: not the paper, but the habit of
building systems that make you reliable. Reliability, more than talent, is what earns responsibility at work.

## K. Common Failure Points and Remediation
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
These are the failures observed most often at capstone time. For each: the likely cause, the fix, and how to prevent it next time.
K.1 The shared folder is not actually shared
Symptom: The instructor cannot open the Drive folder. Likely cause: The learner forgot to share, shared the wrong folder, or
shared as a link that is restricted. Fix: Right-click the folder → Share → add the instructor's email → set Viewer → Send.
Prevent: Tick "share Viewer with the instructor" as a numbered step in the checklist.
K.2 Formulas are not real formulas
Symptom: The totals look right but there is no formula in the cell. Likely cause: The learner typed the answer instead of using
=SUM , or typed numbers as text. Fix: Select the cell, click it and check the formula bar; retype using =SUM(D2:D13) ; if numbers
are text, use Format > Number. Prevent: Always verify by clicking the cell and reading the formula bar.
K.3 The inbox is still over ten
Symptom: Inbox count is high; labels exist but the inbox is cluttered. Likely cause: Filters created but never applied to existing
mail, or no triage habit. Fix: Archive/label/delete/snooze until below ten; tick Also apply filter to matching conversations on
the filters. Prevent: Run the morning triage daily.
K.4 Tasks exist but have no dates or MITs
Symptom: A long undated task list. Likely cause: Tasks captured but never organised (the "capture" half without the "organise"
half). Fix: Add dates; star exactly three MITs; move them into Today . Prevent: Apply the verb + object + date rule and the rule
of three.
K.5 Files are in the wrong folder or badly named
Symptom: Files scattered, names like Document1 or final-final . Likely cause: Naming convention not followed. Fix: Rename
with YYYY-MM-DD - Topic - Name and move to the correct subfolder. Prevent: Name files the moment they are created.
K.6 The learner cannot explain their own system
Symptom: During the viva, the learner goes silent or contradicts the screen. Likely cause: The system was copied without
understanding; no presentation practice. Fix: Rehearse the five-minute structure (Section F.4) once before presenting; answer
honestly where unsure. Prevent: Practise the walkthrough with a partner.
K.7 Panic and running out of time
Symptom: The learner freezes with 20 minutes left. Likely cause: Trying to build everything at once. Fix: Breathe. Follow the
build order: Drive → Gmail → Docs/Sheets → Tasks → Workflow. Prevent: Use the milestone checkpoints from Section F.2
before S6.
Distinguish likely vs confirmed: always confirm (click the Share button, read the formula bar) before fixing.

## M. Knowledge Check and Viva Bank
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
M.1 Written knowledge check (final, 10 questions)
(Learner version — answers in the key below.)
Q1. What are the three sharing permissions, and which gives read-only access? Q2. What is the difference between a Gmail label
and a filter? Q3. Give the task-title formula taught in this course. Q4. What is a MIT? Q5. Write the Sheets formula that totals C2
to C20 . Q6. In one sentence, what is a workflow? Q7. Where do you press to see the shortcut list in Gmail? In Docs? Q8. What
do the As-Is and To-Be maps represent? Q9. True or false: automatically archiving a rough, disorganised process is the correct
first step. Q10. In one sentence, what does the CEA Digital Productivity certificate prove?
INSTRUCTOR ANSWER KEY (Q1–Q10)
A1. Viewer, Commenter, Editor. Viewer is read-only. A2. A label is a tag you apply to messages; a filter is an automatic rule that
applies labels (and other actions) to incoming mail. A3. Verb + object + due date + context. A4. A Most Important Task — one
of the (maximum) three priority tasks chosen for the day. A5. =SUM(C2:C20) . A6. A repeatable sequence of steps for completing
a recurring task. A7. Gmail: ? . Docs: Ctrl + / . A8. The As-Is map shows how a workflow currently works (with its waste); the
To-Be map shows the improved version. A9. False. Organise first, automate second. A10. It proves the holder can set up and
maintain a complete digital productivity system using free cloud tools — a competent beginner, not an expert or consultant.
M.2 Viva question bank (instructor copy)
The viva is a short live conversation. Ask 3–5 questions per learner, adapting to what they show. Model answers are provided
so marking is consistent.
V1. Walk me through your Drive structure and why you chose it. Model: names the folders, explains that they match the
business's main areas, and notes the numbering keeps order and the date-first naming sorts by time.
V2. Show me one filter and explain what it does. Model: opens Settings > Filters (or the filter list), reads the criteria (for
example From bank), and states the actions (label Finance/Bills + Skip Inbox), and why.
V3. What is the difference between a task and a project in your system? Model: a task is one action; a project is many tasks
and appears as its own list; e.g., "Buy fabric" is a task, "October Collection" is a project.
V4. How do you decide what to work on first each day? Model: morning review, check calendar, choose three MITs, star them,
move to Today , work with notifications off.
V5. Show me a formula in your sheet and explain it. Model: clicks a cell, reads the formula bar ( =SUM(D2:D13) ), and explains it
adds the numbers in that range.
V6. Why did you share that folder as Viewer and not Editor? Model: least privilege — the instructor only needs to read; Editor
would let someone change or delete files.
V7. Walk me through your daily workflow. Model: morning triage (calendar, tasks, inbox), a focused work block on one MIT,
and a shutdown that files documents and updates tasks.
V8. What was the hardest part of building this system, and how did you solve it? Model: an honest answer naming a real
difficulty and the steps taken; shows problem-solving, not perfection.
V9. If a new customer order arrives tomorrow, what happens in your system? Model: order captured (form or task), a task
with a date created, customer labelled/labelled mail, invoice made from a template, receipt filed in Drive.
V10. If your laptop were stolen tonight, what survives and why? Model: everything in Google Drive, Gmail, Docs, Sheets and
Tasks survives because it lives in the cloud under the account; only local files are lost.
V11. What would you improve if you had another week? Model: a specific, realistic improvement (for example "connect a
Form to my order sheet", or "add more filters"), showing self-awareness.
V12. What does this certificate mean — and not mean — for you? Model: it means I can set up and run a productivity
system with free tools; it does not make me a consultant or expert, and I must keep practising.
Marking the viva (5 marks): award full marks for accurate, vocabulary-rich answers and fluent live demonstration; about three
for mostly correct answers with prompting; zero to one for inability to locate or explain the system.

## O. Instructor Evaluation Notes
<!-- kind:instructor_notes -->
Observable signs of genuine competence:
The learner navigates their own system quickly and confidently during the viva.
The learner explains the why behind choices (least privilege, date-first naming, rule of three).
The learner's system is complete and internally consistent, not just present.
The learner answers honestly when unsure instead of bluffing.
The learner demonstrates at least one automation actually working.
Common misconceptions to watch for and correct:
"A certificate makes me an expert." → It makes you a competent beginner; state this plainly and kindly.
"If the numbers look right, the formula is fine." → Always check the formula bar.
"Sharing as Editor is more helpful." → Least privilege; use Viewer unless editing is needed.
"I can explain it next time." → If you cannot explain your own system, it needs practice now.
Remediation for learners who do not meet the standard:
1. Identify which of the five pillars is weakest and rebuild just that pillar with the learner.
2. Allow a short, supervised re-demonstration after a brief practice session, if policy permits a resit.
3. Pair the learner with a stronger classmate for a practice viva before re-assessment.
4. Record the gap clearly on the sign-off sheet so the learner knows exactly what to fix.
Differentiation:
Level 1 (supported): allow the build to run longer, provide a partially completed system, and accept a shorter two-minute
presentation.
Level 2 (standard): the capstone as written, five-minute presentation, full viva.
Level 3 (challenge): add a Google Form linked to the order sheet, a third filter, a nested label structure, and a one-month
automation plan.
Timing guidance: Protect the final 10 minutes for the wrap-up and sign-off — finishing the course on a clear, honest, positive
note matters. If the class is large, run presentations in two waves across the session and keep the build time tight so every
learner presents.
