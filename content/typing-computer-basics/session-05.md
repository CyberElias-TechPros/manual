# Email Online Communication

## A. Session Identification
<!-- kind:identification -->
Field Detail
Session Number 5
Title Email & Online Communication
Field Detail
Week 2
Module Digital Independence
Duration 105 minutes
Difficulty Beginner
Prerequisite Knowledge Sessions 1–4 — full computer operation, internet, safe browsing
Connection to Previous Session Builds on Sessions 1–4 by teaching the learner to communicate professionally through
email
Preparation Gmail creation guides, instructor email address, sample attachments, handouts

## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Explain what email is and how it works in simple terms.
2. Create a Gmail account with a professional email address.
3. Compose a professional email with a clear subject line, proper greeting, well-structured body, and professional sign-off.
4. Send and receive emails confidently.
5. Attach files to an email and download attachments received by email.
6. Distinguish between To, CC, and BCC and explain when to use each.
7. Use Reply vs Reply All appropriately.
8. Complete a basic online form (e.g., a practice registration form).
9. Join a Google Meet online meeting using a meeting link.
10. Demonstrate email etiquette and safety including recognising suspicious emails.

## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
The "Letter in the Post" Analogy
Instructor Setup: Bring a physical envelope with a letter inside. Place it on a table at the front of the room.
Instructor Script: "Good morning, everyone. Welcome to the second-to-last session. Today we are going to learn about email.
But before we go online, I want you to look at this." (Hold up the envelope.) "This is an envelope. Inside is a letter. If I wanted to
send this letter to my friend in Lagos, what would I do?" (Pause for answers.) "I would write the address on the envelope, put a
stamp on it, and post it. How long would it take to reach Lagos?" (Pause.) "Three to five days, maybe more.
Now watch this." (Open your Gmail on the projector and send an email to yourself.) "I have just sent an email. It contains a
message and a file. How long did it take?" (Pause.) "Five seconds. It is already in the inbox.
Email is the fastest way to send messages, documents, photographs and files anywhere in the world. It is used by billions of
people every day. In this session, you will create your first email account, send your first email, and learn how to communicate
professionally. This is the skill that will make you truly digitally independent. Let us begin."
Expected Learner Response: Excitement about creating their first email account. Some nervousness about getting it "right."

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Instructor Script: "Before we start, let us remember what we learned last time. Quick review."
Review Questions and Expected Answers:
1. "What is the internet?"
A giant network of connected computers.
2. "What is the difference between a URL and a search?"
A URL is a specific website address typed into the address bar. A search is when you type keywords into a search engine.
3. "How do you know if a website is safe?"
Check the URL, look for the padlock icon, and check if the design looks professional.
4. "What is phishing?"
When a scammer pretends to be a trusted organisation and tricks you into giving them your password or personal
information.
5. "What is the most important rule of internet safety?"
Stop and think before you click, download or share.
How to Address Common Gaps:
If a learner cannot remember the padlock rule, remind them: "The padlock means the website is secure."
If a learner forgot what phishing is, remind them: "Phishing is a fake email or website that tries to steal your information."

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: What Is Email? (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Email stands for 'electronic mail.' It is a way to send messages, documents and files to anyone in the world
who has an email address. It is the most common form of professional communication in the world.
How Does Email Work? When you send an email, it travels through the internet to the recipient's email server. The server stores
the email until the recipient logs in and checks their inbox. This happens in seconds.
An email address has two parts:
The username (before the @ symbol) — this is your unique name
The domain (after the @ symbol) — this is the email provider
For example: john.doe@gmail.com
Username: john.doe
Domain: gmail.com (Google's email service)
Why Email Matters: Email is used for everything — job applications, business communication, school assignments, online
banking, government services. Almost every online service requires an email address. If you do not have an email address, you
are locked out of a huge part of the digital world.
Email Providers: The most popular email providers are:
Gmail (Google) — free, reliable, and the most widely used
Outlook (Microsoft) — also free, popular in corporate environments
Yahoo Mail — older but still used by many people
We will use Gmail in this course because it is free, easy to use and works well on both computers and phones."
Topic 2: Creating a Gmail Account (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Now let us create a Gmail account. This is your first professional email address. Follow along on your own
machines."
Step-by-Step Instructions:
1. "Open your browser. Go to gmail.com ."
2. "Click 'Create account' (usually in the top-right corner or centre of the page)."
3. "Choose 'For my personal use' (or 'For myself')."
4. "Enter your first name and last name."
5. "Choose your username. This will be your email address. For example, if your name is Adaeze Okafor, your username could
be adaeze.okafor or adaezeokafor2026 . If the username is taken, Gmail will suggest alternatives. Choose one that is
professional."
6. "Create a strong password. Use a mix of letters, numbers and symbols. Do not use your name, your birthday or
'password123.' Write the password down on a piece of paper and keep it safe."
7. "Click 'Next.'"
8. "Enter your phone number. Google will send a verification code to your phone. This is to prove you are a real person. Enter
the code when you receive it."
9. "Enter your recovery email address (optional but recommended). This is a backup email where Google can send a reset link
if you forget your password."
10. "Enter your birthday and gender."
11. "Click 'Next.'"
12. "Read the Privacy and Terms. Scroll to the bottom and click 'I agree.'"
13. "Congratulations! Your Gmail account is created. You will see your inbox."
Important Notes:
Your email address should be professional. Avoid numbers that look random (e.g., adaeze12345@gmail.com ). Use your real
name if possible.
Your password should be strong and unique. Do not use the same password for every account.
Write down your email address and password on a piece of paper and keep it safe.
Topic 3: The Gmail Interface (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Now that you have a Gmail account, let me show you the main parts of the interface."
Key Parts of Gmail:
Inbox: This is where you receive new emails. Unread emails are bold.
Sent: This is where you see emails you have sent.
Drafts: This is where emails you are writing but have not sent yet are saved.
Spam: This is where Gmail puts suspicious emails. Check it occasionally in case a legitimate email ends up there.
Compose button: This is the button you click to write a new email. It is usually in the top-left corner and says "Compose"
with a plus icon.
Search bar: This is at the top of the page. You can search for emails by sender, subject or keyword.
Profile icon: This is in the top-right corner. Click it to manage your Google account or sign out.
Inbox Layout: Each email in your inbox shows:
The sender's name (on the left)
The subject line (in the middle)
The date or time received (on the right)
A preview of the email content (below the subject)
Unread emails are bold. Read emails are in normal text. You can click on any email to open it.
Topic 4: Composing a Professional Email (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Now let us learn how to write a professional email. An email has five parts:
1. Subject Line The subject line tells the recipient what the email is about. It should be clear and specific. A good subject line:
"Meeting Rescheduled to Friday 10am." A bad subject line: "Hello" or "Important" or no subject at all.
2. Greeting The greeting is how you address the recipient. Common greetings:
"Dear Mr. Okafor," (formal, for someone you do not know well)
"Dear Adaeze," (semi-formal, for someone you know)
"Hello John," (casual, for colleagues)
"Good morning," (time-appropriate)
Do not use informal greetings like "Hey" or "What's up?" in professional emails.
3. Body The body is the main message. It should be:
Clear: Say what you mean. Do not use vague language.
Concise: Keep it short. Get to the point.
Structured: Use paragraphs. One idea per paragraph.
Polite: Use "please" and "thank you."
4. Sign-off The sign-off is how you end the email. Common sign-offs:
"Best regards," (formal)
"Sincerely," (formal)
"Kind regards," (semi-formal)
"Thanks," (casual)
5. Name Your name goes below the sign-off. You can also include your title, organisation and contact information.
Example of a Professional Email:
Subject: Application for Administrative Assistant Position
Dear Mr. Okafor,
I am writing to apply for the Administrative Assistant position at your company, as advertised on your website. I have attached
my CV and a cover letter for your consideration.
I have two years of administrative experience and I am proficient in Microsoft Word, Excel and PowerPoint. I am organised,
detail-oriented and comfortable working in a fast-paced office environment.
I would welcome the opportunity to discuss my application in an interview. I am available at your convenience.
Thank you for your time and consideration.
Best regards, Adaeze Okafor Phone: 0801 234 5678 Email: adaeze.okafor@gmail.com"
Topic 5: To, CC, and BCC (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "When you compose an email, you will see three fields for recipients: To, CC, and BCC. Let me explain each
one.
To: This is the primary recipient — the person you are directly addressing. Put their email address in the To field.
CC (Carbon Copy): CC means "carbon copy." This is for people who need to see the email but are not the primary recipient. For
example, if you are sending an email to a client but want your manager to see it, you would put your manager's email in CC.
BCC (Blind Carbon Copy): BCC means "blind carbon copy." This is for people who need to see the email but should not be
visible to other recipients. For example, if you are sending an email to 50 clients and you do not want them to see each other's
email addresses, you would put all their email addresses in BCC.
When to Use Each:
To: The person you are directly addressing.
CC: People who need to be informed but do not need to act.
BCC: People who need to see the email but should not be visible to others. Also used for mass emails to protect privacy.
Common Mistake — Reply All: When you receive an email that was sent to multiple people, you can choose to Reply
(respond only to the sender) or Reply All (respond to everyone). Use Reply All only when your response is relevant to everyone. If
your response is only for the sender, use Reply. Reply All is one of the most misused features in email. It can cause
embarrassment and wasted time."

## G. INSTRUCTOR DEMONSTRATION (15 minutes)
<!-- kind:demo -->
Demonstration 1: Composing and Sending an Email (5 minutes)
What the Instructor Prepares:
Gmail open on the projector
A professional email address to send to (e.g., cea.assessment@gmail.com)
What the Instructor Says and Does: "I am going to compose and send a professional email. Watch carefully."
1. "I click the 'Compose' button in the top-left corner. A new window opens."
2. "In the 'To' field, I type the recipient's email address: cea.assessment@gmail.com ."
3. "In the 'Subject' field, I type: 'Introduction from [Instructor Name] — CEA Learner.'"
4. "In the body, I write: 'Dear CEA Team, My name is [Instructor Name]. I am a learner at Cyber Elias Academy. I am writing to
introduce myself and to confirm that I have successfully created my Gmail account. I look forward to completing the
Typing & Computer Basics course. Best regards, [Instructor Name]'"
5. "I click the 'Send' button at the bottom of the window. The email is sent."
What Learners Should Observe: The instructor composing a professional email with a clear subject line, greeting, body, and
sign-off.
Expected Result: Learners can describe the five parts of a professional email.
Questions the Instructor Can Ask:
"What are the five parts of a professional email?" (Answer: Subject line, greeting, body, sign-off, name.)
"What is the difference between To and CC?" (Answer: To is for the primary recipient. CC is for people who need to see the
email but are not the primary recipient.)
Demonstration 2: Attaching a File to an Email (5 minutes)
What the Instructor Prepares:
A sample file on the Desktop (e.g., a .txt file called "Practice Document.txt")
Gmail open with a new compose window
What the Instructor Says and Does: "Now I am going to attach a file to an email. Watch carefully."
1. "I click 'Compose' to open a new email."
2. "I fill in the To, Subject and Body fields."
3. "I click the paperclip icon at the bottom of the compose window. This is the 'Attach files' button."
4. "A file picker opens. I navigate to the Desktop. I find the file 'Practice Document.txt.' I double-click it."
5. "The file is attached. I can see its name at the bottom of the compose window."
6. "I click 'Send.' The email with the attachment is sent."
What Learners Should Observe: The instructor clicking the paperclip icon, navigating to the file, and attaching it.
Expected Result: Learners can describe the steps to attach a file to an email.
Common Errors and Corrections:
If the learner cannot find the paperclip icon: "It is at the bottom of the compose window. It looks like a paperclip."
If the learner cannot find the file: "Click 'Browse' or navigate to the Desktop. The file should be there. If not, ask the
instructor to provide it."
Demonstration 3: Downloading an Email Attachment (5 minutes)
What the Instructor Prepares:
An email with an attachment already in the instructor's inbox
A designated download location (e.g., the Desktop)
What the Instructor Says and Does: "Now I am going to show you how to download an attachment from an email you
receive."
1. "I have received an email with an attachment. I can see the attachment icon (a paperclip) next to the sender's name in my
inbox."
2. "I click on the email to open it."
3. "At the bottom of the email, I can see the attachment. It shows the file name and a 'Download' button."
4. "I click 'Download.' The file is saved to my Downloads folder."
5. "To find the file, I open File Explorer and navigate to 'Downloads.' The file is there."
What Learners Should Observe: The instructor clicking on the email, clicking the download button, and finding the file in the
Downloads folder.
Expected Result: Learners can describe the steps to download an email attachment.
Safety Reminder: "Only download attachments from people you know and trust. If you receive an unexpected attachment
from an unknown sender, do not download it. It could be a virus."

## I. INDEPENDENT CLASS EXERCISE (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:exercise -->
Exercise: "Email Etiquette Quiz"
Scenario: "You are working as an administrator at a company in Port Harcourt. Your boss asks you to review some emails that
were sent by junior staff. She wants to know which emails are professional and which ones need improvement. Below are four
email descriptions. For each one, decide if it is professional or needs improvement, and explain why."
Task: Read each email description and write down:
1. Is it professional or needs improvement?
2. Why?
Email Descriptions:
Email A: Subject: "Meeting" Body: "Hey, the meeting is tomorrow. Don't be late. — Tunde"
Email B: Subject: "Request for Leave" Body: "Dear Mr. Adeyemi, I would like to request leave from the 15th to the 20th of
October for a family matter. I have attached the leave application form for your approval. Thank you for your understanding.
Best regards, Funmi Adewale"
Email C: Subject: "URGENT!!!" Body: "SEND ME THE REPORT NOW!!!"
Email D: Subject: "Weekly Sales Report — Week 42" Body: "Dear Team, Please find attached the weekly sales report for Week 42.
Total sales for the week were ₦2,450,000, which is a 12% increase from last week. Let me know if you have any questions. Best
regards, Chika Obi"
Requirements:
Write in full sentences where possible
You may discuss with a partner, but write your own answers
Time Allowance: 10 minutes
Success Criteria:
All four emails correctly identified as professional or needing improvement
At least three explanations that correctly identify the strengths or weaknesses
Hints Available If the Learner Gets Stuck:
Hint 1: "Does the email have a clear subject line?"
Hint 2: "Does the email use a professional greeting and sign-off?"
Hint 3: "Is the tone polite and respectful?"
Complete Answer Key for the Instructor:
Email A: Needs Improvement

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)

Case: "Adaeze's Job Application"
Situation: Adaeze has just finished her NYSC. She sees a job advert for an administrative assistant at a company in Port

Subject: "Job" Body: "Here is my CV. — Adaeze"
She attaches her CV and sends it. Two weeks later, she receives a rejection email. The reason: her email was unprofessional.
The Problem: Adaeze's email was too informal and incomplete. She did not use a proper greeting, she did not introduce herself,
she did not mention the position she was applying for, and she did not use a professional sign-off. The HR manager received
hundreds of applications. Adaeze's email stood out — for the wrong reasons.
Why It Matters: In the professional world, your email is often the first impression you make. A well-written email shows that
you are professional, detail-oriented and serious about the opportunity. A poorly written email suggests the opposite. Many
qualified candidates lose job opportunities because of bad email skills.
Relevant Concept: Professional email writing. If Adaeze had taken this course, she would have known how to write a proper
professional email.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Using an Informal Email Address
Symptoms: The learner creates an email address like sexyboy2000@gmail.com or cutegirl123@gmail.com . Likely Cause: The learner
does not understand that an email address is a professional identity. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "What is your email
address?" If it contains slang, numbers or informal words, it is unprofessional. Safe Corrective Steps: Explain that an email
address is like a name tag. It should be professional and based on the learner's real name. Help the learner create a new,
professional email address. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner's email address is professional and based on
their real name. How to Prevent Recurrence: Before creating an email address, ask the learner, "Would you be comfortable
giving this email address to a potential employer?" If the answer is no, help them choose a better one.
Mistake 2: Forgetting to Attach a File
Symptoms: The learner writes "Please find attached..." but does not attach the file. Likely Cause: The learner forgets to click the
paperclip icon. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Did you attach the file?" Check the sent email. Is there an attachment?
Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to always check for the attachment before clicking Send. Show them how to look for
the file name at the bottom of the compose window. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner checks for the
attachment before sending. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make it a habit. Before every email, ask: "Did I attach the file?"
Mistake 3: Using Reply All When Reply Is Appropriate
Symptoms: The learner uses Reply All to respond to an email that was sent to many people, but their response is only relevant
to the sender. Likely Cause: The learner does not understand the difference between Reply and Reply All. How to Investigate:
Ask the learner, "Did everyone need to see your response?" Safe Corrective Steps: Explain the difference between Reply and
Reply All. Teach the learner to use Reply unless everyone needs to see the response. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved:
The learner uses Reply when appropriate and Reply All only when necessary. How to Prevent Recurrence: Remind the learner:
"Reply All is for everyone. Reply is for the sender. Choose carefully."
Mistake 4: Writing in All Caps
Symptoms: The learner writes an email in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS. Likely Cause: The learner does not know that writing in all
caps is considered shouting. How to Investigate: Read the learner's email. Is it in all caps? Safe Corrective Steps: Explain that
writing in all caps is like shouting. It is rude and unprofessional. Teach the learner to use normal capitalisation. How to Confirm
the Problem Is Resolved: The learner writes in normal capitalisation. How to Prevent Recurrence: Remind the learner: "All
caps is shouting. Use normal letters."
Mistake 5: Not Checking Spam Folder
Symptoms: The learner says, "I did not receive the email." But the email is in the Spam folder. Likely Cause: Gmail sometimes
puts legitimate emails in Spam. How to Investigate: Ask the learner to check their Spam folder. Safe Corrective Steps: Teach
the learner to check their Spam folder regularly. Show them how to mark an email as "Not spam" if it is legitimate. How to
Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner checks their Spam folder and finds the missing email. How to Prevent
Recurrence: Remind the learner: "Check your Spam folder if you are expecting an email but do not see it."

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
Questions
1. Multiple Choice: What are the five parts of a professional email? a) Subject, greeting, body, sign-off, name b) To, CC, BCC,
attachment, send c) Username, domain, password, inbox, sent d) Browser, URL, search, download, bookmark
Answer: a) Subject, greeting, body, sign-off, name
2. Short Answer: What is the difference between To, CC, and BCC?
Answer: To is for the primary recipient. CC is for people who need to see the email but are not the primary recipient. BCC is for
people who need to see the email but should not be visible to other recipients.
3. Multiple Choice: What should you do if you receive an email from an unknown sender with an attachment? a) Download the
attachment immediately b) Open the attachment to see what it is c) Do not download the attachment — it could be a virus d)
Forward the attachment to your friends
Answer: c) Do not download the attachment — it could be a virus
4. Explain in Your Own Words: Why is it important to use a professional email address?
Answer: A professional email address is based on your real name and does not contain slang, numbers or informal words. It
shows that you are serious and professional. An unprofessional email address can cost you job opportunities and make a bad
first impression.
5. Practical Identification: Open Gmail. Compose a new email. Identify the To field, the Subject field, the Compose button, and
the Attach button (paperclip icon).
Answer: The learner correctly identifies all four elements.
6. Scenario-Based: Your friend says, "I sent an email but the recipient says they did not get it. What happened?" What are two
things you would tell your friend to check?
Answer: (1) Check the Sent folder to verify the email was sent. (2) Check if the recipient's email address was typed correctly. (3)
Check if the email ended up in the recipient's Spam folder.
7. Error-Detecting: A learner writes in their assignment: "I used Reply All to respond to an email from my boss." When is this
appropriate, and when is it not?
Answer: Reply All is appropriate when everyone who received the original email needs to see the response. It is not appropriate
when the response is only for the sender. In most cases, Reply is the better choice.
