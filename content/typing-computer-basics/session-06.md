## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Power on a computer and log in to a Windows account without assistance.
2. Create a folder on the Desktop with a specific name.
3. Open Microsoft Word and type a short paragraph (minimum 50 words).
4. Save the document inside the created folder.
5. Open a web browser and search for a specific file.
6. Download the file from a safe, trusted source.
7. Open Gmail and compose a new email.
8. Attach the downloaded file to the email.
9. Send the email to the instructor.
10. Complete a knowledge check quiz covering all sessions.
11. Demonstrate improved typing speed compared to their Session 2 baseline.
12. Reflect on their learning and identify areas for continued growth.

## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
"You Are Ready"
Instructor Script: "Good morning, everyone. This is it — the final session. Two weeks ago, many of you had never turned on a
computer. Today, you are going to prove that you can use one independently.
I am not going to help you. I am not going to give you step-by-step instructions. I am going to give you a task, and you are
going to complete it on your own. This is not a test of perfection. It is a test of independence. Can you sit down at a computer

If you have attended the previous five sessions and practised, you are ready. If you are nervous, that is normal. Take a deep
breath. You know how to do this. Let us begin."
Expected Learner Response: A mix of excitement and nervousness. Some learners will be confident. Others will be anxious. The
instructor's calm, encouraging tone sets the atmosphere.

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Instructor Script: "Before we start the assessment, let us do a quick review of everything you have learned. I will ask questions
and you answer out loud."
Review Questions and Expected Answers:
1. "What are the five main parts of a computer?"
Monitor, keyboard, mouse, system unit (CPU), speakers.
2. "What are the five things you can do with a mouse?"
Left-click, right-click, double-click, drag-and-drop, scroll.
3. "Where do your fingers rest on the keyboard?"
On the home row: A S D F (left hand) and J K L ; (right hand).
4. "What does Ctrl+S do?"
It saves the current work.
5. "What is the difference between a file and a folder?"
A file is a single piece of information. A folder is a container for files.
6. "What is the internet?"
A giant network of connected computers.
7. "How do you know if a website is safe?"
Check the URL, look for the padlock icon, and check if the design looks professional.
8. "What are the five parts of a professional email?"
Subject line, greeting, body, sign-off, name.
9. "What is the most important rule of internet safety?"
Stop and think before you click, download or share.
10. "What is the correct way to shut down a computer?"
Click Start, click Power, click Shut down.
How to Address Common Gaps:
If a learner cannot answer a question, do not embarrass them. Ask another learner to help. The goal is to activate prior
knowledge, not to test.

## I. INDEPENDENT CLASS EXERCISE (50 minutes)
<!-- kind:exercise -->
Exercise: "Demonstrated Independence"
Scenario: "You are a new employee at a small business in Port Harcourt. On your first day, your manager asks you to complete a
set of computer tasks. You have never done these tasks before, but you have completed a computer course at Cyber Elias
Academy. You sit down at the computer and begin."
Task: Complete the following 13 steps without assistance:
1. Turn on the computer and log in to the Windows account.
2. Open File Explorer.
3. Create a new folder on the Desktop named "CEA Assessment — [Your Name]."
4. Open Microsoft Word.
5. Type a short paragraph (minimum 50 words) introducing yourself and describing what you have learned in this course.
6. Save the document inside the folder you created in step 3.
7. Open a web browser.
8. Go to the website written on the whiteboard.
9. Download the file from that website.
10. Open Gmail.
11. Compose a new email to: cea.assessment@gmail.com
12. Attach the downloaded file to the email.
13. Send the email.
Requirements:
Complete all 13 steps independently
Do not ask the instructor for step-by-step guidance
If you get stuck, try to troubleshoot on your own first
Use your notes and the skills you have learned
Time Allowance: 50 minutes
Success Criteria:
All 13 steps completed
The folder is correctly named and contains the Word document
The email is sent with the correct attachment
The learner can explain what they did and why
Hints Available If the Learner Gets Stuck:
The instructor may provide a single hint if a learner is truly stuck for more than 5 minutes. The hint should be a question,
not an answer. For example: "What did we use to create a folder?" or "Where did we click to attach a file?"
The instructor should NOT provide the full answer. The learner should figure it out themselves.
Complete Answer Key for the Instructor:
The instructor observes each learner and records their progress on the assessment checklist. The checklist is provided in Part 6
(Capstone Project Pack).
What the Instructor Should Look For:
Did the learner complete all 13 steps?
Did the learner use correct procedures (e.g., saving in the right folder, attaching the right file)?
Did the learner demonstrate safe browsing habits?
Did the learner type using correct finger placement?
Did the learner troubleshoot any problems independently?
Did the learner ask for help? If so, how many times?
Grading: The capstone is graded as Pass / Not Yet Competent.
Pass: The learner completed all 13 steps independently (with no more than 2 hints from the instructor).
Not Yet Competent: The learner could not complete all 13 steps, or required more than 2 hints. The learner is given the
opportunity to practise and retake the assessment.

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "Emmanuel's First Day"
Situation: Emmanuel completed the Typing & Computer Basics course at CEA. He passed the capstone assessment. Two weeks
later, he starts a new job as an office assistant at a law firm in Port Harcourt. On his first day, his boss says: "Create a folder on
your desktop called 'Client Files.' Type a short memo about yourself and save it in that folder. Then email it to me."
Emmanuel sits down at the computer. He takes a deep breath. He has done this before. He turns on the computer, logs in,
creates the folder, opens Word, types the memo, saves it, opens his email, attaches the file, and sends it. Total time: 8 minutes.
His boss is impressed. "You are the fastest new starter we have had," she says.
The Problem: Emmanuel's colleague, Chinedu, also started on the same day. Chinedu did not take the CEA course. When his
boss gives him the same task, he stares at the screen. He does not know how to create a folder. He does not know how to attach
a file. He has to ask for help. His boss is not impressed.
Why It Matters: Emmanuel and Chinedu have the same job. But Emmanuel can work independently. Chinedu cannot. In a
workplace, independence is valuable. Employers do not have time to hold your hand. They expect you to be able to sit down
and get to work.
Relevant Concept: All the skills taught in Sessions 1–5. Emmanuel's success is the result of two weeks of focused, practical
learning.
Worked Solution: Emmanuel succeeded because he had practised every step of the workflow multiple times. He had created
folders, saved documents, downloaded files, and sent emails with attachments. By the time he did it in a real workplace, it was
second nature.
Reasoning: The capstone assessment is not just a test. It is a rehearsal for real life. When you can complete the workflow
independently in class, you can complete it independently in a workplace.
Possible Mistake: Emmanuel might have been nervous on his first day. Nervousness can make you forget things. But because
he had practised so much, his hands remembered what to do even when his brain was stressed.
Correction: Practice until the skills become automatic. Do not just practise until you can do it. Practise until you cannot fail.
Transfer Exercise: "Think about a task you might be asked to do in a real job. Write down the steps you would take. Then
practise those steps until they become automatic."
Professional Connection: Every office job requires the skills taught in this course. Receptionists, administrators, sales officers,
teachers, accountants, managers — everyone. The ability to operate a computer independently is the foundation of all
professional digital work.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Forgetting to Save the Document
Symptoms: The learner types the paragraph in Word but does not save it. Likely Cause: Nervousness or rushing. How to
Investigate: Ask the learner, "Did you save your document?" Safe Corrective Steps: Remind the learner to save early and save
often. Use Ctrl+S. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner saves the document in the correct folder. How to
Prevent Recurrence: Make saving a habit. Save after every significant change.
Mistake 2: Saving the Document in the Wrong Location
Symptoms: The learner saves the document on the Desktop instead of in the created folder. Likely Cause: The learner does not
navigate to the correct folder when saving. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Where did you save the document?" Check
the Desktop. Is the file there? Safe Corrective Steps: Show the learner how to navigate to the correct folder in the Save dialog.
How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner saves the document in the correct folder. How to Prevent Recurrence:
Remind the learner to check the address bar in the Save dialog before clicking Save.
Mistake 3: Attaching the Wrong File
Symptoms: The learner attaches a file other than the downloaded file. Likely Cause: The learner navigates to the wrong folder
when attaching. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Which file did you attach?" Check the sent email. Is the correct file
attached? Safe Corrective Steps: Show the learner how to navigate to the Downloads folder when attaching. How to Confirm
the Problem Is Resolved: The learner attaches the correct file. How to Prevent Recurrence: Remind the learner to check the
file name before clicking Send.
Mistake 4: Typing the Wrong Email Address
Symptoms: The learner types the wrong email address in the To field. Likely Cause: Rushing or not checking the address. How
to Investigate: Ask the learner, "What email address did you type?" Check the sent email. Safe Corrective Steps: Remind the
learner to double-check the email address before sending. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner types the
correct email address. How to Prevent Recurrence: Write the email address on the whiteboard and have learners copy it
carefully.
Mistake 5: Panicking When Something Goes Wrong
Symptoms: The learner encounters a problem (e.g., the download does not work) and freezes. Likely Cause: Lack of confidence
or fear of breaking something. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "What happened? What did you try?" Safe Corrective
Steps: Encourage the learner to stay calm. Remind them that they can fix most problems by trying again or asking a specific
question. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner troubleshoots the problem independently. How to Prevent
Recurrence: Remind the learner that making mistakes is part of learning. The important thing is to stay calm and try to fix the
problem.

## N. REFLECTION AND RECAP (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:reflection -->
Plain-English Summary
Today you demonstrated that you can use a computer independently. You turned on the computer, created a folder, typed a
document, saved it, downloaded a file from the internet, attached it to an email, and sent it — all without help. You also
completed a knowledge check quiz and reflected on your learning. You have completed the Typing & Computer Basics course.
You should be proud of yourself.
Five Important Takeaways from the Entire Course
1. A computer is a tool. It is not magic. It does exactly what you tell it to do.

Common Misconceptions
"I can just learn this on my own." Some learners think they do not need a course. They may be right — some people can
figure things out on their own. But most people benefit from structured, guided learning. This course gives them a
foundation that self-taught learners often lack.
"I will never need to use a computer." Some learners think they will avoid computers in their daily life. In 2026, this is
almost impossible. Banking, communication, shopping, education, government services — everything requires basic
computer skills.
"I am too old to learn this." Some older learners feel they cannot learn new technology. This is not true. Age is not a barrier
to learning computer skills. Patience and practice are the only requirements.
Suggested Remedial Exercises
For learners who do not pass the capstone: Allow them to practise on academy machines during opening hours. Provide a
step-by-step guide they can follow. Let them retake the assessment when they are ready.
For learners who score below 50% on the knowledge check: Review the questions they got wrong. Provide additional
practice exercises. Let them retake the quiz.
For learners whose typing speed has not improved: Provide a daily typing practice plan. Recommend free typing practice
websites. Measure their speed again in one month.
