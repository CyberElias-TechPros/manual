## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Organise design files logically and professionally.
2. Select their best work for a portfolio.
3. Present designs to an audience with confidence.
4. Explain design decisions using correct vocabulary.
5. Receive and implement feedback professionally.
6. Give constructive feedback to peers.

## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
"The Portfolio Review"
Instructor Setup: Display two portfolios — one well-organised, one poorly organised.
Instructor Script: "Good morning, everyone. Look at these two portfolios. They contain the same designs. But one is easy to
navigate and looks professional. The other is disorganised and hard to follow. Which one would you show to a potential client?
- Pause.]
A portfolio is a collection of your best work. It is how you show potential clients or employers what you can do. A well-
organised portfolio makes you look professional. A poorly organised portfolio makes you look careless — even if the designs
themselves are good.
Today, you will learn how to organise your work, select your best designs, present them confidently and handle feedback
professionally. These are the skills that turn a designer into a professional."

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Review Questions and Expected Answers:
1. "What are the key elements of a business card?"
Business name, your name and title, phone number, email, address, logo.
2. "What are the four types of logos?"
Wordmark, lettermark, icon/symbol, combination.
3. "How do you export a design for print?"
Download as PDF Print at 300 DPI.
4. "What is the 60-30-10 rule?"
60% dominant colour, 30% secondary colour, 10% accent colour.
5. "What is the most important principle of branding?"
Consistency.

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: Organising Design Files (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Professional designers organise their files carefully. This makes it easy to find work, share files with clients
and maintain version control.
File Organisation Tips:
1. Use folders. Create a folder for each project or client.
2. Use descriptive file names. Instead of 'design1.png,' use 'restaurant-flyer-v1.png.'
3. Use version numbers. When you revise a design, save it as 'v2,' 'v3,' etc. This way you can go back to previous versions if
needed.
4. Separate final files from working files. Keep your final exported files in a separate folder from your Canva working files.
5. Back up your work. Save copies of your work in at least two places — your computer and a USB drive or cloud storage.
File Naming Convention: [Client Name] - [Design Type] - [Version] - [Date] Example: Adaezes-Fashion-Flyer-v1-08102026.png
Topic 2: Building a Portfolio (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "A portfolio is a curated collection of your best work. It is not everything you have ever created — it is your
strongest pieces that demonstrate your range and skills.
What to Include in a Portfolio:
1. Your best work. Only include designs you are proud of.
2. Variety. Show different types of designs — flyers, social posts, business cards, logos.
3. Context. For each design, include a brief description — what was the purpose, who was the audience, what tools did you
use.
4. Process. If possible, show the process — initial concept, revisions, final design.
How to Create a Simple Portfolio:
1. Canva Presentation. Create a Canva presentation with one slide per design.
2. PDF Portfolio. Export your designs as images and compile them into a PDF.
3. Online Portfolio. Use a free platform like Behance or a simple Google Slides presentation.
Portfolio Tips:
Start with your strongest piece.
End with your second strongest piece.
Keep it concise — 5–10 pieces is enough for a beginner portfolio.
Update it regularly as you create new work.
Topic 3: Presenting Designs (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Presenting your work is a skill. A good presentation can make a mediocre design look great. A bad
presentation can make a great design look mediocre.
Presentation Tips:
1. Start with the brief. Explain what the client asked for.
2. Explain your decisions. Why did you choose these colours? Why this font? Why this layout?
3. Show the process. If you have multiple versions, show how the design evolved.
4. Be confident. You know more about your design than anyone else in the room.
5. Invite feedback. Ask specific questions — "What do you think of the colour choice?" "Is the hierarchy clear?"
Handling Feedback:
1. Listen carefully. Do not interrupt or get defensive.
2. Ask clarifying questions. "Can you explain what you mean by 'too busy'?"
3. Thank the person. Feedback is a gift — even if it is hard to hear.
4. Evaluate the feedback. Not all feedback is useful. Consider the source and the context.
5. Implement what makes sense. You do not have to implement every suggestion, but you should consider each one.
Topic 4: Giving Constructive Feedback (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Giving feedback is as important as receiving it. Good feedback is specific, constructive and kind.
How to Give Constructive Feedback:
1. Start with what works. "I like the colour combination — it feels fresh and modern."
2. Be specific. Instead of "I do not like it," say "The text is hard to read because the contrast is low."
3. Suggest solutions. "Have you tried making the headline larger?"
4. Be kind. Critique the work, not the person.
5. Ask questions. "What was your reasoning behind this choice?"
Feedback Formula: "I like [specific element] because [reason]. I wonder if [suggestion] would improve [specific aspect]. Have
you considered [alternative]?"

Canva open on the projector

2. "Slide 1: Title slide — my name and 'Design Portfolio.'"
3. "Slide 2: First design — a flyer. I add the exported image and a brief description."
4. "Slide 3: Second design — a business card. Same format."
5. "Slide 4: Third design — a social media post. Same format."
6. "Slide 5: Thank you slide with my contact information."
7. "I apply a consistent theme across all slides."
8. "I download as PDF."
What Learners Should Observe: The instructor creating a simple, professional portfolio in Canva.
Demonstration 2: Presenting a Design (5 minutes)
What the Instructor Prepares:
One of the designs from the portfolio
What the Instructor Says and Does: "Now I will present this design as if I were presenting to a client."
1. "The brief was to create a flyer for a restaurant's grand opening."
2. "I chose warm colours — red and orange — because they are associated with food and energy."
3. "I made the restaurant name the largest text because that is the most important information."
4. "I used a clear call to action — 'Grand Opening — This Saturday' — so people know what to do."
5. "I left plenty of white space so the design does not feel cluttered."
6. "I would love to hear your feedback. What do you think of the colour choice? Is the hierarchy clear?"
What Learners Should Observe: The instructor presenting a design with confidence and explaining decisions.
Demonstration 3: Receiving Feedback (5 minutes)
What the Instructor Prepares:
The same design, with a simulated "client" (another instructor or a learner)
What the Instructor Says and Does: "The client says: 'I like the design but I think it is too busy. Can you simplify it?'"
1. "I listen carefully and do not get defensive."
2. "I ask a clarifying question: 'When you say busy, do you mean there is too many elements, or the colours are too bright?'"
3. "The client says: 'There are too many elements.'"
4. "I thank the client: 'Thank you for the feedback. I will remove the decorative elements and simplify the layout.'"
5. "I make the revision and present the updated design."
What Learners Should Observe: The instructor receiving feedback professionally and implementing it.

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "The Designer Who Lost a Client"
Situation: A freelance designer presents work to a potential client. The designer shows 20 designs without any explanation. The
client is overwhelmed and confused. The designer does not ask for feedback or explain their decisions. The client hires a
different designer.
The Problem: The designer failed to present their work effectively. They did not explain their decisions, did not organise their
portfolio and did not engage the client in a conversation.
Relevant Concept: Portfolio presentation. If the designer had presented 5 strong pieces with clear explanations, the client
would have been impressed.
Worked Solution: The designer learns to:
1. Select their 5 best pieces.
2. Organise them from strongest to strongest.
3. Explain each design decision clearly.
4. Ask for feedback and respond professionally.
5. The next client is impressed and hires them.
Transfer Exercise: "You are presenting your work to a potential employer. How would you structure your presentation?"

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Including Everything in the Portfolio
Symptoms: The portfolio has 20+ designs of varying quality. Fix: Select only your best 5–10 pieces. Quality over quantity.
Mistake 2: Not Explaining Design Decisions
Symptoms: The presenter shows designs without any explanation. Fix: Always explain the brief, your decisions and the
reasoning behind them.
Mistake 3: Getting Defensive About Feedback
Symptoms: The designer argues with feedback instead of listening. Fix: Listen carefully, ask clarifying questions, thank the
person, then evaluate.
Mistake 4: Inconsistent Portfolio Design
Symptoms: Each slide has a different theme or layout. Fix: Apply a consistent theme across all portfolio slides.
Mistake 5: Not Having a Call to Action
Symptoms: The portfolio ends without contact information. Fix: Always end with a thank you slide and your contact
information.

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
1. What should a portfolio contain? Answer: Your best work, not everything you have created.
2. How many pieces should a beginner portfolio have? Answer: 5–10 pieces.
3. What is the feedback formula? Answer: "I like [specific element] because [reason]. I wonder if [suggestion] would improve
- specific aspect]."
4. What should every portfolio end with? Answer: A thank you slide with contact information.
5. What is the most important rule when receiving feedback? Answer: Listen carefully and do not get defensive.
