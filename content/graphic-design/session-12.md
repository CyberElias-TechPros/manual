## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Plan and organise a multi-part design project from a brief.
2. Design a simple logo using shapes, text and brand colours.
3. Design a business flyer with clear hierarchy and call to action.
4. Design a social media post for Instagram or Facebook.
5. Design a business card with essential information.
6. Present the brand package to the class with confidence.
7. Handle live critique professionally.
8. Reflect on their learning across the entire course.

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Instructor Script: "Before we start, let us do a quick review of everything you have learned."
Review Questions and Expected Answers:
1. "What are the six core design principles?"
Hierarchy, contrast, alignment, balance, white space, purpose/audience.
2. "What is the 60-30-10 rule?"
60% dominant colour, 30% secondary colour, 10% accent colour.
3. "What are the four font categories?"
Serif, sans-serif, script, display.
4. "How do you export for print?"
PDF Print at 300 DPI.
5. "What are the six steps of the design process?"
Research, plan, create, review, revise, deliver.
6. "What should a portfolio contain?"
Your best 5–10 pieces with descriptions.
7. "What is the most important rule when receiving feedback?"
Listen carefully and do not get defensive.

## I. INDEPENDENT CLASS EXERCISE (60 minutes)
<!-- kind:exercise -->
Exercise: "Mini Brand Package"
Scenario: "You are a designer hired to create a mini brand package for a new business. Create all four deliverables as a unified
package."
Task: Complete the following project without assistance:
Step 1: Choose Your Business (2 minutes) Choose a type of business. Examples:
A fashion boutique
A restaurant
A tech startup
A salon
A provision shop
A church media team
Or any other business you like
Step 2: Design a Simple Logo (10 minutes)
1. Create a new design in Canva with dimensions 500 x 500 pixels.
2. Choose 2–3 brand colours.
3. Add a shape that represents the business.
4. Add the business name or initials.
5. Use a clean, bold font.
6. Group all elements.
7. Save as 'My Logo.'
Step 3: Design a Business Flyer (20 minutes)
1. Create a new design with A4 dimensions (2480 x 3508 pixels).
2. Set the background to one of your brand colours.
3. Add the business name in large, bold text.
4. Add a tagline or offer.
5. Add an image that represents the business.
6. Add key information — what, where, when.
7. Add a call to action.
8. Add contact information.
9. Apply your brand colours and fonts.
10. Download as PDF Print.
11. Save as 'My Flyer.'
Step 4: Design a Social Media Post (15 minutes)
1. Create a new design with Instagram dimensions (1080 x 1080 pixels).
2. Use the same brand colours and fonts.
3. Add one image.
4. Add one headline.
5. Add one call to action.
6. Download as PNG.
7. Save as 'My Social Post.'
Step 5: Design a Business Card (10 minutes)
1. Create a new design with business card dimensions (1050 x 600 pixels).
2. Add the business name.
3. Add your name and title.
4. Add the logo.
5. Add essential contact information.
6. Apply your brand colours and fonts.
7. Download as PDF Print.
8. Save as 'My Business Card.'
Step 6: Save All Documents (3 minutes) Save all four designs in Canva in a folder called 'CEA Final Project — [Your Name].'
Requirements:
Complete all 6 steps independently.
All four designs must use the same brand colours and fonts.
The flyer and business card must be exported as PDF Print.
The social media post must be exported as PNG.
The logo must be exported as PNG.
Time Allowance: 60 minutes
Success Criteria:
All four designs created to a satisfactory standard.
All designs use consistent brand colours and fonts.
All files exported in the correct format.
All files saved in Canva.

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "Emmanuel's First Client"
Situation: Emmanuel completed the Graphic Design course at CEA. He passed the capstone assessment. Two weeks later, a
friend asks him to create a brand package for a new business. Emmanuel sits down at the computer and creates a logo, flyer,
social media post and business card — all in one afternoon. His friend is thrilled.
The Problem: Emmanuel's friend needed a complete brand package quickly and could not afford a professional designer.
Relevant Concept: All the skills taught in Sessions 1–11. Emmanuel's success is the result of four weeks of focused, practical
learning.
Worked Solution: Emmanuel succeeded because he had practised every step of the process multiple times. He had created
logos, flyers, social media posts and business cards. By the time he did it for a real client, it was second nature.
Transfer Exercise: "Think about a business that needs a brand package. Write down the steps you would take to create it."

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Choosing a Business That Is Too Complex
Symptoms: The learner chooses a business that is too complex and cannot complete the project in time. Fix: Encourage the
learner to choose a simple business.
Mistake 2: Not Managing Time
Symptoms: The learner spends too much time on one design and does not have time for the others. Fix: Teach the learner to
allocate time for each design.
Mistake 3: Inconsistent Branding
Symptoms: The four designs use different colours and fonts. Fix: Remind the learner to use the same brand colours and fonts
across all designs.
Mistake 4: Not Exporting in the Correct Format
Symptoms: The learner exports everything as PNG instead of PDF Print for print materials. Fix: Remind the learner to export
print materials as PDF Print and digital materials as PNG.
Mistake 5: Not Saving Designs
Symptoms: The learner creates designs but does not save them in Canva. Fix: Remind the learner to save all designs in Canva
with descriptive names.

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (15 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
Quiz
Section A: Design Principles (5 questions)
1. What are the six core design principles?
2. What is the 60-30-10 rule?
3. What is the rule of thirds?
4. Why is white space important?
5. What is the difference between a serif and a sans-serif font?
Section B: Canva Skills (5 questions)
6. How do you create a custom-sized design in Canva?
7. How do you group elements in Canva?
8. How do you set up a brand kit in Canva?
9. How do you resize a design in Canva?
10. What is the difference between PNG and PDF Print?
Section C: Real-World Design (5 questions)
11. What are the five key elements of a business flyer?
12. What is the standard size of a business card?
13. What are the four types of logos?
14. What should a portfolio contain?
15. What are the six steps of the design process?
Section D: Professional Workflow (5 questions)
16. What are the six key questions to ask a client?
17. How many revision rounds should you include in your price?
18. What should you deliver to a client at the end of a project?
19. What is the most important rule when receiving feedback?
20. What is the most important skill in client work?
Answer Key
Section A:
1. Hierarchy, contrast, alignment, balance, white space, purpose/audience.
2. 60% dominant colour, 30% secondary colour, 10% accent colour.
3. Dividing an image into nine equal parts and placing key elements along the lines.
4. It gives the viewer's eye a place to rest and makes the design easier to read.
5. Serif fonts have small lines at the ends of letters. Sans-serif fonts do not.
Section B: 6. Click 'Create a design,' then 'Custom size,' and enter the dimensions. 7. Select multiple elements, right-click, and
choose 'Group' (or press Ctrl+G). 8. Click 'Brand Kit' in the left sidebar, add brand colours and fonts. 9. Click 'Resize' in the top
bar, enter new dimensions, and click 'Resize.' 10. PNG is for digital use (screens). PDF Print is for print (paper).
Section C: 11. Business name, tagline/offer, key information, call to action, contact information. 12. 3.5 x 2 inches (1050 x 600
pixels at 300 DPI). 13. Wordmark, lettermark, icon/symbol, combination. 14. Your best 5–10 pieces with descriptions. 15.
Research, plan, create, review, revise, deliver.
Section D: 16. Purpose, audience, information, format, timeline, budget. 17. 2–3 rounds. 18. Final exported files, source files (if
requested), and a README file (optional). 19. Listen carefully and do not get defensive. 20. Communication — listening, asking
questions and explaining decisions.

Common Misconceptions
"I can just learn this on my own." Some learners think they do not need a course. They may be right — some people can
figure things out on their own. But most people benefit from structured, guided learning.
"I will never need to design anything." Some learners think they will avoid design in their daily life. In 2026, this is almost
impossible. Every business, church, school and event needs design.
"I am not creative enough." Some learners think design requires natural creativity. In reality, design is a skill that can be
learned through principles and practice.
Suggested Remedial Exercises
For learners who do not pass the capstone: Allow them to practise on academy machines during opening hours. Provide a
step-by-step guide they can follow. Let them retake the assessment when they are ready.
For learners who cannot create a logo: Provide simple logo templates and guide them through customisation.
For learners who cannot manage time: Provide a time allocation guide and check in with them during the project.
Differentiation Guidance
Level 1 — Supported (for learners who struggle):
Provide templates and examples for reference.
Offer one-on-one support during the project.
Allow them to retake the assessment after additional practice.
Level 2 — Independent (standard):
Complete the capstone project as written.
Answer all knowledge check questions.
Complete the reflection assignment.
Level 3 — Challenge (for learners who finish early):
Ask the learner to create additional brand materials (e.g., a letterhead, email signature, social media banner).
Ask the learner to research and write down three more design principles.
Ask the learner to help a struggling peer.
