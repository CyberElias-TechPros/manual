# Canva Interface

## A. Session Identification
<!-- kind:identification -->
Field Detail

Week 2
Field Detail

Duration 105 minutes
Difficulty Beginner
Prerequisite Knowledge Sessions 1–3 — Design Foundations

Preparation Canva accessible, account creation guide prepared

## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Create a Canva account using their email address.
2. Navigate the Canva dashboard and home screen.
3. Start a design from a template or from custom dimensions.
4. Identify the main parts of the Canva editor: toolbar, panels, canvas, and top bar.
5. Add and edit text using the text panel.
6. Add and edit images using the uploads and elements panels.
7. Save and organise designs in the Canva dashboard.
8. Explain why Canva is a suitable tool for beginner designers.

hierarchy, contrast, alignment, balance, white space, colour theory, typography, image selection and composition. You now

But knowing is not enough. You also need to be able to DO. Today, we start learning the tool you will use to create designs:

administrators, social media managers and professional designers. It is beginner-friendly but powerful enough for professional
work.
Watch this. [Display the design.] I created this in 5 minutes using Canva. By the end of this session, you will be able to create
designs like this too.
Let us begin."
Expected Learner Response: Excitement about finally using a tool. Curiosity about how Canva works.

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Instructor Script: "Before we start, let us remember the design principles we learned last week. Quick review."
Review Questions and Expected Answers:
1. "What are the six core design principles?"
Hierarchy, contrast, alignment, balance, white space, purpose/audience.
2. "What is the 60-30-10 rule?"
60% dominant colour, 30% secondary colour, 10% accent colour.
3. "What is the rule of thirds?"
Dividing an image into nine equal parts and placing key elements along the lines.
4. "What are the four font categories?"
Serif, sans-serif, script, display.
5. "What is the maximum number of fonts you should use in a design?"
Two.
How to Address Common Gaps:
If a learner cannot remember the principles, ask a partner to help.
If a learner forgot the rule of thirds, draw a quick 3x3 grid on the whiteboard.

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: What Is Canva? (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Canva is a free, web-based design tool. It runs in your web browser — you do not need to install anything.
It has a free tier that is powerful enough for most design work, and a paid tier (Canva Pro) with additional features.
Why Canva?
It is free (for the features we will use).
It is beginner-friendly — no design experience needed.
It has thousands of templates, images, fonts and elements.
It works on any computer with a web browser.
It is used by professionals worldwide.
Canva vs Other Tools:
Photoshop: Powerful but expensive and complex. Overkill for most beginner design work.
CorelDRAW: Professional vector design tool. Also complex and expensive.
Microsoft PowerPoint: Can be used for simple designs but is not designed for graphic design.
Canva: Free, beginner-friendly and powerful enough for most commercial design work.
When to Use Other Tools:
Use Photoshop for photo retouching and advanced image editing.
Use CorelDRAW for logo construction and large-format print.
Use Canva for flyers, social media posts, business cards, presentations and most everyday design work."
Topic 2: Creating a Canva Account (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Let us create a Canva account. Follow along on your own machines."
Step-by-Step Instructions:
1. "Open your web browser. Go to canva.com."
2. "Click 'Sign up' in the top-right corner."
3. "Choose 'Sign up with email' (or use Google/Facebook if you prefer)."
4. "Enter your email address."
5. "Create a password. Use a strong password — a mix of letters, numbers and symbols."
6. "Enter your name."
7. "Click 'Create account.'"
8. "Canva will ask what you want to use it for. Choose 'Personal' or 'Small business.'"
9. "Canva will ask about your experience. Choose 'Beginner' or 'I'm new to design.'"
10. "Canva will show you a tutorial. You can skip it for now — we will learn everything you need in this course."
11. "You are now on the Canva dashboard. This is your home screen."
Important Notes:
Your Canva account is free. You do not need to enter payment information.
If you already have a Canva account, sign in instead of creating a new one.
If you do not have an email address, ask the instructor for help.
Topic 3: The Canva Dashboard and Home Screen (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "The Canva dashboard is your home screen. Here is what you see:
Top Bar:
Canva logo: Click to return to the dashboard.
Search bar: Search for templates, images and elements.
Create a design button: Start a new design.
Templates button: Browse template categories.
Projects button: View your saved designs.
Share button: Share designs with others.
Profile icon: Access your account settings.
Main Area:
Recent designs: Your most recently edited designs.
Recommended templates: Templates suggested by Canva based on your usage.
Create a design: Quick-start options for common design types (social media post, presentation, poster, etc.).
Left Sidebar:
Home: Return to the dashboard.
Projects: All your saved designs.
Templates: Browse all templates.
Photos: Your uploaded photos.
Elements: Shapes, icons, illustrations and other design elements.
Text: Text styles and font pairings.
Uploads: Images and files you have uploaded.
Folders: Organise your designs into folders."
Topic 4: Starting a Design (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "There are two ways to start a design in Canva: from a template or from scratch.
Starting from a Template:
1. Click "Create a design" or "Templates."
2. Choose a design type (e.g., "Social Media Post," "Flyer," "Presentation").
3. Browse the template gallery.
4. Click on a template to open it in the editor.
5. Customise the template by changing text, colours, fonts and images.
Starting from Scratch (Custom Dimensions):
1. Click "Create a design."
2. Click "Custom size" (usually in the top-right corner).
3. Enter the width and height in pixels, inches or millimetres.
4. Click "Create new design."
5. A blank canvas appears with your custom dimensions.
Common Design Dimensions:
Instagram Post: 1080 x 1080 pixels (square)
Facebook Post: 1200 x 628 pixels
Flyer (A4): 2480 x 3508 pixels (at 300 DPI for print)
Business Card: 1050 x 600 pixels (standard 3.5 x 2 inches at 300 DPI)
Presentation (16:9): 1920 x 1080 pixels
Poster (A3): 3508 x 4961 pixels (at 300 DPI for print)
Best Practice: Always check the required dimensions before starting a design. If you are designing for print, use 300 DPI. If you
are designing for digital, 72 DPI is sufficient."
Topic 5: The Canva Editor Interface (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "The Canva editor is where you create and design. Here are the main parts:
Top Bar:
File menu: Save, download, share
Undo/Redo buttons: Reverse or repeat actions
Design name: Click to rename your design
Share button: Share your design with others
Download button: Export your design as an image or PDF
Left Sidebar:
Templates: Browse and apply templates
Elements: Shapes, icons, lines, frames, illustrations
Text: Add headings, subheadings and body text
Uploads: Upload and use your own images
Photos: Browse Canva's free image library
Background: Change the background colour or pattern
Folders: Organise your design elements
Canvas (Centre): This is where your design appears. You can click on any element to select and edit it. You can drag elements to
move them, resize them using the corner handles, and rotate them using the rotation handle.
Right Panel (Contextual): When you select an element, a panel appears on the right with editing options specific to that
element. For example, if you select text, you will see font, size, colour and alignment options."

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "Adaeze's Social Media Presence"
Situation: Adaeze runs a small bakery in Port Harcourt. She wants to promote her business on Instagram but does not know
how to create professional-looking posts. She takes photos with her phone and posts them directly, but they look amateur and
do not attract customers.
The Problem: Adaeze's Instagram posts look unprofessional because they lack design. There is no consistent colour scheme, no
clear hierarchy and no branding.
Why It Matters: Social media is one of the most powerful marketing tools for small businesses. A professional social media
presence can attract customers, build trust and increase sales. An amateur presence can drive customers away.
Relevant Concept: Canva skills. If Adaeze knew how to use Canva, she could create professional-looking Instagram posts in
minutes.
Worked Solution: If Adaeze had taken this course, here is what she would do:
1. Create a Canva account.
2. Set up a brand kit with her bakery's colours and fonts.
3. Create an Instagram post template with her logo, a consistent layout and her brand colours.
4. Each time she wants to post, she opens the template, changes the text and image, and downloads the design.
5. Her Instagram feed now looks professional and consistent.
Reasoning: Consistency is key in branding. When all your designs use the same colours, fonts and layout, people recognise your
brand instantly. Canva makes this easy with templates and brand kits.
Possible Mistake: Adaeze might create a different design for each post, with different colours and fonts. This looks inconsistent
and unprofessional.
Correction: Create a template and reuse it. Change only the text and image. Keep the colours, fonts and layout consistent.
Transfer Exercise: "Your friend Chinedu sells phone accessories. He posts on Instagram but his posts look messy. What advice
would you give him?"
Professional Connection: Every small business needs a social media presence. Social media posts are designs. The skills you
learn in this course will help you create professional-looking posts that attract customers.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Not Creating a Canva Account
Symptoms: The learner tries to use Canva without an account. Likely Cause: The learner does not know they need an account.
How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Do you have a Canva account?" Safe Corrective Steps: Guide the learner through the
account creation process. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner has a Canva account. How to Prevent
Recurrence: Make account creation a standard first step in every Canva session.
Mistake 2: Using the Wrong Dimensions
Symptoms: The learner creates a design with the wrong dimensions. Likely Cause: The learner does not know the standard
dimensions. How to Investigate: Check the design dimensions. Are they correct for the intended format? Safe Corrective
Steps: Teach the learner the standard dimensions for common design types. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The
learner uses correct dimensions. How to Prevent Recurrence: Provide a "Standard Design Dimensions" reference card.
Mistake 3: Not Saving Designs
Symptoms: The learner creates a design but does not save it. Likely Cause: The learner does not know that Canva auto-saves.

4. Explain in Your Own Words: Why is it important to use the correct dimensions for a design?
Answer: Using the correct dimensions ensures that the design fits the intended format. An Instagram post with the wrong

5. Practical Identification: Open Canva. Identify the left sidebar, the canvas, the top bar, and the Download button.

7. Error-Detecting: A learner writes in their assignment: "I created a Facebook post with dimensions 1080 x 1080 pixels." What is
wrong with this, and what should they have done instead?
