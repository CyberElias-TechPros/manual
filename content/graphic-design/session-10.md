## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
"The Brief"
Instructor Setup: Display a sample design brief on the projector.
Instructor Script: "Good morning, everyone. Over the past nine sessions, you have learned design principles, Canva skills and
real-world design. Today, we learn the professional workflow — how designers work with clients from the first meeting to the
final delivery.
A design brief is a document that tells the designer what the client needs. It includes the purpose, audience, requirements,
timeline and budget. A good brief prevents misunderstandings and ensures the designer creates what the client actually wants.
Today, you will learn how to work from a brief, manage revisions, organise files and deliver professional work. These are the
skills that separate a hobbyist from a professional."

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: Understanding Client Requirements (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "The first step in any design project is understanding what the client needs. This is done through a design
brief — either a formal document or a conversation.
Key Questions to Ask a Client:
1. What is the purpose of the design? What do you want it to achieve?
2. Who is the target audience? Who will see this design?
3. What information must be included? Text, images, logos, contact details.
4. What is the format? Print or digital? What size?
5. What is the timeline? When do you need it?
6. What is the budget? How much can you spend on design?
7. Are there any brand guidelines? Colours, fonts, logos, tone.
8. Who is the approver? Who has the final say on the design?
Why These Questions Matter: Without clear requirements, you risk creating something the client does not want. This wastes
time, money and goodwill. A good brief prevents this.
Topic 2: Working from a Design Brief (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Once you have the brief, you can start the design process. Here is a simple workflow:
Step 1: Research
Look at similar designs for inspiration.
Research the client's industry and audience.
Collect any brand assets (logos, colours, fonts).
Step 2: Plan
Sketch a rough layout on paper.
Decide on the hierarchy — what is most important?
Choose colours, fonts and images.
Step 3: Create
Build the design in Canva.
Apply the design principles you have learned.
Save multiple versions as you work.
Step 4: Review
Step back and look at the design with fresh eyes.
Check it against the brief — does it meet all the requirements?
Ask a friend or colleague for feedback.
Step 5: Revise
Make changes based on your review and feedback.
Save the revised version.
Step 6: Deliver
Export in the correct format.
Organise the files.
Send to the client with a brief explanation.
Topic 3: The Revision Workflow (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Revisions are a normal part of the design process. Clients rarely love the first version. Here is how to handle
revisions professionally.
The Revision Process:
1. Present the design. Explain your decisions and reasoning.
2. Receive feedback. Listen carefully and take notes.
3. Clarify. Ask questions if the feedback is unclear.
4. Evaluate. Consider each piece of feedback. Not all feedback is useful — use your judgement.
5. Revise. Make the changes that improve the design.
6. Present again. Show the revised design and explain what changed.
7. Repeat until the client is satisfied.
8. Finalise. Once approved, export the final files and deliver.
Revision Tips:
Limit revisions to 2–3 rounds. More than that suggests the brief was unclear.
Charge for additional revisions if you are working professionally.
Keep all versions so you can go back if needed.
Do not take feedback personally. It is about the work, not you.
Topic 4: File Organisation and Handover (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "When you deliver a design to a client, you should organise the files professionally.
What to Deliver:
1. Final exported files in the correct format (PNG, PDF, etc.).
2. Source files (Canva link or editable file) — if the client wants to make future edits.
3. A README file (optional) explaining what each file is and how to use it.
File Naming Convention: [Client Name] - [Design Type] - [Version] - [Date] Example: Adaeze-Fashion-Flyer-Final-08102026.pdf

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "The Client Who Knew What They Wanted"
Situation: A designer is hired to create a logo for a new business. The client has a clear vision — they want a simple, modern
logo in blue and white. The designer creates a complex, colourful logo because they think it looks better. The client is
disappointed and asks for a revision.
The Problem: The designer ignored the client's brief and imposed their own preferences.
Relevant Concept: Working from a brief. The designer should have followed the client's requirements, not their own
preferences.
Worked Solution: The designer learns to:
1. Listen to the client's requirements.
2. Create a design that meets the brief.
3. Present the design and explain how it meets the requirements.
4. Handle feedback professionally.
5. The client is satisfied and hires the designer for future work.
Transfer Exercise: "A client asks for a simple design but you think a complex design would look better. What do you do?"

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Not Reading the Brief Carefully
Symptoms: The designer creates something that does not meet the brief. Fix: Read the brief carefully and check your design
against it before presenting.
Mistake 2: Taking Feedback Personally
Symptoms: The designer gets defensive when receiving feedback. Fix: Remember — feedback is about the work, not you.
Mistake 3: Not Saving Versions
Symptoms: The designer overwrites previous versions and cannot go back. Fix: Save each version with a version number (v1, v2,
v3).
Mistake 4: Delivering Disorganised Files
Symptoms: The client receives files with random names in random folders. Fix: Use a clear file naming convention and organise
files in folders.
Mistake 5: Not Charging for Revisions
Symptoms: The designer does unlimited free revisions. Fix: Include 2–3 revision rounds in your price. Charge for additional
rounds.
