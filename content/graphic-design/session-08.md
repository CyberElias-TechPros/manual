## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Design a standard-size business card with essential information.
2. Design a poster using large-format design principles.
3. Design invitations for weddings, birthdays and church events.
4. Design certificates with proper layout and borders.
5. Create a simple logo using shapes, text and colour.
6. Apply print specifications (300 DPI, bleed, crop marks) correctly.
7. Explain the difference between print and digital design requirements.

## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
"The Business Card Test"
Instructor Setup: Display three business cards — one excellent, one average, one poor.
Instructor Script: "Good morning, everyone. Look at these three business cards. They are for the same type of business — a
hair salon. Which one would make you trust the salon? Which one would make you throw it away? [Pause for answers.]
The best business card has the salon name as the most important element. It has the essential information — name, service,
phone number, address. It uses clean fonts and a simple layout. It looks professional.
The worst business card has too much text, too many fonts, and the information is hard to find. It looks amateur.
Today, you will learn how to design business cards, posters, invitations, certificates and simple logos. These are the identity
materials that every business needs. Let us begin."

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
Review Questions and Expected Answers:
1. "What are the key elements of a business flyer?"
Business name, tagline/offer, key information, call to action, contact information.
2. "What are the standard dimensions for an Instagram post?"
1080 x 1080 pixels.
3. "How do you export a design for print?"
Download as PDF Print at 300 DPI.
4. "What is the 60-30-10 rule?"
60% dominant colour, 30% secondary colour, 10% accent colour.
5. "How do you create a simple logo in Canva?"
Use a shape, add text, group them together.

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: Business Card Design (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "A business card is a small card that contains your business's essential contact information. It is one of the
most important identity materials for any business.
Standard Business Card Size:
Nigeria/International: 3.5 x 2 inches (1050 x 600 pixels at 300 DPI)
Always check with your print shop for their preferred size.
Essential Information on a Business Card:
1. Business name (most important)
2. Your name and title
3. Phone number
4. Email address
5. Physical address (if applicable)
6. Website or social media handles
7. Logo (if available)
Business Card Design Principles:
1. Hierarchy: The business name is the largest. Your name and title are second. Contact information is smallest.
2. Simplicity: A business card is small. Do not try to fit too much information.
3. Readability: Use fonts that are readable at small sizes.
4. Brand Consistency: Use your brand colours and fonts.
5. White Space: Leave breathing room — do not fill every inch.
Common Business Card Mistakes:
Too much text — the card looks cluttered.
Too small fonts — hard to read.
Too many colours — looks unprofessional.
Missing essential information — no phone number or address.
Low-resolution design — prints blurry."
Topic 2: Poster Design (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "A poster is a large-format design intended to be viewed from a distance. Posters are used for events,
advertisements, announcements and decorations.
Poster Sizes:
A3: 11.7 x 16.5 inches (3508 x 4961 pixels at 300 DPI)
A2: 16.5 x 23.4 inches (4961 x 7016 pixels at 300 DPI)
Custom sizes are also common.
Poster Design Principles:
1. Large Text: Posters are viewed from a distance. Text must be large and bold.
2. Simple Message: One main message. Do not try to say everything.
3. Strong Visual: One strong image or graphic that grabs attention.
4. Clear Hierarchy: What you see from far away (headline) vs what you see up close (details).
5. Contrast: High contrast for readability at a distance.
Topic 3: Invitations and Certificates (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Invitations and certificates are common design needs for events, churches, schools and organisations.
Invitations: An invitation tells people about an event and encourages them to attend.
Key Elements of an Invitation:
1. Event name (e.g., "Wedding Ceremony," "Birthday Party," "Church Anniversary")
2. Date and time
3. Venue
4. Host name(s)
5. Dress code (if applicable)
6. RSVP information
Invitation Design Principles:
1. Tone: The design should match the tone of the event. A wedding invitation is elegant. A birthday invitation is fun. A church
invitation is formal.
2. Hierarchy: The event name is the most important. Date and time are second. Venue is third.
3. Readability: All text must be readable. Do not use decorative fonts for essential information.
Certificates: A certificate is a formal document that recognises an achievement or participation.
Key Elements of a Certificate:
1. Title (e.g., "Certificate of Achievement," "Certificate of Participation")
2. Recipient name (most important — usually the largest text)
3. Description of the achievement
4. Date
5. Signature lines
6. Organisation name and logo
Certificate Design Principles:
1. Formality: Certificates should look formal and official.
2. Border: A decorative border adds a sense of importance.
3. Hierarchy: The recipient's name is the most important element.
4. White Space: Leave plenty of white space for a clean, elegant look."
Topic 4: Simple Logo Design (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "A logo is a visual mark that represents a business, organisation or brand. It is the most important element of
brand identity.
Types of Logos:
1. Wordmark: Text only (e.g., Google, Coca-Cola). The business name is the logo.
2. Lettermark: Initials only (e.g., IBM, CNN). The first letters of the business name.
3. Icon/Symbol: An image or shape (e.g., Apple, Nike). A visual mark without text.
4. Combination: Text + icon (e.g., Burger King, Adidas). A wordmark with a symbol.
Simple Logo Design in Canva: You can create a simple, effective logo using Canva's shape and text tools.
Steps:
1. Choose a shape that represents the business (e.g., a circle for unity, a square for stability, a triangle for energy).
2. Add the business name or initials.
3. Choose one or two brand colours.
4. Use a clean, bold font.
5. Group the shape and text.
6. Export as PNG with transparent background (if available in free tier) or as a high-resolution PNG.
Logo Design Principles:
1. Simplicity: A simple logo is more memorable and more versatile.
2. Relevance: The logo should be relevant to the business.
3. Memorability: A good logo is easy to remember.
4. Versatility: A good logo works in different sizes and formats.
5. Timelessness: A good logo does not follow trends that will quickly become dated.

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "The Church Anniversary"
Situation: A church in Port Harcourt is celebrating its 25th anniversary. They need a banner, invitations, certificates and a
programme cover. A member volunteers to design them but has no design training. The designs look amateur and the pastor is
not satisfied.
The Problem: The volunteer does not understand print specifications, hierarchy or how to create formal designs.
Relevant Concept: Print and identity design. If the volunteer had taken this course, they would know how to design
professional print materials.
Worked Solution: The volunteer learns to:
1. Design a formal banner with large, readable text.
2. Design elegant invitations with the right tone.
3. Design certificates with proper borders and layout.
4. Export everything as PDF Print at 300 DPI.
5. The pastor is impressed and the anniversary is a success.
Transfer Exercise: "Your friend is organising a wedding. She needs invitations and a programme cover. What advice would you
give her?"

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Too Much Information on a Business Card
Symptoms: The business card is cluttered with text. Fix: Include only essential information — name, title, phone, email,
address.
Mistake 2: Fonts Too Small on Print Materials
Symptoms: Text is hard to read when printed. Fix: Use a minimum of 10pt for body text on print materials.
Mistake 3: Low-Resolution Images in Print Designs
Symptoms: Images are blurry when printed. Fix: Use high-resolution images (300 DPI) for print.
Mistake 4: Informal Tone for Formal Events
Symptoms: A wedding invitation looks like a party flyer. Fix: Match the tone to the event. Formal events need formal designs.
Mistake 5: Not Leaving White Space on Certificates
Symptoms: The certificate looks cluttered. Fix: Leave plenty of white space for an elegant, formal look.
