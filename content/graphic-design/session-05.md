## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Understand layers and how they work in Canva.
2. Use positioning and alignment tools to arrange elements precisely.
3. Group and ungroup elements for easier editing.
4. Apply transparency and effects to elements.
5. Crop and mask images using frames.
6. Set up a Canva brand kit with custom colours and fonts.
7. Apply brand colours and fonts consistently across designs.
8. Explain why brand consistency matters in professional design.

right uses different colours, fonts and layout for each post.

learn how to set up a brand kit in Canva and apply it consistently across all your designs. Let us begin."

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: Understanding Layers (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "In Canva, every element on your design — text, images, shapes, backgrounds — is a layer. Layers are
stacked on top of each other, like sheets of paper. The layer at the top of the stack is in front. The layer at the bottom is behind.
Why Layers Matter: Understanding layers helps you control which elements are in front and which are behind. For example, if
you want text to appear on top of an image, the text layer must be above the image layer.
How to Manage Layers in Canva:
1. Click on an element to select it.
2. Right-click and choose 'Layer' to see layer options:
'Bring to front': Move the element to the top of the stack.
'Bring forward': Move the element up one layer.
'Send to back': Move the element to the bottom of the stack.
'Send backward': Move the element down one layer.
Layer Tips:
The background is always the bottom layer.
Text should usually be above images and shapes.
If you cannot select an element, it may be behind another element. Use 'Send to back' on the front element to access it."
Topic 2: Positioning and Alignment (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Precise positioning and alignment are what separate a professional design from an amateur one. Canva has
tools to help you align elements perfectly.
Positioning Tools:
1. Select an element.
2. Right-click and choose 'Position' to see positioning options:
'Center': Centre the element on the canvas.
'Middle': Centre the element vertically.
'Top' / 'Bottom': Align to the top or bottom edge.
'Left' / 'Right': Align to the left or right edge.
Alignment Tools:
1. Select multiple elements (hold Shift and click).
2. Right-click and choose 'Align' to see alignment options:
'Align left': Align all selected elements to the left edge.
'Align centre': Align all selected elements to the centre.
'Align right': Align all selected elements to the right edge.
'Align top': Align all selected elements to the top edge.
'Align middle': Align all selected elements to the vertical centre.
'Align bottom': Align all selected elements to the bottom edge.
Spacing Tools:
1. Select multiple elements.
2. Right-click and choose 'Spacing' to distribute elements evenly:
'Distribute horizontally': Equal spacing between elements horizontally.
'Distribute vertically': Equal spacing between elements vertically.
Alignment Tips:
Use the alignment tools instead of eyeballing. Eyeballing is imprecise.
Use the spacing tools to create even gaps between elements.
Hold Shift when dragging to constrain movement to horizontal or vertical."
Topic 3: Grouping and Transparency (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Grouping and transparency are two powerful tools for managing complex designs.
Grouping: Grouping combines multiple elements into a single unit. This makes it easier to move, resize or edit them together.
1. Select multiple elements (hold Shift and click).
2. Right-click and choose 'Group' (or press Ctrl+G).
3. The elements are now grouped. You can move or resize them as one unit.
4. To ungroup, right-click and choose 'Ungroup' (or press Ctrl+Shift+G).
Transparency: Transparency makes an element see-through. This is useful for:
Creating overlay effects.
Making background images less dominant.
Creating watermark effects.
1. Select an element.
2. Click the transparency button (checkerboard icon) in the top toolbar.
3. Drag the slider to adjust the transparency (0% = fully opaque, 100% = fully transparent).
Grouping and Transparency Tips:
Group elements that belong together (e.g., a logo and a tagline).
Use transparency to create subtle background effects.
Do not make text too transparent — it must remain readable."
Topic 4: Brand Setup (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "A brand kit is a collection of your brand's colours, fonts and logos. Setting up a brand kit in Canva ensures
that all your designs are consistent.
Setting Up Brand Colours:
1. Go to your Canva dashboard.
2. Click 'Brand Kit' in the left sidebar (or 'Brand' in the top bar).
3. Click 'Add a brand colour.'
4. Enter the hex code or use the colour picker to choose your brand colour.
5. Repeat for all your brand colours.
Setting Up Brand Fonts:
1. In the Brand Kit, click 'Add a brand font.'
2. Choose a heading font and a body font from the font dropdowns.
3. These fonts will now be available in all your designs under 'Brand fonts.'
Using Your Brand Kit:
1. Open any design.
2. Click on an element (e.g., text or shape).
3. In the colour picker, you will see your brand colours under 'Brand colours.'
4. In the font dropdown, you will see your brand fonts under 'Brand fonts.'
Best Practice:
Choose 2–3 brand colours (one dominant, one secondary, one accent).
Choose 2 brand fonts (one for headings, one for body text).
Use your brand colours and fonts in every design."

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "The Business That Built a Brand"
Situation: Adaeze runs a small fashion design business in Port Harcourt. She has been using Canva for a few months but her
designs look inconsistent. Each flyer, social media post and business card uses different colours and fonts. Customers do not
recognise her brand.
The Problem: Adaeze's designs lack brand consistency. She does not have a brand kit set up. She chooses colours and fonts
randomly for each design.
Why It Matters: Brand consistency builds recognition and trust. When customers see consistent colours, fonts and layouts,
they instantly recognise the brand. Inconsistent branding looks unprofessional and confuses customers.
Relevant Concept: Brand setup. If Adaeze had set up a brand kit and used it consistently, her designs would look professional
and recognisable.
Worked Solution: If Adaeze had taken this course, here is what she would do:
1. Choose 2–3 brand colours that represent her fashion brand.
2. Choose 2 brand fonts — one for headings, one for body text.
3. Set up a brand kit in Canva.
4. Create a reusable template for social media posts.
5. Use the same colours, fonts and layout for every design.
6. Her brand is now instantly recognisable.
Reasoning: Brand consistency is not about being boring. It is about being recognisable. When people see your colours and
fonts, they should think of your business — even before they read the text.
Possible Mistake: Adaeze might think that using the same colours and fonts for every design is boring. She might want to try
something new each time. But consistency is more important than variety in branding.
Correction: You can vary the images and text, but keep the colours, fonts and layout consistent. That is how professional brands
work.
Transfer Exercise: "Your friend Chinedu sells phone accessories. He uses different colours and fonts for every Instagram post.
What advice would you give him?"
Professional Connection: Every business, no matter how small, needs consistent branding. The skills you learn in this session
will help you build a recognisable brand for any business.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Not Setting Up a Brand Kit
Symptoms: The learner creates designs without setting up a brand kit. Likely Cause: The learner does not know about brand kits
or does not understand their importance. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Do you have a brand kit set up?" Safe
Corrective Steps: Guide the learner through the brand kit setup process. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The
learner has a brand kit set up. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make brand kit setup a standard step in every Canva project.
Mistake 2: Not Using Alignment Tools
Symptoms: The learner eyeballs the positioning of elements instead of using alignment tools. Likely Cause: The learner does
not know about the alignment tools. How to Investigate: Look at the design. Are the elements precisely aligned? Safe
Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to use the alignment and spacing tools. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The
learner uses alignment tools. How to Prevent Recurrence: Remind the learner: "Use the tools, not your eyes."
Mistake 3: Not Grouping Related Elements
Symptoms: The learner moves elements individually instead of grouping them. Likely Cause: The learner does not know about
grouping. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Did you group related elements?" Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to
group related elements. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner groups related elements. How to Prevent
Recurrence: Remind the learner: "Group elements that belong together."
Mistake 4: Making Text Too Transparent
Symptoms: The learner makes text too transparent, making it hard to read. Likely Cause: The learner uses transparency for
aesthetic effect without considering readability. How to Investigate: Can you read the text easily? Safe Corrective Steps:
Teach the learner to keep text at 100% opacity (or close to it) for readability. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The
learner keeps text readable. How to Prevent Recurrence: Remind the learner: "Text must always be readable."
Mistake 5: Using Too Many Brand Colours
Symptoms: The learner chooses 5 or more brand colours. Likely Cause: The learner thinks more colours make the brand more
interesting. How to Investigate: Count the brand colours. Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to use 2–3 brand colours
maximum. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner uses 2–3 brand colours. How to Prevent Recurrence:
Remind the learner: "2–3 colours maximum."

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
Questions
1. Multiple Choice: What are layers in Canva? a) Different pages in a design b) Stacked elements on top of each other c)
Different colour schemes d) Different font categories
Answer: b) Stacked elements on top of each other
2. Short Answer: What is a brand kit and why is it important?
Answer: A brand kit is a collection of your brand's colours, fonts and logos. It is important because it ensures consistency across
all your designs, which builds brand recognition and trust.
3. Multiple Choice: How do you group elements in Canva? a) Right-click and choose 'Group' b) Press Ctrl+G c) Both a and b d)
You cannot group elements in Canva
Answer: c) Both a and b
4. Explain in Your Own Words: Why should you use alignment tools instead of eyeballing?
Answer: Eyeballing is imprecise. Alignment tools ensure that elements are perfectly aligned, which makes the design look
professional. Small misalignments are noticeable and make a design look amateur.
5. Practical Identification: Open Canva. Identify the Brand Kit, the alignment tools, and the grouping option.
Answer: The learner correctly identifies all three elements.
6. Scenario-Based: Your friend says, "I set up a brand kit but I still choose different colours for each design. What is the point of
having a brand kit?" What advice would you give?
Answer: The point of a brand kit is to ensure consistency. If you choose different colours for each design, you are not using
your brand kit. Always use your brand colours and fonts — that is how you build a recognisable brand.
7. Error-Detecting: A learner writes in their assignment: "I made the text 50% transparent because it looked too bold." What is
wrong with this, and what should they have done instead?
Answer: Making text 50% transparent makes it hard to read. The learner should have kept the text at 100% opacity and instead
reduced the font size or changed the font weight to make it less bold.

Common Misconceptions
"Layers are too complicated." Some learners think layers are an advanced concept. Correct this by showing them how
simple it is to bring elements forward or send them back.
"Eyeballing is good enough." Some learners think they can align elements by eye. Correct this by showing them the
difference between eyeballed and tool-aligned designs.
"Brand kits are only for big companies." Some learners think brand kits are only for large businesses. Correct this by
explaining that even small businesses and individuals need consistent branding.
Suggested Remedial Exercises
For learners who cannot manage layers: Have them practise with a simple design (background, image, text) and change the
layer order multiple times.
For learners who do not use alignment tools: Have them create a design with multiple elements and align them using only
the tools (no eyeballing).
For learners who do not set up a brand kit: Have them set up a brand kit for a fictional business and create three designs
using the same brand kit.
