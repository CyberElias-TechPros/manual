## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Apply effects such as shadows, outlines and curves to text and elements.
2. Create simple shapes and logos using Canva's shape tools.
3. Use grids and guides for precise layout.
4. Resize designs for different formats using Canva's resize feature.
5. Export designs in the correct format for print and digital.
6. Explain the difference between print (CMYK, 300 DPI) and digital (RGB, 72 DPI) specifications.
7. Choose the right export format for different use cases.

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: Effects — Shadows, Outlines and Curves (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Effects add depth and visual interest to your designs. Canva has several effects you can apply to text and
elements.
Shadow: A shadow creates a drop shadow behind an element, making it stand out from the background.
1. Select an element.
2. Click 'Effects' in the top toolbar.
3. Choose 'Shadow.'
4. Adjust the shadow's direction, blur and transparency.
Outline: An outline adds a border around text or shapes.
1. Select an element.
2. Click 'Effects' in the top toolbar.
3. Choose 'Outline.'
4. Adjust the outline's thickness and colour.
Curve: The curve effect bends text into an arc. This is useful for logos and decorative text.
1. Select a text element.
2. Click 'Effects' in the top toolbar.
3. Choose 'Curve.'
4. Adjust the curve amount using the slider.
Best Practice:
Use effects sparingly. Too many effects make a design look cluttered.
Shadows work well for call-to-action buttons and headings.
Outlines work well for text on busy backgrounds.
Curves work well for logos and decorative text.
Topic 2: Creating Simple Shapes and Logos (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Canva's shape tools allow you to create simple logos and graphic elements. You do not need to be an
illustrator to create a clean, professional logo.
Basic Shapes:
1. Go to the left sidebar and click 'Elements.'
2. Click 'Shapes.'
3. Choose a shape (e.g., circle, square, triangle).
4. Drag the shape onto the canvas.
5. Resize, rotate and colour the shape.
Combining Shapes: You can combine multiple shapes to create more complex designs.
1. Add multiple shapes to the canvas.
2. Position them to create your design.
3. Group them together (Ctrl+G).
4. Apply effects (shadow, outline) if needed.
Simple Logo Design: A simple logo can be created using:
A shape (e.g., a circle or square)
Text (the business name)
A brand colour
Example:
1. Add a circle shape.
2. Change the fill colour to your brand colour.
3. Add text in the centre of the circle.
4. Choose a clean, bold font.
5. Group the shape and text.
6. You now have a simple logo.
Best Practice:
Keep logos simple. A simple logo is more memorable and more versatile.
Use a maximum of 2 colours and 1 font in a logo.
Make sure the logo works in black and white as well as colour.
Topic 3: Resizing for Different Formats (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Sometimes you need to create the same design in different sizes. For example, you might want to use the
same design for an Instagram post, a Facebook post and a flyer. Canva's resize feature makes this easy.
How to Resize a Design:
1. Open the design you want to resize.
2. Click 'Resize' in the top bar (or 'File' > 'Resize').
3. Enter the new dimensions or choose a preset.
4. Click 'Resize.'
5. Canva creates a copy of your design with the new dimensions.
6. Adjust the layout if needed (elements may need to be repositioned).
Important Note: Resizing does not automatically adjust the layout. You may need to reposition elements after resizing. Always
check the resized design before downloading.
Topic 4: Export Formats — Print vs Digital (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Choosing the right export format is essential. The wrong format can result in blurry prints or huge file sizes.
Digital Export (for screen):
PNG: Best for social media posts, web graphics and designs with transparent backgrounds. High quality, large file size.
JPG: Best for photographs and designs without transparent backgrounds. Smaller file size, slightly lower quality.
Resolution: 72 DPI is sufficient for screen.
Print Export (for paper):
PDF (Standard): Best for flyers, business cards and documents. Preserves quality and fonts.
PDF (Print): Best for professional printing. Includes crop marks and bleed.
Resolution: 300 DPI is required for print.
RGB vs CMYK:
RGB: Red, Green, Blue. Used for digital displays (screens).
CMYK: Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black. Used for print.
Canva uses RGB by default. For professional printing, you may need to convert to CMYK using another tool. For most small
print jobs (e.g., printing at a local print shop), RGB is acceptable.
Export Checklist:
1. Is the design for digital or print?
2. Choose the correct format (PNG/JPG for digital, PDF for print).
3. Check the resolution (72 DPI for digital, 300 DPI for print).
4. Check the dimensions (correct size for the intended format).
5. Download and verify the file.

## I. INDEPENDENT CLASS EXERCISE (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:exercise -->
Exercise: "Export Format Quiz"
Scenario: "You are a designer. Your client asks you to prepare designs for different uses. For each scenario, choose the correct
export format and explain why."
Task: For each scenario, write down:
1. The correct export format (PNG, JPG, PDF Standard, PDF Print)
2. The correct resolution (72 DPI or 300 DPI)
3. Why you chose that format
Scenarios:
1. An Instagram post for a clothing brand.
2. A flyer for a church event (to be printed at a local print shop).
3. A business card (to be printed professionally).
4. A website banner for a tech startup.
5. A photograph to be shared on WhatsApp.
Requirements:
Choose the correct format for each scenario
Explain your choice
Time Allowance: 10 minutes
Success Criteria:
All 5 scenarios correctly answered
Explanations are clear and accurate
Hints Available If the Learner Gets Stuck:
Hint 1: "For digital use, choose PNG or JPG. For print, choose PDF."
Hint 2: "For social media, PNG is best. For photographs, JPG is fine."
Hint 3: "For professional printing, always use PDF Print at 300 DPI."
Complete Answer Key for the Instructor:
1. Instagram post: PNG, 72 DPI. PNG is best for social media because it supports high quality and transparent backgrounds.
2. Flyer for print: PDF Print, 300 DPI. PDF preserves quality and fonts for print. 300 DPI ensures crisp printing.
3. Business card: PDF Print, 300 DPI. Professional printing requires high resolution and PDF format.
4. Website banner: PNG or JPG, 72 DPI. Digital displays only need 72 DPI. PNG if transparency is needed, JPG if not.
5. WhatsApp photograph: JPG, 72 DPI. JPG is best for photographs because it has a smaller file size.

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "The Blurry Business Card"
Situation: A small business owner in Port Harcourt designs a business card in Canva. She exports it as a low-resolution PNG and
takes it to a print shop. The printed business cards are blurry and pixelated. The print shop tells her she needs to provide a high-
resolution PDF.
The Problem: The business owner exported the design in the wrong format and at the wrong resolution. She did not
understand the difference between digital and print specifications.
Why It Matters: Exporting in the wrong format can ruin an otherwise good design. A blurry business card looks unprofessional
and can cost you customers.
Relevant Concept: Export formats. If the business owner had known to export as PDF Print at 300 DPI, the business cards would
have been crisp and professional.
Worked Solution: If the business owner had taken this course, here is what she would do:
1. Design the business card in Canva with the correct dimensions (1050 x 600 pixels for a standard 3.5 x 2 inch card at 300 DPI).
2. Export as PDF Print.
3. Take the PDF to the print shop.
4. The printed business cards are crisp and professional.
Reasoning: Print and digital have different requirements. Digital designs are viewed on screens, which have lower resolution.
Print designs are viewed on paper, which requires higher resolution. Using the wrong format is like wearing shoes on the wrong
feet — it might work, but it will not look right.
Possible Mistake: The business owner might think that PNG is always the best format because it is high quality. But PNG is
designed for screens, not print. For print, PDF is the correct format.
Correction: Always ask yourself: "Is this design for screen or paper?" If screen, use PNG or JPG at 72 DPI. If paper, use PDF at 300
DPI.
Transfer Exercise: "Your friend designed a flyer in Canva and exported it as PNG. He wants to print 100 copies. What advice
would you give him?"
Professional Connection: Every design you create will eventually be exported — for screen or for paper. Knowing the correct
format and resolution is essential for professional-quality work.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Using Too Many Effects
Symptoms: The learner applies multiple effects to every element. Likely Cause: The learner thinks more effects make the design
more interesting. How to Investigate: Look at the design. Are there effects on every element? Safe Corrective Steps: Teach
the learner to use effects sparingly — one or two effects per design. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner
uses effects sparingly. How to Prevent Recurrence: Remind the learner: "Less is more."
Mistake 2: Exporting in the Wrong Format
Symptoms: The learner exports a print design as PNG or a digital design as PDF. Likely Cause: The learner does not understand
the difference between print and digital formats. How to Investigate: Ask the learner, "Is this design for screen or paper?" Safe
Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to always ask "screen or paper?" before exporting. How to Confirm the Problem Is
Resolved: The learner chooses the correct format. How to Prevent Recurrence: Provide an "Export Formats Reference" card.
Mistake 3: Not Checking Resolution
Symptoms: The learner exports a design without checking the resolution. Likely Cause: The learner does not understand DPI.
How to Investigate: Check the exported file's resolution. Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to use 72 DPI for digital and
300 DPI for print. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner uses the correct resolution. How to Prevent
Recurrence: Remind the learner: "72 for screen, 300 for print."
Mistake 4: Not Adjusting Layout After Resizing
Symptoms: The learner resizes a design but does not adjust the layout. Likely Cause: The learner thinks resizing automatically
adjusts the layout. How to Investigate: Look at the resized design. Are elements out of place? Safe Corrective Steps: Teach
the learner to always check and adjust the layout after resizing. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner adjusts
the layout after resizing. How to Prevent Recurrence: Remind the learner: "Always check after resizing."
Mistake 5: Making Logos Too Complex
Symptoms: The learner creates a logo with too many shapes, colours and fonts. Likely Cause: The learner thinks a complex logo
looks more professional. How to Investigate: Look at the logo. How many shapes, colours and fonts are there? Safe
Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to keep logos simple — one shape, one or two colours, one font. How to Confirm the
Problem Is Resolved: The learner creates simple logos. How to Prevent Recurrence: Remind the learner: "Simple is
memorable."

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
Questions
1. Multiple Choice: What is the best export format for a design that will be printed? a) PNG b) JPG c) PDF d) GIF
Answer: c) PDF
2. Short Answer: What is the difference between RGB and CMYK?
Answer: RGB (Red, Green, Blue) is used for digital displays (screens). CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Black) is used for print.
3. Multiple Choice: What resolution should you use for print designs? a) 72 DPI b) 150 DPI c) 300 DPI d) 600 DPI
Answer: c) 300 DPI
4. Explain in Your Own Words: Why should you keep logos simple?
Answer: Simple logos are more memorable, more versatile and easier to reproduce across different media. A complex logo may
look good on a large screen but become unreadable on a small business card.
5. Practical Identification: Open Canva. Identify the Effects button, the Resize option, and the Download button.
Answer: The learner correctly identifies all three elements.
6. Scenario-Based: Your friend says, "I exported my flyer as PNG because PNG is high quality." The flyer is going to be printed.
What advice would you give?
Answer: PNG is designed for screens, not print. For print, you should export as PDF Print at 300 DPI. This ensures crisp,
professional-quality printing.
7. Error-Detecting: A learner writes in their assignment: "I resized my design from Instagram post to flyer but I did not check
the layout." What is wrong with this, and what should they have done?
Answer: Resizing does not automatically adjust the layout. The learner should have checked the resized design and repositioned
any elements that were out of place.
