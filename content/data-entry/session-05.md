## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Explain what reconciliation is and why it matters.
2. Compare dataset totals against source document totals.
3. Identify and resolve discrepancies during reconciliation.
4. Create a professional data entry report documenting all processes.
5. Apply professional documentation standards to data entry work.

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: What Is Reconciliation? (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Reconciliation is the process of comparing two sets of records to ensure they match. In data entry, this
means comparing your dataset totals against source document totals — bank statements, invoices, receipts.
Why Reconciliation Matters:
Catches errors that validation missed
Ensures financial accuracy
Builds trust in the dataset
Required by auditors and accountants"
Topic 2: The Reconciliation Process (15 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Here is the systematic process for reconciling data:
Step 1: Calculate Dataset Totals
Sum all monetary columns in the dataset.
Count the total number of records.
Step 2: Calculate Source Document Totals
Sum all amounts on the source documents (bank statement, invoices).
Count the total number of source documents.
Step 3: Compare
Subtract the dataset total from the source total.
If the difference is zero — reconciled.
If the difference is not zero — investigate.
Step 4: Investigate Discrepancies
Check for missing records (in source but not dataset).
Check for extra records (in dataset but not source).
Check for amount differences (same record, different amount).
Check for duplicates.
Step 5: Resolve and Document
Correct all discrepancies.
Document every finding and resolution."
Topic 3: Creating a Professional Data Entry Report (15 minutes)
Instructor Script: "A data entry report documents everything you did to the dataset — entry, cleaning, validation and
reconciliation.
Report Structure:
1. Header: Dataset name, date, preparer name, department.
2. Executive Summary: Brief overview of what was done and the final result.
3. Data Entry Summary: How many records entered, from what source, using what tools.
4. Cleaning Summary: What errors were found and fixed during cleaning.
5. Validation Summary: What discrepancies were found during validation.
6. Reconciliation Summary: Dataset total vs source total, differences found, how resolved.
7. Final Result: Confirmation that the dataset is clean, validated and reconciled.
8. Sign-off: Preparer name, date, reviewer name, date."

## G. INSTRUCTOR DEMONSTRATION (15 minutes)
<!-- kind:demo -->
Demonstration: Reconciling a Dataset (15 minutes)
What the Instructor Prepares:
A dataset and source documents with a ₦50,000 difference
What the Instructor Says and Does:
1. "I calculate the dataset total: =SUM(F2:F21) = ₦2,450,000."
2. "I calculate the source total from the bank statement: ₦2,500,000."
3. "The difference is ₦50,000. I need to find it."
4. "I check for missing records — all 20 records are in both."
5. "I check for duplicates — none found."
6. "I compare each record individually. Record #15: dataset shows ₦100,000 but the invoice shows ₦150,000. Found it!"
7. "I correct the amount. The new total is ₦2,500,000."
8. "The dataset now matches the source. Reconciled."
9. "I create a reconciliation report documenting the process."

## I. INDEPENDENT CLASS EXERCISE (10 minutes)
<!-- kind:exercise -->
Exercise: "Reconciliation Scenarios"
Answer the following:
1. Dataset total: ₦1,000,000. Source total: ₦1,000,000. What is the result?
2. Dataset total: ₦500,000. Source total: ₦550,000. What do you do?
3. You find a record in the source that is not in the dataset. What type of error is this?
4. What is the final step in the reconciliation process?
5. What should a reconciliation report include?
Time Allowance: 10 minutes
Complete Answer Key:
1. Reconciled — no further action needed.
2. Investigate the ₦50,000 difference — check for missing records, amount differences or duplicates.
3. Missing record — add it to the dataset.
4. Document the reconciliation process and create a report.
5. Executive summary, data entry summary, cleaning summary, validation summary, reconciliation summary, final result and
sign-off.

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "The Audit"
Situation: An external auditor arrives at a company to review their financial records. The auditor asks for the sales dataset and
the data entry documentation. The administrator provides a clean dataset with a full report — entry log, cleaning log, validation
report and reconciliation report. The auditor is impressed and completes the audit quickly with no issues.
The Problem: Without documentation, the auditor would have to re-check every record, taking weeks instead of days.
Relevant Concept: Documentation. Professional documentation saves time, builds trust and satisfies auditors.
Worked Solution: The administrator creates a complete data entry report for every dataset. The audit is smooth and the
company passes with no issues.
Transfer Exercise: "Why do auditors need documentation of data entry processes?"

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Not Calculating Totals Accurately
Fix: Use Excel formulas (=SUM) rather than manual addition.
Mistake 2: Stopping at the First Discrepancy
Fix: Continue investigating until ALL discrepancies are resolved.
Mistake 3: Not Documenting the Reconciliation Process
Fix: Always create a reconciliation report.
Mistake 4: Reconciling Without Source Documents
Fix: Never reconcile without the original source documents.
Mistake 5: Not Getting a Second Person to Review
Fix: Have someone else review your reconciliation before finalising.

<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
1. What is reconciliation? Answer: The process of comparing two sets of records to ensure they match.
2. What is the first step in the reconciliation process? Answer: Calculate the dataset totals.
3. What should you do if the dataset total does not match the source total? Answer: Investigate — check for missing records,
extra records, amount differences and duplicates.
4. What should a data entry report include? Answer: Executive summary, data entry summary, cleaning summary, validation
summary, reconciliation summary, final result and sign-off.
5. Why is documentation important? Answer: It creates a record that others can follow, verify and audit.
