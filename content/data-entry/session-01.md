## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Define data entry and explain its importance in business.
2. Identify the four main data types: text, numbers, dates and currency.
3. Distinguish between data entry and data management.
4. Explain professional standards for accuracy, consistency and confidentiality.
5. Measure their typing speed in words per minute (WPM) and accuracy percentage.
6. Explain why attention to detail matters in data entry.

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (35 minutes)
<!-- kind:lecture -->
Topic 1: What Is Data Entry? (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Data entry is the process of inputting information into a computer system. It can be typing text, numbers,
dates or any other data into a spreadsheet, database or other software.
Where Is Data Entry Used?
Banks — entering customer transactions
Hospitals — entering patient records
Schools — entering student grades
Offices — entering sales records, inventory, customer information
Government — entering census data, tax records
E-commerce — entering product details, orders
Why Does Data Entry Matter? Data is the foundation of every business decision. If the data is wrong, the decisions based on
that data will be wrong. Accurate data entry ensures that businesses have reliable information to work with."
Topic 2: Types of Data (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "In data entry, you will work with four main types of data. Each type has its own rules and formatting
requirements.
1. Text Data: Any combination of letters, numbers and symbols. Examples: names, addresses, descriptions.
Always check spelling carefully.
Be consistent with capitalisation (e.g., always use Title Case for names).
2. Numerical Data: Numbers used for calculations. Examples: quantities, prices, ages.
Do not include currency symbols in numerical data (use formatting instead).
Use the correct number of decimal places.
3. Date Data: Dates in a consistent format. Examples: 01/10/2026, 15/11/2026.
Always use the same date format throughout the dataset.
In Nigeria, the standard format is DD/MM/YYYY (day/month/year).
4. Currency Data: Monetary values. Examples: ₦15,000, ₦2,500.50.
Always use the correct currency symbol.
Use commas as thousand separators.
Use the correct number of decimal places (usually 2 for kobo)."
Topic 3: Data Entry vs Data Management (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Data entry and data management are related but different.
Data Entry:
Inputting data into a system
Focused on accuracy and speed
Following established formats and rules
Data Management:
Organising, storing and maintaining data
Includes data entry but also includes cleaning, validation, analysis and reporting
Focused on data quality and usability
In this course, you will learn both — starting with data entry and moving into data management."
Topic 4: Professional Standards (10 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Professional data entry requires:
1. Accuracy: Every piece of data must be correct. A single error can have serious consequences. Always double-check your
work.
2. Consistency: Use the same format for all similar data. If you write dates as DD/MM/YYYY, use that format for every date. If
you write names in Title Case, use Title Case for every name.
3. Confidentiality: Data often contains sensitive information — customer names, phone numbers, financial records. Never share
data with unauthorised people. Never leave data visible on your screen when you step away.
4. Efficiency: Accuracy is essential, but speed also matters. Learn keyboard shortcuts. Develop a rhythm. With practice, you can
be both fast and accurate.
5. Documentation: When you clean or modify data, document what you changed and why. This creates a record that others
can follow."
Topic 5: Typing Accuracy (5 minutes)
Instructor Script: "Typing speed and accuracy are the foundation of data entry. You cannot be a good data entry professional if
you type slowly or make many errors.
What Is a Good Typing Speed?
Minimum for data entry: 25 WPM (words per minute)
Good: 35–45 WPM
Excellent: 50+ WPM
What Is a Good Accuracy?
Minimum for data entry: 95%
Good: 98%
Excellent: 99–100%
How to Improve:
Practise for 15 minutes every day.
Use a typing practice website.
Focus on accuracy first — speed will follow.
Do not look at the keyboard."

## J. REAL-WORLD CASE STUDY (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:case_study -->
Case: "The Wrong Phone Number"
Situation: A receptionist at a hotel in Port Harcourt enters a guest's phone number into the booking system. She is in a hurry
and types one digit wrong. The hotel tries to call the guest to confirm the booking. The call goes to a stranger. The guest arrives
at the hotel but has no reservation. The guest is furious and leaves a bad review online.
The Problem: A single wrong digit in a phone number caused the hotel to lose a customer and receive a bad review.
Why It Matters: In data entry, accuracy is not optional — it is essential. Every piece of data you enter has consequences.
Relevant Concept: Professional standards. If the receptionist had double-checked the phone number, the problem would not
have occurred.
Worked Solution: The receptionist learns to always double-check phone numbers before saving. She develops a habit of
reading the number back to the guest to confirm. The hotel also implements a verification step in their booking process.
Reasoning: Accuracy habits — double-checking, reading back, verifying — prevent costly errors.
Possible Mistake: The receptionist might think that one wrong digit is not a big deal. In reality, in data entry, there is no such
thing as a small error.
Correction: Every error matters. Develop the habit of double-checking every piece of data you enter.
Transfer Exercise: "You are entering customer data and you are not sure if a phone number is correct. What do you do?"
Professional Connection: Every office job that involves data requires accuracy. Receptionists, administrators, data entry clerks,
accountants — everyone. The habits you learn in this course will serve you in any data-related role.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Mistake 1: Inconsistent Formatting
Symptoms: Dates, names and phone numbers are formatted differently throughout the dataset. Likely Cause: The learner does
not follow a consistent format. How to Investigate: Look at the dataset. Are all dates in the same format? Are all names in the
same case? Safe Corrective Steps: Establish a format standard and apply it consistently. How to Confirm the Problem Is
Resolved: All data follows the same format. How to Prevent Recurrence: Create a format guide and refer to it while entering
data.
Mistake 2: Typing Currency Symbols Manually
Symptoms: Currency values are stored as text, not numbers. Likely Cause: The learner types the ₦ symbol before the number.
How to Investigate: Click on a currency cell. Is it left-aligned (text) or right-aligned (number)? Safe Corrective Steps: Type the
number only and apply currency formatting. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: Currency values are stored as numbers
with currency formatting. How to Prevent Recurrence: Always type numbers first, then apply formatting.
Mistake 3: Not Double-Checking Work
Symptoms: Errors are found after the data is submitted. Likely Cause: The learner does not verify their work. How to
Investigate: Ask the learner, "Did you double-check your work?" Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to always read back
what they typed and compare it to the source. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner double-checks their
work. How to Prevent Recurrence: Make double-checking a habit — every time, without exception.
Mistake 4: Looking at the Keyboard
Symptoms: The learner types slowly and makes many errors. Likely Cause: The learner has not memorised the keyboard layout.
How to Investigate: Watch the learner type. Are they looking down? Safe Corrective Steps: Encourage the learner to practise
typing without looking at the keyboard. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner types without looking down.
How to Prevent Recurrence: Daily typing practice for 15 minutes.
Mistake 5: Not Saving Work Regularly
Symptoms: The learner loses work when the computer crashes. Likely Cause: The learner does not save regularly. How to
Investigate: Ask the learner, "When did you last save?" Safe Corrective Steps: Teach the learner to press Ctrl+S every few
minutes. How to Confirm the Problem Is Resolved: The learner saves regularly. How to Prevent Recurrence: Set a timer that
goes off every 5 minutes. When it rings, everyone presses Ctrl+S.

## M. KNOWLEDGE CHECK (5 minutes)
<!-- kind:knowledge_check -->
Questions
1. Multiple Choice: What is data entry? a) The process of analysing data b) The process of inputting information into a
computer system c) The process of creating databases d) The process of writing code
Answer: b) The process of inputting information into a computer system
2. Short Answer: What are the four main data types?
Answer: Text, numbers, dates and currency.
3. Multiple Choice: What is the standard date format in Nigeria? a) MM/DD/YYYY b) DD/MM/YYYY c) YYYY/MM/DD d) DD-
MM-YYYY
Answer: b) DD/MM/YYYY
4. Explain in Your Own Words: Why is it important to format currency values as numbers rather than typing the ₦ symbol
manually?
Answer: Typing the ₦ symbol manually stores the value as text, not a number. Text values cannot be used in calculations. By
typing the number and applying currency formatting, the value remains a number that can be calculated.
5. Practical Identification: Open Excel. Enter the following data with correct formatting:
A name in Title Case
A phone number (digits only)
A date in DD/MM/YYYY format
A price formatted as ₦ currency
Answer: The learner correctly enters all four data types with proper formatting.
6. Scenario-Based: Your friend says, "I typed ₦15,000 into Excel but I cannot add it to other numbers. What did I do wrong?"
What advice would you give?
Answer: You probably typed the ₦ symbol manually, which stores the value as text. Delete the cell, type just the number 15000,
and then apply currency formatting from the Home tab.
7. Error-Detecting: A learner writes in their assignment: "The four data types are text, numbers, dates and percentages." What is
wrong with this, and what should they write instead?
Answer: Percentages are not a separate data type — they are a type of numerical data. The four data types are text, numbers,
dates and currency.

Common Misconceptions
"Data entry is just typing." Some learners think data entry is simply typing fast. Correct this by explaining that accuracy,
consistency and understanding data types are equally important.
"Formatting does not matter." Some learners think that as long as the data is there, formatting is optional. Correct this by
showing how inconsistent formatting makes data hard to use.
"I can type fast enough." Some learners overestimate their typing speed. Correct this by measuring their WPM and
showing them the data entry standard.
Suggested Remedial Exercises
For learners who cannot identify data types: Provide additional examples and have them classify data into the four types.
For learners who format currency incorrectly: Have them practise entering currency values and applying formatting
multiple times.
For learners with slow typing speed: Provide a daily typing practice plan and measure their progress weekly.
Differentiation Guidance
Level 1 — Supported (for learners who struggle):
Provide a "Data Types Reference" handout for reference.
Pair with a more confident learner for practical exercises.
Allow extra time for the independent exercise.
Focus on data types and formatting before typing speed.
Level 2 — Independent (standard):
Complete all exercises as written.
Answer all knowledge check questions.
Complete the assignment to the standard shown in the rubric.
Level 3 — Challenge (for learners who finish early):
Ask the learner to create a more detailed data types reference with additional examples.
Ask the learner to research and write down three more data entry best practices.
Ask the learner to teach the four data types to a partner.
