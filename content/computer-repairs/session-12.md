# Final Project (Capstone) — Diagnose, Repair, Document and Present

## A. Session Identification
<!-- kind:identification -->
**Week / module:** Week 4 — Module 4: Professional Practice (Capstone)

**Recommended duration:** 105 minutes (plus a short certificate sign-off)

**Difficulty:** Beginner, but it tests all accumulated skills (Sessions 1–11)

**Prerequisite knowledge:** Sessions 1–11 — components, safety and tools, disassembly/reassembly, diagnostic workflow, hardware and software diagnostics, component replacement, cleaning and maintenance, OS installation, customer service, and service reports

**Connection to previous work:** This is the integration of the whole course. Every prior session taught one skill; today you combine them into a single professional repair job, exactly as a shop would.

**Preparation before class:** Prepare one faulty machine per learner (or one per pair for large classes) with a simulated fault; ensure a fault log exists for every machine so the examiner knows the real cause; prepare tool kits, anti-static straps, replacement parts and cleaning supplies; print the Capstone Brief, the Capstone Rubric, the Viva Question List and the Certificate Sign-off sheets; set up assessment stations; confirm each machine's twist is documented. Safety and ethics flag Safety procedures (ESD, unplug before opening, never open a PSU) are assessed, not optional. Data safety (back up before any wipe) is assessed. Only licensed software is used. No learner may proceed past a safety breach without correction.

## B. Learning Outcomes
<!-- kind:outcomes -->
By the end of this session, the learner will be able to:
1. Receive a faulty machine and complete a customer intake, capturing the reported fault accurately.
2. Diagnose the fault using the systematic diagnostic workflow (observe, ask, hypothesise, test, resolve).
3. Apply correct safety procedures (ESD protection, power isolation, safe tool use) throughout.
4. Perform the repair — replacement, cleaning or OS task — safely and correctly.
5. Verify the fix with real tests and confirm the machine is fully functional.
6. Produce a complete professional service report documenting the entire job.
7. Present and defend their work in a short viva/demonstration, answering questions about their decisions.
8. Reflect honestly on their competence, their challenges and their next steps as a technician.

Anti-static wrist straps and mats; safety glasses if compressed air is used; first aid kit available.

Assessment stations, each with a machine and its fault log; a clear counter for the intake role-play; a quiet corner for the
viva so it is not disturbed.

## D. OPENING ACTIVITY (6 minutes)
<!-- kind:opening -->
Instructor setup: Stand beside a faulty machine on the counter. Do not open it yet. Put the Capstone Brief on the projector.
What the instructor says:
"Four weeks ago, most of you had never opened a computer case. You did not know RAM from a hard drive. Look at you
now.
Today there is no teaching and no demonstration. Today you work. A customer — that is me — will bring you a faulty
machine. You will greet the customer, take the fault, diagnose it, repair it, prove it works, write the service report, and
explain your work to me.
I will not help you. Not because I do not care, but because your future employer and your future customers will not be
standing beside you. Today you prove to yourself that you can do this alone.
Two rules stand above everything: work safely, and protect the customer's data. Break either rule and it is an automatic
fail for that part, no matter how good the repair is. Everything else, you already know. Let us begin."
Expected learner response: A mix of nerves and excitement. Reassure them: the brief, the template, the tools and the workflow
posters are all in front of them. They are not being asked to memorise — they are being asked to apply.
Why this opener works: It raises the stakes (independent performance) while lowering panic (everything is provided). It also
states the two non-negotiables, safety and data, before any work begins.

## E. PREREQUISITE REVIEW (7 minutes)
<!-- kind:prereq_review -->
This review is the last-minute confidence check before the assessment. Run it fast, as a whole-class call-and-response, so
learners hear the key facts one final time.
Question 1: "What are the five steps of the diagnostic workflow?"
Answer: observe, ask questions, form a hypothesis, test, resolve/verify.
Question 2: "What are the six major components?"
Answer: motherboard, CPU, RAM, storage (HDD/SSD), PSU, GPU.
Question 3: "What is ESD, and how do you prevent it?"
Answer: electrostatic discharge — a sudden flow of static electricity that damages components. Prevent it with an anti-
static wrist strap, an anti-static mat, and by touching bare metal of the case before handling components.
Question 4: "What must you do before opening any case, and what must you never open?"
Answer: unplug the power, then hold the power button for a few seconds to discharge residual power. Never open the PSU
case — its capacitors can hold a lethal charge.
Question 5: "What do these tools test — MemTest86, CrystalDiskInfo, HWMonitor?"
Answer: MemTest86 tests RAM; CrystalDiskInfo reports hard drive/SSD health; HWMonitor shows temperatures and
voltages.
Question 6: "Name the sections of a service report."
Answer: header/report number, customer and device details, reported fault, diagnosis/findings, action taken, parts used,
tests and result, costs, recommendations, warranty, sign-off.
Question 7: "What is the academy's rule about software licences?"
Answer: install only properly licensed software; never use pirated or cracked Windows.
Bridge to the assessment (say this):
"Those seven answers are your toolkit. If you can hold onto them under pressure, you will do well. Now the review is over
— the customer is coming."
Gap handling: If the class is visibly shaky on any single question, spend 60 seconds re-explaining it only, without restarting the
whole review. Do not extend this into teaching: the session time belongs to the learners' independent work.

## F. FULL LECTURE NOTES (Capstone Briefing)

F.1 What the capstone is
The idea. The capstone is one complete, realistic repair job done independently. It assesses not just whether you can fix the
machine, but whether you can behave like a professional technician from first greeting to final signature.
The scenario. You are the technician at Cyber Elias Tech, a repair shop in Port Harcourt. A customer (the instructor) brings in a
faulty computer. You must:
1. Conduct a customer intake.
2. Diagnose the fault using the systematic workflow.
3. Perform the repair safely.
4. Verify the fix.
5. Write a service report.
6. Present and defend your work in a short viva/demonstration.
What is being assessed (overview). Seven areas — safety adherence, diagnostic method, correct identification, repair quality,
verification/testing, service report, and presentation/viva — worth 30 marks, plus a 5-mark viva/demo component. The full
rubric is in Section I.
Your deliverables.
A correctly diagnosed fault.
A safely performed repair.
A verified, fully functional machine.
A complete, professional service report.
A confident presentation/viva.
Time. Approximately 70 minutes for intake, diagnosis, repair and report; 5 minutes for the viva/demo (per learner, run in parallel
or in a slot).

Communication. Explain your thinking calmly in the viva.

professional, not just the technician."

## G. Instructor Demonstration
<!-- kind:demo -->
There is no instructor demonstration in this session.
The capstone is a learner-led independent assessment. Demonstrating the repair would defeat its purpose. Instead, the
instructor:
States the brief (Section D) and reviews prerequisites (Section E).
Assigns each learner their machine and intake scenario.
Observes, times and marks using the rubric (Section I).
Runs the viva using the question list (Section J).
Does not give technical hints during the independent work.
Permitted assistance: safety interventions only. If a learner is about to cause harm (for example, reaching to open a PSU, or

concern against the relevant rubric criterion. No other help is given.

## H. Guided Practical Exercise
<!-- kind:guided_practical -->
There is no guided practical in this session.

I. INDEPENDENT CLASS EXERCISE — THE CAPSTONE (≈70 minutes + viva)
I.1 The task
Scenario: "You are a computer repair technician at Cyber Elias Tech. A customer brings in a faulty computer. Diagnose the
problem, perform the repair, verify the fix and produce a service report. Present your work to the instructor."
Procedure:
Step 1 — Customer intake (5 minutes).
1. Greet the customer (instructor) warmly.
2. Ask good diagnostic questions; capture the reported fault in the customer's own words.
3. Ask about important data (this is mandatory).
4. Set expectations: diagnosis first, then a written estimate before any billable work.
Step 2 — Diagnose (20 minutes).
1. Observe (look, listen, smell, feel) before opening anything.
2. Ask further questions if needed.
3. Form a hypothesis and say it out loud (for the assessor).
4. Test with the correct tools (memory test, SMART check, temperature, PSU test).
5. Identify the root cause and record every test and result.
Step 3 — Repair (25 minutes).
1. Safety first: power off, unplug, discharge, anti-static strap on, mat under the machine.
2. Perform the repair with the correct tools and correct technique.
3. Replace, reseat, clean or install the OS (with a backup first if data is at risk).
4. Only licensed software.
Step 4 — Verify (10 minutes).
1. Power on and confirm the specific fault is resolved.
2. Run diagnostic tests again (memory, SMART, temperature, Device Manager, boot).
3. Confirm all components work: display, sound, network, storage, ports.
4. State clearly: "Fault resolved" only when the evidence supports it.
Step 5 — Service report (10 minutes).
1. Complete all sections of the report.
2. Use plain language and specific details.
3. Include correct totals and warranty.
4. Get the customer's (instructor's) signature.
Step 6 — Present / viva (5 minutes).
1. Explain what the fault was, how you found it, what you did and how you verified it.
2. Answer the assessor's viva questions (Section J) confidently and honestly.
Time allowance: 70 minutes + 5-minute viva.
Success criteria: Fault correctly diagnosed; repair performed safely and correctly; fix verified; complete professional service
report; clear, honest presentation.

J. VIVA / DEMONSTRATION — QUESTION LIST WITH MODEL ANSWERS
Instructor-only (mostly). Ask 4–6 questions per learner, chosen to probe their actual work. Model answers are guides,
not scripts; a learner who reasons correctly in their own words should be credited.
Q1. "In your own words, what was the fault and how did you find it?"
Model: The learner names the actual fault and walks through observe → ask → hypothesise → test → conclude. They
should mention the specific test that confirmed the cause (for example, memory test errors, SMART warnings, high
temperatures, or a known-good PSU test).
Q2. "What safety precautions did you take before opening the case, and why?"
Model: Powered off and unplugged the machine; held the power button to discharge residual power; wore an anti-static
wrist strap connected to bare metal; used an anti-static mat; used the correct screwdriver. These protect the technician and
the components.
Q3. "Why did you test the way you did, rather than swapping parts?"
Model: Testing finds the root cause and avoids wasting time and the customer's money on parts that are not faulty.
Random part-swapping can also introduce new faults.
Q4. "How did you confirm the fault was truly fixed?"
Model: Re-ran the same test that failed before (for example, memory diagnostic clean, SMART healthy, temperatures
normal, machine boots to Windows), plus a full-system check (Device Manager, display, sound, network).
Q5. "Walk me through your service report — what would you say to the customer?"
Model: The learner summarises the reported fault, the diagnosis, the repair, the parts, the total cost, the warranty, and the
recommendation in plain language.
Q6. "If the customer's machine had important data and the fix required a reinstall, what would you have done?"
Model: Backed up and verified the data first, informed the customer, and obtained approval before wiping; only then
installed Windows. Data safety before any wipe.
Q7. "What would you do if you could not find the fault?"
Model: Be honest — do not guess or pretend. Return to the workflow, re-observe, re-ask, re-test, check the simplest causes
first; consult a senior technician; tell the customer the truth about the uncertainty.
Q8. "Show me where the RAM / storage / PSU is and explain what it does."
Model: The learner points to the correct component and gives its function (RAM = short-term memory; storage =
permanent files; PSU = converts wall power to the voltages the PC needs).
Q9. "Was any part of this job done legally? What about the software?"
Model: Only properly licensed software was used; no pirated Windows. The learner can state the academy's licensing rule.
Q10. "What would you do differently next time?"
Model: An honest reflection — for example, "I would ask more questions during intake", "I would check temperature
earlier", or "I would keep my workspace tidier". Self-awareness is a sign of competence.
Q11. "Why did you record the customer's exact words in the report?"
Model: They can contain clues to the cause, and they prove the technician listened and understood the customer's
experience.
Q12. "A customer is angry that the repair is late. How would you handle it?"
Model: Listen without interrupting, empathise, focus on solutions, and set calm boundaries; communicate honestly about
the new time.
Marking the viva (5 marks):
5 — Clear, confident, honest; answers accurately; explains decisions; owns limitations.
3 — Understandable; answers most questions correctly; minor gaps.
1 — Uncertain; struggles to explain own work.
0 — Cannot explain the work or misrepresents it.

## K. COMMON MISTAKES AND TROUBLESHOOTING (Capstone failure points)
<!-- kind:troubleshooting -->
Use this as the remediation map when a learner underperforms. These are the most common failure points and what to do
about them.
K.1 Skipping safety steps
Symptom: Opens the case without the strap or without unplugging. Why: Rushing, nerves, overconfidence. Fix: Stop, reset the
learner, restate the rule, let them continue safely (rubric reflects the lapse). Prevent: Make safety a habit drilled since Session 2;
the strap comes out before the case.
K.2 Jumping to a repair without diagnosis
Symptom: Immediately replaces a part guessed to be at fault. Why: Impatience; forgetting the workflow. Fix: Pause; require
them to state a hypothesis and one test that would confirm it before touching the machine. Prevent: Practise "say your
hypothesis out loud" in every diagnostic session.
K.3 Fixing the symptom, not the root cause
Symptom: Machine boots after a reseat but the real fault (for example, a failing drive) remains. Why: Stopping at the first sign of
life. Fix: Require verification tests; ask "how do you know the cause is gone?" Prevent: Always test the underlying cause, not
just the symptom.
K.4 Not verifying the fix
Symptom: "It switched on, so it's fixed." Why: Misunderstanding what verification means. Fix: Re-run the specific failing test;
check the whole system. Prevent: Use the post-repair checklist every time.
K.5 Poor or incomplete service report
Symptom: Missing sections, vague action lines, wrong totals, no signature. Why: Rushed at the end; forgets the template. Fix:
Complete the template section by section; check arithmetic; add signatures. Prevent: Write the report from the same facts as
the repair; practise in Session 11.
K.6 Data safety lapse
Symptom: Wipes or reinstalls without backing up or without customer consent. Why: Forgetting the customer's data in the
heat of the task. Fix: Stop; if data may be lost, halt and refer (recovery is beyond this course); record the lapse. Prevent: Ask
about data in intake; back up and verify before any wipe.
K.7 Cannot explain the work in the viva
Symptom: Repaired the machine but cannot say how or why. Why: Learnt by imitation rather than understanding; panic. Fix:
Walk them back through their own steps; ask simpler "what" and "why" questions. Prevent: Narrate reasoning during practice
so it becomes natural.
K.8 Licensing shortcut
Symptom: Uses a pirated or cracked Windows/software. Why: Convenience; not understanding the risk. Fix: Stop immediately;
require a licensed install; explain the legal and safety consequences. Prevent: State the licensing rule every time software is
installed.
Distinguish likely vs confirmed applies here too: do not assume a learner "does not know" — observe the actual behaviour
and confirm with a question before judging. The rubric is about demonstrable performance.

O.1 Observable signs of genuine competence
The learner puts on the anti-static strap and isolates power without being reminded.
The learner states a clear hypothesis before testing, and tests logically.
The learner identifies the true root cause, matching the fault log, with evidence.
The learner repairs cleanly, with the correct component and technique.

The learner's service report is complete, specific, honest and signed.

The learner uses only licensed software and can state the rule.
O.2 Common misconceptions to watch for and correct
"I can just learn this on my own." → Structured practice prevents unsafe habits and builds method. Self-teaching without
feedback often cements bad technique.
"Repair is only about fixing broken things." → It is equally maintenance, customer service and documentation.
"Passing means I am an expert." → Passing means you are a safe, competent beginner. Expertise takes years and further
study.
"If it switches on, it is fixed." → Verification requires re-testing the actual fault and checking the whole system.
"Reports and safety slow me down." → They protect the customer, the shop and you; a careless wipe or a dishonest report
can end a career.
"A cracked Windows is fine to save money." → Illegal and unsafe; only licensed software.
O.3 Suggested remedial exercises / remediation
Did not pass the capstone: allow supervised practice on academy machines during opening hours; reassess when ready. A
second attempt is normal and expected — competence, not speed, is the goal.
Cannot diagnose: provide additional guided diagnostic scenarios, starting with simple causes, and require the learner to say
the hypothesis out loud each time.
Cannot follow safety: one-on-one safety drill until the strap/unplug routine is automatic; a safety breach must be
corrected before any reassessment.
Cannot write a report: provide a partially completed report to finish; then progress to writing independently against the
template.
Cannot explain the work (viva): practise narrating reasoning during simple tasks; use "what did you do, and why?"
questioning until it is natural.
O.4 Differentiation
Level 1 — Supported:
Allow extra time for the capstone.
Provide a structured step-by-step prompt sheet and gentle time reminders.
Offer one-on-one coaching outside the assessment, and a retake after practice.
Choose the simplest simulated fault (for example, loose RAM) for the first attempt.
Level 2 — Independent:
Complete the capstone, knowledge check and reflection as written.
Level 3 — Challenge:
Assign a machine with two simultaneous faults (for example, dusty overheating plus a failing drive), or a fault requiring an
OS installation as part of the repair.
Require a longer service report with a customer-facing summary paragraph.
Ask higher-order viva questions (for example, "How would you prevent this fault recurring?").
